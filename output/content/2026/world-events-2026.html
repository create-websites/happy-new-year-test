October 2026

Nobel season, Ebola reaches a fifth country, Hormuz talks stall

October brought no breakthrough on the Iran war: talks to reopen the Strait of Hormuz stalled. Ebola Bundibugyo reached Nigeria, the fifth country to confirm a case this year, Morocco seated its first woman prime minister, and the Nobel Prizes honoured optogenetics, neutrino astronomy and international law.

  1. Oct 4Politics & elections

    About 70,000 people marched in Madrid in housing protests.

  2. Oct 4Conflict & security

    German Chancellor Friedrich Merz visited Kyiv with a EUR 1 billion military package in aid.

  3. Oct 4Conflict & security

    North Korea said it had hit a target about 1,000 km away with an intermediate-range missile, raising regional tensions.

  4. Oct 5-12Science, space & health

    Nobel Prizes announced: Medicine to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel (optogenetics); Physics to Francis Halzen (IceCube neutrinos); Literature to Anne Carson; Peace to Navi Pillay; Economics to Robert Hall and Michael Woodford. The Chemistry prize went to French and Japanese scientists.

  5. Oct 6-22Science, space & health

    Ebola Bundibugyo spread: Kenya confirmed its first case on Oct 6 and Nigeria on Oct 20. By mid-October Congolese authorities counted more than 9,500 confirmed cases and over 4,200 deaths, and WHO warned of a "more complex" outbreak.

  6. Oct 7Conflict & security

    Israel marked three years since the October 7 attack ,.

  7. Oct 8Climate & disasters

    WWF's Living Planet Report 2026 found monitored wildlife populations in Africa fell by an average of 80 percent between 1970 and 2022.

  8. Oct 12-18Conflict & security

    US-Iran talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz stalled as Iran weighed a US reply; on Oct 18 Tehran urged Washington to accept its proposal.

  9. Oct 12-18Economy & trade

    IMF and World Bank meetings in Bangkok ended without new initiatives for developing countries facing high debt costs, inflation and AI-related risks.

  10. Oct 13-16Politics & elections

    Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri secured a four-party coalition (207 seats) and Morocco announced a 32-member cabinet led by its first woman prime minister.

  11. Oct 18Politics & elections

    Trump endorsed Benjamin Netanyahu's re-election bid.

  12. Oct 19Conflict & security

    Ukraine struck an oil terminal in St. Petersburg after a fragile energy ceasefire collapsed.

  13. Oct 21Climate & disasters

    UNEP's Emissions Gap Report 2026, titled The Point of Return, urged countries to cut emissions far faster.

  14. Oct 22Technology

    A flaw in the PHP programming language took websites offline.

September 2026

Morocco elects, an Arctic accord, and back-channel Hormuz talks

September saw Morocco vote its way to a first woman prime minister, the US, Denmark and Greenland sign an Arctic security agreement at the UN, and American and Iranian negotiators explore a phased deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz even as ships and drones came under fire. Russia's ruling party kept its Duma majority.

  1. Sep 1Technology

    The US Federal Trade Commission sued Amazon, alleging an advertising overcharging scheme worth about $20 billion.

  2. Sep 2Economy & trade

    Uber announced it would cut about 3,300 jobs, roughly 10% of its global workforce, mostly in management.

  3. Early SepPolitics & elections

    Leaders of major powers including Russia, China, India, Iran, Pakistan and Turkey met at the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Kyrgyzstan.

  4. Sep 7Politics & elections

    Thousands attended the funeral in Belgrade of Ratko Mladic, the Bosnian Serb commander who died in a UN hospital on Aug 27 while serving a life sentence.

  5. Sep 18-21Politics & elections

    Russia's ruling United Russia party kept its majority in the national parliament.

  6. Sep 20-21Climate & disasters

    Typhoon Dujuan approached Tokyo, with evacuation orders issued for about 1.6 million people.

  7. Sep 21Conflict & security

    Iran said it shot down a US MQ-1 drone over the Strait of Hormuz, and a tanker there was hit by an unknown projectile.

  8. Sep 21Technology

    A telecommunications outage caused by a construction error disrupted hundreds of flights on the US East Coast.

  9. Sep 22Politics & elections

    The US, Denmark and Greenland signed an Arctic security agreement at the UN General Assembly.

  10. Sep 23Politics & elections

    Morocco held parliamentary elections; the Authenticity and Modernity Party (PAM) came first, and on Sep 29 King Mohammed VI appointed Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri as the first woman prime minister.

  11. Sep 24Conflict & security

    Reuters reported that US and Iranian negotiators in New York were exploring a phased path in which Iran would reopen Hormuz and the US would lift its economic blockade.

  12. Sep 28Conflict & security

    Eight US Marines were reported injured in an Iranian missile attack in the Strait of Hormuz; Russia hit Ukraine's National Academy of Sciences in Kyiv.

  13. Sep 28Economy & trade

    The US and China agreed reciprocal tariff-cut lists covering about $30 billion of imports each. Fair Observer's September roundup also lists a Trump-Xi summit among the month's key developments.

August 2026

Hormuz deadlock, a deadly Indonesian quake, a by-election in Clacton

August was dominated by the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz: US-Iran talks deadlocked while Iran and Oman negotiated a shipping corridor and a US naval blockade of Iranian ports stayed in place. A magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck Flores, Indonesia, and Nigel Farage won the Clacton by-election he had forced.

  1. Aug 3-4Conflict & security

    The 30-nation RIMPAC naval exercise, the world's largest, ended; Iran and Oman held talks on a route for ships through the Strait of Hormuz.

  2. Aug 5Politics & elections

    Abdul El-Sayed won the Democratic Senate primary in Michigan, a win for the party's progressive wing.

  3. Aug 5-14Conflict & security

    Taiwan held its 10-day Han Kuang military exercise, joined by the US and partner countries.

  4. Aug 13Conflict & security

    Ukraine attacked Russia's Black Sea port of Novorossiysk, hitting warships at a naval base and halting two grain terminals.

  5. Aug 13Conflict & security

    US-Iran talks remained deadlocked: Iran said Hormuz would stay blocked until its conditions were met, while the US kept a naval blockade on Iranian ports.

  6. Aug 14Politics & elections

    Nigel Farage won the Clacton by-election, called after he resigned as an MP, in a contest boycotted by the major parties.

  7. Aug 15Climate & disasters

    A magnitude 7.7 earthquake off Flores Island, Indonesia, killed at least 51 people, damaged more than 1,300 homes and displaced about 5,000.

  8. Aug 23Climate & disasters

    A human-caused wildfire approached Reno, Nevada, forcing thousands to evacuate.

  9. Aug 26Science, space & health

    A fire in the newborn nursery of a major hospital in Islamabad killed 14 babies, according to reports.

  10. Aug 27Politics & elections

    Ratko Mladic, convicted of genocide and war crimes, died in a UN hospital.

  11. Aug 28Politics & elections

    A US court again rejected President Trump's attempt to erase his hush-money conviction.

July 2026

The Iran ceasefire collapses; Spain wins the World Cup

July opened with tanker attacks near Hormuz and ended the US-Iran ceasefire: the US resumed nightly strikes from around July 11. In the UK, Nigel Farage resigned his seat to force a by-election and Andy Burnham's path to Downing Street cleared. On July 19, Spain beat Argentina 1-0 in extra time to win the World Cup.

  1. Jul 4Politics & elections

    The United States marked its 250th anniversary with events across the country, held amid heatwaves and power outages.

  2. Jul 7Conflict & security

    A third tanker in 24 hours was hit in the Strait of Hormuz, according to the UK maritime agency UKMTO.

  3. Jul 7Politics & elections

    Nigel Farage said he would resign as an MP and stand in a by-election in Clacton. NATO leaders met the same day with Keir Starmer attending as caretaker prime minister.

  4. Jul 9Economy & trade

    Russia banned diesel exports amid a deepening fuel crisis. In the UK, Andy Burnham's route to No. 10 cleared when the last potential rival ruled out a bid.

  5. From about Jul 11Conflict & security

    The US-Iran ceasefire collapsed. The US launched a new campaign of strikes on Iran, and Iran retaliated against Gulf states; by Jul 19 the US had struck for nine nights.

  6. Jul 19Sport & culture

    Spain won the World Cup, beating Argentina 1-0 in extra time through Ferran Torres in the 106th minute. Kylian Mbappe won the Golden Boot and Rodri the Golden Ball.

  7. Jul 19Technology

    Twenty-nine countries established the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organisation in Shanghai.

  8. Jul 22Science, space & health

    The Ebola outbreak in Congo passed 1,000 deaths, and WHO said the true scale could be two to four times larger than official figures.

  9. By Jul 23Politics & elections

    Commentary was already referring to Andy Burnham as the new prime minister, following Starmer's June resignation.

June 2026

Starmer resigns, Colombia swings right, the World Cup begins

June saw the 48-team World Cup open in Mexico City, Keir Starmer resign as UK prime minister, and Colombia elect Abelardo de la Espriella by less than a point. In the Middle East a fragile US-Iran ceasefire was repeatedly tested before a mid-June memorandum on reopening Hormuz, and Israel and Lebanon announced a new ceasefire.

  1. Jun 3Conflict & security

    Iran struck Kuwait and Bahrain in one of the most damaging attacks since the ceasefire; on Jun 5 the US shot down missiles and drones headed for Kuwait, Bahrain and the Strait of Hormuz.

  2. Jun 3Politics & elections

    Denmark's Mette Frederiksen formed her third government, and Hungary's new prime minister Peter Magyar met Emmanuel Macron in Paris.

  3. Jun 4Conflict & security

    Israel and Lebanon announced a new ceasefire, though Hezbollah signalled it would not cooperate. Zelensky also proposed a full ceasefire and a face-to-face meeting with Putin in an open letter.

  4. Jun 11Sport & culture

    The World Cup opened in Mexico City with Mexico hosting South Africa, the first edition co-hosted by the US, Canada and Mexico.

  5. Jun 14Conflict & security

    Officials said the Palestinian death toll in Gaza had passed 73,000, with Israeli strikes continuing despite a ceasefire.

  6. Mid-JunConflict & security

    The US and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding that provided for reopening the Strait of Hormuz; it left Iran and Oman to define the strait's future administration.

  7. Jun 15-17Politics & elections

    The G7 summit in France opened amid visible divisions among leaders.

  8. Jun 19Politics & elections

    Andy Burnham won the Makerfield by-election, returning to Parliament and launching his bid for the Labour leadership.

  9. Jun 21Politics & elections

    Colombia's runoff: Trump-endorsed Abelardo de la Espriella won with 49.66% to Ivan Cepeda's 48.70%; authorities declared him the winner on Jun 24 after a recount, with a term starting Aug 7.

  10. Jun 22Politics & elections

    Keir Starmer resigned as UK prime minister and Labour leader after fewer than two years, to remain in post until a successor was chosen.

  11. Jun 24Science, space & health

    France reported a first Ebola case, a doctor returning from a humanitarian mission in the affected area of the DRC.

  12. Through JuneClimate & disasters

    Heat dominated Europe: France put emergency services and the military on wildfire alert, and May was recorded as the world's second-warmest May.

May 2026

Trump meets Xi in Beijing; Ebola outbreak declared

May brought the declaration of a deadly Ebola Bundibugyo outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, a Trump-Xi summit in Beijing with plenty of pageantry but few firm deals, and heavy Labour losses in UK local elections. The Iran ceasefire strained as Iranian missiles were intercepted over the UAE.

  1. May 4Conflict & security

    The UAE said it intercepted 15 Iranian missiles in the first major violation of the ceasefire; on May 10 a cargo ship caught fire off Qatar after a projectile attack.

  2. May 7Politics & elections

    Labour lost more than 1,400 council seats in UK local and devolved elections, with gains for Reform UK and the Greens, adding pressure on Keir Starmer.

  3. May 8Politics & elections

    Laura Fernandez was sworn in as Costa Rica's president, pledging security and judicial reforms.

  4. May 9Sport & culture

    The Eurovision final was held, and the Venice Biennale opened (running to Nov 12).

  5. May 11Science, space & health

    The cruise ship MV Hondius, hit by a hantavirus outbreak, anchored at Tenerife.

  6. Mid-MayScience, space & health

    The DRC declared an outbreak of Ebola caused by the Bundibugyo virus; by May 23 WHO rated the risk "very high" in the Congo.

  7. May 14-15Economy & trade

    Trump and Xi held a two-day summit in Beijing. It produced warm words but no extension of the tariff truce and little tangible help on the Iran war; Xi warned that mishandling Taiwan could lead to conflict. Trump invited Xi to the White House for Sept 24.

  8. May 21Conflict & security

    Ukraine kept up long-range strikes on Russian oil infrastructure, including a refinery hit.

  9. May 22Sport & culture

    A British climber summited Everest for a record 20th time; two Indian climbers were believed to have died on the descent.

  10. May 24Conflict & security

    A suicide bomber killed at least 23 people and injured more than 70 at a railway track in Pakistan.

April 2026

Artemis II returns, Orban loses, a US-Iran ceasefire and blockade

April began with Artemis II carrying four astronauts around the Moon and ended with the UAE announcing it would leave OPEC. In between, Hungary's Viktor Orban lost power after 16 years, and a two-week US-Iran ceasefire gave way to a US naval blockade and a re-closed Strait of Hormuz.

  1. Apr 1-10Science, space & health

    NASA's Artemis II crew of four flew around the Moon and splashed down in the Pacific on Apr 10. They travelled 252,756 miles from Earth, the farthest humans have been, and the first crewed lunar voyage in over 50 years.

  2. Apr 7Conflict & security

    The US and Iran agreed a two-week ceasefire; on Apr 11 talks in Pakistan ended without a peace deal, according to Vice President Vance.

  3. Apr 10Politics & elections

    Senior General Min Aung Hlaing was sworn in as President of Myanmar.

  4. Apr 12Politics & elections

    Hungary's Viktor Orban conceded after Peter Magyar's Tisza party won a landslide, with a record turnout of nearly 80%, ending 16 years of Orban's rule.

  5. Apr 12-18Conflict & security

    Israel and Lebanon observed a 10-day ceasefire.

  6. Apr 17Politics & elections

    King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden visited Kyiv, the first monarch to visit Ukraine since Russia's full-scale invasion.

  7. Apr 19Conflict & security

    Iran re-closed the Strait of Hormuz in response to a US naval blockade of its ports; the US seized an Iranian-flagged ship near the strait.

  8. Apr 22Economy & trade

    Hungary withdrew its veto on a $105 billion loan for Ukraine, and Ukraine reopened a Russian oil pipeline.

  9. Apr 24Politics & elections

    Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu disclosed a prostate cancer diagnosis and treatment.

  10. Apr 26Sport & culture

    Ukraine and the world marked the 40th anniversary of the Chornobyl nuclear disaster.

  11. Apr 28Economy & trade

    The UAE announced it would leave OPEC and OPEC+ effective May 1, a major blow to the oil producers' group already strained by the Iran war.

March 2026

The Iran war widens; a new Supreme Leader

The war that began on Feb 28 spread across the Gulf in March. Iran named Mojtaba Khamenei as supreme leader, Iranian strikes hit Gulf states, Hormuz traffic collapsed and governments tapped oil reserves. In the US, nationwide "No Kings" protests took place on Mar 28.

  2. Mar 4Conflict & security

  3. Mar 8-9Politics & elections

  4. Mar 9Conflict & security

  5. Mar 12Conflict & security

  6. Mar 15Sport & culture

  8. Mar 17Conflict & security

  9. Mar 19Politics & elections

January 2026

Maduro captured, Iran's deadly crackdown, a Greenland framework

January opened with the US capturing Venezuela's Nicolas Maduro, saw a deadly crackdown on mass protests in Iran, and ended up with Trump and NATO announcing a framework on Greenland and Arctic security at Davos. Spain suffered its worst rail disaster in more than a decade.

  1. Jan 3Conflict & security

    The US carried out large-scale strikes on Venezuela and captured President Nicolas Maduro and his wife, flying them out of the country; the US said Maduro would face drug-related charges in New York.

  2. Jan 8-27Politics & elections

    Protests in Iran that began in late December over economic grievances became a mass movement, met by an internet shutdown and a deadly crackdown. Iran's government put the death toll at 3,117, while rights groups verified more than 6,000.

  3. Jan 18Climate & disasters

    A high-speed train derailed and hit an oncoming train near Adamuz in southern Spain; the death toll reached at least 45, Spain's worst rail disaster since 2013.

  4. Jan 19-23Economy & trade

    The World Economic Forum met in Davos, Switzerland, amid trade tensions and talk of a fragmenting world order.

  5. Jan 21Politics & elections

    At Davos, Trump said he and NATO chief Mark Rutte had formed a "framework" for a future deal on Greenland and the Arctic, and dropped a tariff threat against European countries; few details were given.

  6. Jan 22Science, space & health

    The United States completed its withdrawal from the World Health Organization.

  7. Jan 24Conflict & security

    Russia launched a heavy barrage on Ukraine, leaving millions without power, as US-Russia-Ukraine talks continued in Abu Dhabi.

  8. Jan 24-25Climate & disasters

    A powerful winter storm led to more than 9,000 flight cancellations across the United States.

  9. Jan 26Conflict & security

    Israel's military announced that the remains of the final hostage held in Gaza had been recovered.

Frequently asked questions

What were the biggest world events of 2026 from January to October?

The US capture of Venezuela's Maduro (Jan 3), the US-Israel war on Iran that began on Feb 28 and the resulting Strait of Hormuz crisis, Artemis II's lunar flyby (April), Viktor Orban's election defeat in Hungary (Apr 12), the Ebola Bundibugyo outbreak declared in the DRC in May, Keir Starmer's resignation (Jun 22), Spain's World Cup win (Jul 19) and Morocco's first woman prime minister (Sep 29).

When did the 2026 Iran war start?

It began on Feb 28, 2026, when the US and Israel launched a major military campaign against Iran that killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. Iran named his son Mojtaba Khamenei as successor on Mar 8-9.

Who won the 2026 FIFA World Cup?

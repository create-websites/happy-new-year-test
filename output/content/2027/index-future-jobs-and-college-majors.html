6 key points

The Big Picture

Slower growth overall, faster change inside it

The next decade adds jobs, but more slowly than the last one, and the mix is shifting toward care, energy, data and skilled technical work. The numbers below come from US government projections, a global employer survey and recent graduate-market data.

  1. BLS 2025-35Big picture

    The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects the economy will add 5.9 million jobs from 2025 to 2035, lifting employment from 170.3 million to 176.2 million. That is 3.5% growth, slower than the 10.9% of 2015 to 2025.

  2. WEF 2030Big picture

    The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 expects 170 million new roles and 92 million displaced by 2030, a net gain of 78 million, with disruption equal to 22% of today's jobs.

  3. WEF 2030Skills & hiring

    The same report says nearly 60% of the global workforce will need upskilling or reskilling by 2030. Learning does not stop at graduation.

  4. Graduates 2026Study & journey

    The New York Fed put unemployment for recent graduates (ages 22 to 27) at 5.7% in June 2026, above the all-worker rate. A separate Census-based analysis found 7.3% in summer 2026, within the range of the previous four years. The two use different definitions, so watch the trend rather than comparing levels.

  5. Education paysBig picture

    BLS data for 2025 show full-time workers aged 25 and over with a bachelor's degree earning a median $1,578 a week versus $966 for high school graduates, with unemployment of 2.8% versus 4.3%.

  6. NACE 2026Skills & hiring

    Employers told NACE they expect to hire 5.6% more Class of 2026 graduates, but growth is uneven by industry, and 45% rate the market for new graduates as only fair.

Jobs Growing Fastest

Care, energy and data lead the list

Healthcare dominates the fastest-growing occupations, joined by renewable energy and data roles. Percentages can mislead: a small occupation can grow fast and still add few jobs, so the job counts are shown too.

  1. BLS 2025-35Health & science

    Nurse practitioners are projected to be the fastest-growing occupation, up 41%, an estimated gain of about 137,800 jobs.

  2. BLS 2025-35Tech & AI

    Data scientists are projected to grow 34.6% (95,400 jobs), and computer and information research scientists 21.8% (8,400 jobs).

  3. BLS 2025-35Energy & trades

    Solar panel installers are projected to grow 36.5% (about 11,300 jobs) and wind turbine technicians 29.5% (3,500 jobs). Both are small occupations.

  4. BLS 2025-35Health & science

    Physical therapist assistants are projected to grow 23% (26,200 jobs) and occupational therapy assistants 21.5%.

  5. BLS 2024-34Health & science

    In the previous cycle, healthcare and social assistance was the fastest-growing sector (8.4%) and added the most jobs, roughly 2.0 million, driven by an ageing population. Information security analysts were projected to grow 28.5%, the fastest of any computer occupation.

  6. BLS 2025-35Energy & trades

    BLS says four of the ten fastest-growing industries are driven largely by rising electricity demand, tied in part to AI adoption and data centers.

  7. WEF 2030Tech & AI

    Employers surveyed by the World Economic Forum see the fastest percentage growth in big data specialists, fintech engineers and AI and machine learning specialists, with renewable energy engineers, environmental engineers and electric and autonomous vehicle specialists also on the list.

Jobs Under Pressure

Routine desk work is where the squeeze shows

Shrinking roles cluster in routine office, clerical and service work where software can already handle much of the task. Young workers in those roles appear to feel it first. Projections are not certainties, and BLS itself flags uncertainty about the pace of AI adoption.

  1. BLS 2025-35Under pressure

    Customer service representatives are projected to fall about 5% (141,800 jobs) through 2035 as AI handles more interactions, while web developers, also rated highly AI-exposed, are projected to grow nearly 4%. Exposure is not the same as elimination.

  2. BLS 2024-34Under pressure

    In the earlier cycle, office and administrative support occupations were projected to fall 3.9% (761,900 jobs) and sales roles 2.0%.

  3. BLS 2024-34Under pressure

    BLS singled out procurement clerks (-8.7%), credit authorizers and checkers (-6.2%) and legal secretaries and administrative assistants (-5.8%) as occupations where IT and AI limit demand.

  4. BLS 2025-35Big picture

    Retail trade is projected to lose about 27,500 jobs as e-commerce continues to limit employment in stores.

  5. Stanford 2025Under pressure

    A Stanford study using ADP payroll data found workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed occupations had a 13% relative employment decline after generative AI spread, while older workers and people who use AI to augment their work saw gains. The authors present it as early evidence.

  6. Goldman 2026Study & journey

    A Goldman Sachs analysis found computer science and programming enrollment each fell by more than 10% in 2025-26 while healthcare and engineering rose about 3%. It ranked management and quantitative methods, computer science and statistics at the highest displacement risk, and pharmacy, nursing and education-related fields among the safest.

Skills That Last

Technical fluency plus human judgement

Whatever the major, employers describe the same bundle: comfort with AI and data, plus teamwork, problem-solving and communication. Hiring is also moving from pedigree to proof of skill.

  1. WEF 2030Tech & AI

    The three fastest-growing skills to 2030 are AI and big data, networks and cybersecurity, and technological literacy.

  2. WEF 2030Skills & hiring

    Creative thinking, resilience, flexibility and agility rise alongside the technical skills, and environmental stewardship entered the top ten fastest-growing skills for the first time.

  3. NACE 2026Skills & hiring

    Employers reviewing resumes of Class of 2026 candidates want evidence of teamwork, problem-solving and communication.

  4. NACE 2026Skills & hiring

    Seventy percent of employers use skills-based hiring for entry-level roles, up from 65% a year earlier, and more than 80% list the skills they seek in job descriptions.

  5. NACE 2026Tech & AI

    NACE reports that AI is increasingly an expectation for early-career talent, shaping both the job market and entry-level work.

  6. Hiring signalSkills & hiring

    Employers are increasingly valuing internship and co-op experience over grade point averages, according to NACE data reported by Inside Higher Ed.

What to Study

Pick a field with demand, then add a second skill

Healthcare and engineering look steadiest, computing is still large but more competitive at the entry level, and the strongest plans pair a core field with a complementary skill. Rankings differ by method, so treat them as signals, not verdicts.

  1. HealthcareHealth & science

    A National University analysis of 74 majors ranked nursing first for employment, with a 1.42% unemployment rate, followed by mathematics, computer science and engineering fields.

  2. NursingHealth & science

    BLS projects registered nurse employment to grow 5% from 2024 to 2034, with about 189,100 openings a year and a May 2024 median wage of $93,600.

  3. EngineeringTech & AI

    Engineering is described by education analysts as one of the safest degree categories for a profession-oriented path, and the Goldman data show enrollment rising about 3%. Electrical and energy-related branches line up with the electricity-demand growth BLS describes.

  4. ComputingTech & AI

    Computer science still tops resume-based popularity rankings, yet one analysis put recent computer science graduate unemployment near 7% against a 4.3% national average. A weaker entry-level market is not the same as weak long-run demand, but it is a reason to build depth in a domain.

  5. Data & AITech & AI

    Data science is projected to grow 34.6%, while Goldman also flags statistics as exposed. The mixed signal favors people who can frame problems and judge results, not only run tools.

  6. PairingsStudy & journey

    Career advisers suggest combining a core field with a complement, for example computer science with business analytics or nursing with public health, to widen the roles open to you.

  7. Advanced degreesBig picture

    BLS 2025 medians rise with level: $1,578 a week for a bachelor's, $1,876 for a master's and $2,307 for a doctorate, though more years also means more cost.

Trades and Non-Degree Paths

The AI build-out needs people with tools

Data centers, power grids and smart buildings are creating strong demand for electricians, technicians and other skilled trades, often with paid training and no four-year degree.

  1. Randstad 2026Energy & trades

    A March 2026 Randstad analysis of more than 150 million US job postings found demand for skilled trades growing three times faster than for professional roles, with robotics technician vacancies up 113% since 2022.

  2. WagesEnergy & trades

    Construction workers on data center projects earn about $81,800 a year on average, roughly 32% more than on non-data center builds, according to Skillit.

  3. ElectriciansEnergy & trades

    BLS projects roughly 81,000 electrician openings a year through 2034, and industry sources put electrical work at 45% to 70% of a data center's construction budget.

  4. ShortageEnergy & trades

    Associated Builders and Contractors estimates construction needs about 349,000 net new workers in 2026, and 58% of firms in an AGC and NCCER survey say data center projects increase competition for workers.

  5. TrainingSkills & hiring

    Employers are funding the pipeline: Meta announced a $115 million program with free trades instruction and guaranteed job offers, and BlackRock committed $100 million toward 50,000 trades workers.

  6. Reality checkStudy & journey

    Traditional apprenticeships take four to five years, so these are real careers, not shortcuts. Demand can also shift with the pace of data center investment.

Your Journey, Step by Step

A planning roadmap from high school to first job

This section is editorial guidance built on the evidence above, not a forecast. Use it as a checklist and adjust it to your country, budget and interests.

  1. Grades 9-10Study & journey

    Browse widely: try coding, biology, building things and writing. Keep mathematics and reading strong, and talk to three adults about what their work involves day to day.

  2. Grades 11-12Study & journey

    Shortlist two or three paths, including at least one non-degree or two-year option. Compare programs by graduate outcomes and total cost, not only by name.

  3. College year 1Study & journey

    Keep options open. Take statistics and a writing-intensive course, learn to use AI tools well, and join one project where you build something real.

  4. College year 2Study & journey

    Choose a major using evidence: check the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, your college's first-destination data and the growth lists on this page. Land a first internship or research role.

  5. College year 3Study & journey

    Do an internship in your target field, build a small portfolio and earn one recognized certification if your field has one. Employers weigh proof of skill heavily.

  6. College year 4Study & journey

    Apply early, use referrals and tailor your resume to skills rather than coursework. Compare offers on growth and learning, not only starting pay.

  7. After graduationStudy & journey

    Plan to reskill every few years. With nearly 60% of workers needing new skills by 2030, a learning habit matters more than any single degree.

Frequently asked questions

What jobs will be in demand in the future?

US projections to 2035 point to healthcare (nurse practitioners, therapy assistants, home care), data and security roles, and energy occupations such as solar and wind technicians. Employer surveys add AI and machine learning specialists, big data specialists and fintech engineers, and skilled trades such as electricians are in short supply.

Which college majors have the best job prospects?

Nursing and other healthcare programs, engineering and mathematics rank highest on recent employment-based analyses. Computer science remains popular and well paid but is more competitive for entry-level roles, so pairing it with domain knowledge helps. See the What to Study section for the evidence.

Will AI take away entry-level jobs?

The evidence is mixed. A Stanford payroll study found a 13% relative employment decline for 22 to 25 year olds in the most AI-exposed occupations, while another analysis of Census data found recent-graduate unemployment within its range of the past four years. The safest reading is that some entry-level work is changing and that AI skills now matter. Details are in Jobs Under Pressure.

Is a college degree still worth it?

On average, yes: BLS data for 2025 show higher median earnings and lower unemployment as education rises. The return varies by field and cost, so compare programs rather than assuming. Skilled trades and two-year credentials are strong alternatives in several fields; see Trades and Non-Degree Paths.

Which skills should students build?

Technical literacy (AI, data, cybersecurity) plus human skills such as communication, teamwork, creative thinking and resilience. Employers increasingly hire on demonstrated skills, so internships, projects and portfolios count. More in Skills That Last.

Are skilled trades a good alternative to college?

They can be. Data center and grid construction are driving demand and above-average wages for electricians and related trades, and employers are funding training. Apprenticeships take years, and demand can shift with investment cycles.

How reliable are these job forecasts?

Treat them as informed estimates, not guarantees. BLS revises its projections every year, and the pace of AI adoption is a stated uncertainty. Percentages for small occupations can look large while adding few jobs.

Is this page career or financial advice, and does it cover India?

No. It summarizes public projections and surveys to help students plan, and it is not individual advice. Most figures are for the United States or global employer surveys; students elsewhere, including in India, should also check national labor-market data.

05 · Buy with discipline

Seven rules that save real money

01

Set an exact model number

TV, laptop and appliance names can hide very different specifications. Compare the complete model and bundles, not just the brand.

02

Compare price history

Reference the last 30–90 days with a trusted tracker and a couple of retailers. A giant crossed-out MSRP is not proof of an exceptional deal.

03

Check the total bill

Include shipping, sales tax, delivery, installation, consumables, subscription renewals and disposal fees before you call it a bargain.

04

Do not buy subscriptions blindly

Check year-two charges for cameras, game passes, fitness gear and services. Make recurring costs part of the gift budget.

05

Use price protections carefully

Policies differ. Walmart explicitly excludes its deals events from price matching. Best Buy and Target have holiday terms and exclusions.

06

Prioritize proven needs

Replacement appliances, work tools and frequently used products are often better buys than flashy products that sit unused.

07

Have a one-sentence reason

“I need a 16GB laptop under $500 for school” is useful. “It’s 50% off” is not. Save the difference when nothing fits your list.

✦

Novel idea: buy for the whole household

One family activity, one household upgrade and individual low-cost gifts can create more value than four unrelated expensive gadgets.

New for Nov 19

Amazon and Best Buy start their major events. Recheck AirPods, laptops, headphones and TVs before you commit to early prices.

Canada: The Polar Bear Plunge

Canada's New Year can be cozy indoors or dramatically outdoors. On January 1, some communities hold organized Polar Bear Plunges, inviting participants to briefly enter very cold water as a symbolic fresh start, often connected with charity or local fundraising.

🇨🇦 Canada🎊 January 1 traditions🌍 Around the world

What makes the celebration special

The plunge captures a playful spirit of courage, but it is an optional organized activity, not a rite that every Canadian undertakes. Participants and supporters greet each other with Happy New Year or Bonne Année, reflecting the country's two official languages. There are also concerts, skating traditions and community gatherings across provinces.

Where to experience it

Niagara Falls can provide a spectacular winter setting with its illuminated landscape and tourism activities. Banff and the Rocky Mountains offer snowy scenery and cozy lodges in a very different region; the two destinations are far apart and require separate travel planning.

Planning a memorable New Year visit

Only consider a supervised dip after reviewing medical and organizer safety guidance, and never enter freezing water alone. Dress in dry layers for the return to warmth. For non-swimmers and families, watching the event or donating can be a better way to join.

Say Happy New Year locally

Happy New Year / Bonne Année!

A greeting associated with this destination; pronunciation and usage can vary by language or region.

Good to know before you go

Extreme-cold exposure carries real risks. Public plunges, fireworks and festival schedules depend on municipality and weather conditions.

Holiday plans are affected by weather, permits, crowd limits, local customs and transport schedules. Use this guide to understand the tradition, then confirm exact details for the year of your trip with the responsible event organizer or tourism authority.

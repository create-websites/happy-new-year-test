France: Le Réveillon de la Saint-Sylvestre

In France, New Year's Eve is known as la Saint-Sylvestre, and the celebration can be as intimate as a long meal or as public as a midnight walk through illuminated Paris. The Réveillon dinner is about lingering at the table, savoring conversation and welcoming the year without rushing.

🇫🇷 France🎊 January 1 traditions🌍 Around the world

What makes the celebration special

Classic festive menus may feature oysters, smoked salmon, foie gras, seafood and sparkling wine, though no particular dish is compulsory. Friends exchange kisses, toast with champagne when they choose to, and say Bonne Année. Paris's Eiffel Tower sparkles with its usual lighting schedule, but visitors should not confuse that with a guaranteed New Year's Eve fireworks show at the tower.

Where to experience it

For a major street celebration, look toward the Champs-Élysées and Arc de Triomphe, where recent official countdowns have used projection mapping and fireworks. Visit the Eiffel Tower and the Seine in daylight, then explore the neighborhoods of Paris. Beyond the capital, the French Riviera offers a milder-weather alternative with seaside promenades and special dinners.

Planning a memorable New Year visit

Reserve restaurants and river cruises well in advance; holiday menus are often fixed-price. Metro access and avenue closures can change on December 31, and winter evenings still require a coat. A calm French-style celebration might begin with a good meal and end with a neighborhood stroll.

Good to know before you go

The exact location, timing and format of Paris's public display change with municipal planning. Do not describe Eiffel Tower fireworks as an annual certainty.

Holiday plans are affected by weather, permits, crowd limits, local customs and transport schedules. Use this guide to understand the tradition, then confirm exact details for the year of your trip with the responsible event organizer or tourism authority.

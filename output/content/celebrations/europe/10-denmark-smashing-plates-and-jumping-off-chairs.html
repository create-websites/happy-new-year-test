Denmark: Smashing Plates and Jumping Off Chairs

In Denmark, the transition into January is celebrated with cheer, toasts and one especially physical gesture: many people climb onto a chair or sofa and jump down at the stroke of midnight, symbolically leaping into the year ahead.

🇩🇰 Denmark🎊 January 1 traditions🌍 Around the world

What makes the celebration special

Danish celebrations frequently include a festive dinner, televised speeches and a toast before the jump. Stories also circulate about smashing old plates on friends' doorsteps as a sign of affection or good fortune. That image is better understood as folklore or an uncommon practice than as an everyday activity to imitate; broken ceramics can injure people and create a mess. Say Godt Nytår.

Where to experience it

Copenhagen looks enchanting when Nyhavn's colorful façades meet winter lights. Tivoli Gardens is a seasonal favorite when its holiday program is operating, and the central streets offer many dining choices. Take time for a canal-side walk before nighttime crowds arrive.

Planning a memorable New Year visit

Book a restaurant and check Tivoli opening dates and any timed-entry rules. Public and private fireworks may be restricted by local regulations, and December weather can be wet and windy. Make the midnight jump low, stable and safe rather than turning it into a stunt.

Good to know before you go

The plate-smashing story is not a recommended visitor activity. The chair-jumping ritual and an evening meal give a far more representative celebration.

Holiday plans are affected by weather, permits, crowd limits, local customs and transport schedules. Use this guide to understand the tradition, then confirm exact details for the year of your trip with the responsible event organizer or tourism authority.

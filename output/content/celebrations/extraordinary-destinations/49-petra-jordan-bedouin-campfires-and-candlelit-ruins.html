Petra, Jordan: Bedouin Campfires and Candlelit Ruins

Petra offers a hauntingly beautiful setting for a winter trip to Jordan, where the sandstone walls of the Siq open onto the monumental Treasury. Travelers sometimes pair a daytime archaeological visit with a Bedouin-hosted evening or an organized candlelit experience.

🇯🇴 Petra, Jordan🎊 January 1 traditions🌍 Around the world

What makes the celebration special

Petra by Night is a separately ticketed program offered on selected evenings, when portions of the route may be lit by candles. It is not automatically held on New Year's Eve and should not be advertised as a guaranteed December 31 event. Bedouin tea, food and storytelling can be part of respectful locally organized gatherings. Sana sa'ida is an Arabic seasonal wish.

Where to experience it

Explore the Treasury and surrounding archaeological trails in daylight, allowing enough time for the entrance walk and steep terrain. Wadi Rum is a separate desert destination for guided stargazing or camp stays. A Petra visit and an overnight Wadi Rum stay need careful transfer planning.

Planning a memorable New Year visit

Carry warm layers for cold desert nights, buy tours from recognized operators and obey site rules. Never add unauthorized candles or fires to protected ruins, and respect the people who live and work around the heritage landscape.

Say Happy New Year locally

Sana sa'ida!

A greeting associated with this destination; pronunciation and usage can vary by language or region.

Good to know before you go

Candlelit Petra scenes are tied to scheduled visits, not a permanent nightly or New Year's Eve display. Public access and programs must be confirmed each year.

Holiday plans are affected by weather, permits, crowd limits, local customs and transport schedules. Use this guide to understand the tradition, then confirm exact details for the year of your trip with the responsible event organizer or tourism authority.

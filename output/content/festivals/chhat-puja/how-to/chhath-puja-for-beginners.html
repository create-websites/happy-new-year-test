Follow the four-day Chhath Puja outline, from Nahay Khay and Kharna to evening and morning Arghya, with preparation topics for first-time readers.

Understanding Chhath Puja: A Beginner's Overview

If you are observing or participating in Chhath Puja for the first time, the strict rules and four-day schedule can seem overwhelming. This guide simplifies the ancient Vedic festival dedicated to Surya Dev and Chhathi Maiya.

The Four-Day Breakdown

  • Day 1: Nahay Khay: Take a purifying bath, clean your home thoroughly, and eat a single pure meal of Kaddu Bhaat (bottle gourd and rice) made without onion or garlic.
  • Day 2: Kharna: Fast the entire day without water. In the evening, cook a special jaggery kheer (Rasiya) and roti. Offer it to the deity in silence, eat your share, and begin the 36-hour waterless fast.
  • Day 3: Sandhya Arghya: Prepare bamboo baskets (Daura and Soop) filled with fruits and Thekua. Head to a water body at sunset, stand in the water, and offer milk and water to the setting sun.
  • Day 4: Usha Arghya: Return to the water before dawn. Offer prayers to the rising sun, complete the rituals, and break your fast with ginger and water.
Beginner Tip: Focus on absolute purity. Wash your hands frequently, use separate utensils for cooking prasad, and follow the guidance of elder family members.

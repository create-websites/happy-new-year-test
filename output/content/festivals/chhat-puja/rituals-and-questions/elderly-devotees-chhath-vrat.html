Navigating Chhath Vrat in Old Age

Chhath is a lifelong commitment, but the 36-hour waterless fast and standing in cold water pose severe health risks for the elderly.

Practical Considerations and Exemptions

  • Transferring the Vrat: Tradition dictates that when a Vrati becomes too old or frail, the fast should be officially transferred to the next generation (a daughter-in-law or son). The elder can still guide the rituals without fasting.
  • Modified Fasting: If an elder insists on fasting, families often consult a doctor. They may be advised to skip the Nirjala (waterless) aspect and consume water or fruit to maintain blood pressure.
  • Avoiding Cold Water: Elderly Vratis can offer the Arghya by sitting near the water or standing in a tub of slightly warm water at home to prevent hypothermia or joint pain.

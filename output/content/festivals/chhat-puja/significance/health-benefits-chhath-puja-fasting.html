Chhath Puja Fasting and Sun Exposure: Claims & Safety

Explore the supplied claims about Chhath fasting, detoxification, autophagy and sunlight, alongside clearly separated health and eye-safety notes.

By Prashant DhingraWeb edition updated 17 September 2026Article 2 of 20

In this article

The draft connects a waterless fast with detoxification and autophagy, and Arghya with vitamin D. It supplies no clinical evidence for these claims. The page preserves the source while distinguishing the claims from cited safety information.

Health and evidence note : separate from the source

The supplied draft does not establish that a 36-hour waterless fast detoxifies the body, safely triggers beneficial autophagy, or improves immunity. NCCIH reports limited evidence for detox claims and warns that fasting can cause weakness, fainting and dehydration. NIH ODS notes that vitamin D production varies with factors including time of day and season; sunrise or sunset is not a guaranteed vitamin D treatment. Discuss fasting with a qualified clinician, especially when health conditions or medicines are involved.

The Science of Chhath: Detoxification and Healing

Beyond its profound spiritual importance, Chhath Puja involves practices that align beautifully with natural healing and Ayurvedic principles.

Fasting and Autophagy

The 36-hour Nirjala fast acts as a massive detox for the digestive system. In modern science, prolonged fasting triggers autophagy, a cellular cleanup process that reduces inflammation and promotes cellular regeneration.

Safe Sun Exposure (Heliotherapy)

Offering Arghya during precise sunrise and sunset windows exposes the body to safe levels of ultraviolet radiation. This stimulates the synthesis of Vitamin D, which is crucial for bone health and seasonal immunity right before the harsh winter begins.

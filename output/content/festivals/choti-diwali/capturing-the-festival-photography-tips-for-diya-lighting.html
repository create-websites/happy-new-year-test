Capturing the Festival: Photography Tips for Diya Lighting

Diya photographs are most effective when they capture the warmth of the scene while ensuring people do not have to take any risks near the flames.

Article 47 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Food, hosting & celebration/Article 47

Diya photographs are most effective when they capture the warmth of the scene while ensuring people do not have to take any risks near the flames. Choti Diwali offers close details, portraits and household stories. Choose the picture you want before the lamps are lit.

Begin with a stable composition

Remove any visual distractions and select an angle that places the lamp on a stable surface. Using a tray, hands arranging unlit diyas, or an entrance detail can help convey the story. Ensure children stay at a safe distance and do not move a lit lamp just to enhance the background.

Protect the flame detail

On a phone, tap close to the brightest spot and lower exposure if the flame appears completely washed out. Try a few shots since camera apps differ. A compatible camera performs better in low light; portrait subjects should remain relaxed and avoid stiff poses.

Use surrounding light thoughtfully

A slight amount of ambient light can illuminate faces while maintaining a cozy ambiance. Do not put fabric or makeshift diffusers close to an open flame. Battery-powered lamps come in handy when setting up a posed portrait requiring multiple adjustments.

Capture people as well as objects

With consent, capture images of the preparation, a communal greeting, and the completed meal. Snap a few shots, then set the camera aside. The evening ought to be an enjoyable experience for everyone, not an extended photo session disrupted by worship.

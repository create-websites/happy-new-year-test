Choddo Shak: The 14 Leafy Greens of Bengal

Choddo Shak, meaning fourteen greens, is a Bengali seasonal food tradition linked to Bhoot Chaturdashi. This dish combines culinary practice with family heritage.

Article 24 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Regional traditions/Article 24

Choddo Shak, meaning fourteen greens, is a Bengali seasonal food tradition linked to Bhoot Chaturdashi. This dish combines culinary practice with family heritage. The exact selection can vary with locality, market availability and household practice.

Shop with someone who knows the greens

Markets might offer a ready selection throughout the season. Request the names and check freshness instead of assuming each bundle holds the same species. Avoid foraging unknown leaves or relying solely on a photo to identify edible plants.

Prepare a manageable dish

Separate the leaves, remove any damaged parts, and rinse off all soil completely. Prepare the chosen leaves following a reliable family recipe. A straightforward method can highlight the various textures and flavors better than a dish with heavy seasoning.

Allow for availability

Diaspora households might not have access to fourteen familiar greens. They can prepare a smaller, recognized variety, note the traditional names, and describe the custom during the meal. Counting should never lead to including an unfamiliar plant.

Keep cultural and health claims separate

The meal can symbolize protection or seasonal renewal within a family's beliefs. That does not establish that fourteen greens prevent supernatural harm or cure disease. Serve it as a valued food tradition and enjoy the conversation it makes possible.

Corporate and Personal Gifting on Day Two

On the second day of Diwali, gifting usually takes place amidst last-minute deliveries and family gatherings. A considerate present can remain simple, practical, and clearly intended.

Article 43 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Food, hosting & celebration/Article 43

On the second day of Diwali, gifting usually takes place amidst last-minute deliveries and family gatherings. A considerate present can remain simple, practical, and clearly intended. Timing matters less than whether the recipient feels considered and can receive it conveniently.

Start with the recipient

For relatives, consider food preferences, household size and whether a perishable item will be welcome. For customers or colleagues, check the organisation's gift policy and choose something proportionate to the relationship. A personal note can make a simple item memorable.

Keep the contents transparent

Include allergens and storage instructions on food gifts. Dry fruit boxes, sweets, and reusable household items appeal to various recipients; none is inherently the right option. Do not presume that every family prefers religious items or silver coins.

Plan the final delivery

Verify the address and ensure someone will be present. Group nearby deliveries together instead of making multiple hurried trips. If a delivery is delayed, communicate clearly rather than leaving a perishable package unattended.

Reduce unnecessary packaging

Whenever possible, use a durable reusable container and avoid unnecessary layers of decorative materials. A clear label paired with a heartfelt message often has more impact than a large hamper. Maintain a straightforward record of sent gifts to avoid duplicates and ensure no one is overlooked.

Decluttering Rules Before the Evening Puja

The last Choti Diwali cleaning should make the home more pleasant to live in, rather than tire everyone out before the main festivities. Cleaning can hold symbolic significance…

Article 34 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Lamps & home preparation/Article 34

The last Choti Diwali cleaning should aim to make the home more enjoyable, rather than tire everyone out before the main festivities. While cleaning often symbolizes welcome and renewal, there is no universal set of decluttering rules that every household must follow.

Prioritise the spaces you will use

Start with the entrance, bathroom, kitchen countertop, and worship space. Ensure clear pathways and place essential items in visible, accessible spots. Save organizing cupboards and major sorting tasks for another time unless they are crucial to the celebration.

Use three simple destinations

Return useful objects to their places, set aside items for a considered donation, and separate actual rubbish. Do not throw away documents, family keepsakes or someone else's belongings during a rushed festive sweep.

Share work visibly

Delegate small tasks with well-defined goals: wipe a table, set up a tray, or empty a specific bin. Incorporate preparation and cleanup into the plan instead of expecting one person to handle all leftover work once guests have arrived.

Stop at a sensible time

Set aside time to wash, dress, and eat. The notion of removing stagnant energy is a traditional belief, not proof that a broken item brings bad luck. A home that feels lived-in can remain inviting, and a peaceful family is more important than an impeccably arranged shelf.

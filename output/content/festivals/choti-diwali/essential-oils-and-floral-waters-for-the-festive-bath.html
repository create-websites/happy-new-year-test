Essential Oils and Floral Waters for the Festive Bath

Rose water, kewra, and sandalwood fragrances can conjure a festive bath by invoking memory and ambiance. These are optional enhancements, not essential components for…

Article 19 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Bathing & renewal/Article 19

Rose water, kewra, and sandalwood fragrances can evoke the feeling of a festive bath through memory and ambiance. These are optional elements, not essential components of Choti Diwali. A pleasant aroma does not prove that a product cleanses the body or an aura.

Read what the product actually is

A culinary floral water, a cosmetic toner, and a concentrated essential oil are distinct products. Use each solely for its intended purpose as indicated on the label. Do not assume that a bottle meant for sweets is also safe for skin, or that a diffuser oil should be added to bathwater.

Keep fragrance modest

A simple bath is ideal if someone in the home dislikes scents or has had adverse reactions before. Even natural fragrances can cause irritation. Do not apply undiluted essential oils directly to the skin, nor create a dilution using an untrusted online recipe.

Create atmosphere without skin exposure

Fresh flowers placed safely outside the splash area or a clean towel can provide a festive touch. Keep oils and glass containers out of children's reach and away from any lamp flame.

Make the ritual welcoming

Ask before scenting a shared bathroom. Let family members choose a fragrance-free option without explaining or defending their preference. The experience should leave people comfortable and ready to join the celebration, rather than trapped in someone else's idea of a perfect ritual.

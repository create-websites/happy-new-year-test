Hanuman Puja on Choti Diwali

Certain North Indian families incorporate Hanuman worship during the Diwali season, particularly on Chaturdashi. The emphasis is on devotion, strength, service, and…

Article 8 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Stories & significance/Article 8

In certain North Indian households, Hanuman worship is incorporated during the Diwali season, particularly on Chaturdashi. This practice emphasizes devotion, strength, service, and comfort. It is a regional or family tradition rather than a mandatory observance for all who celebrate Choti Diwali.

Prepare a calm worship space

Tidy the space surrounding an existing image or murti and place a small lamp, water, flowers, and a customary offering. A printed prayer book can assist everyone in participating. Ensure the arrangement is simple enough to remain easy to oversee and clean up afterward.

Offer according to established practice

Sindoor and oil are associated with Hanuman worship in some traditions. Ask the temple or follow your household's practice before applying anything to a murti. Pigments and oils can damage surfaces, and a temple may have specific rules about offerings.

Read with understanding

A brief devotional reading or a well-known section of the Hanuman Chalisa can be paired with a discussion on service and bravery. Translating a few verses for children might capture their interest better than hastily going through a lengthy program they find hard to understand.

Carry devotion into action

Assist an elder, contribute to a household chore, or offer practical help to someone in need. Frame protection from harmful influences as a matter of religious faith, without asserting that a ritual confirms the presence of spirits or substitutes for everyday care.

How to Make the Perfect Traditional Ubtan

Article 13 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Bathing & renewal/Article 13

Traditional ubtan is a freshly prepared paste used in certain celebratory bathing rituals. Typical home recipes consist of gram flour combined with a pinch of turmeric and water or another common liquid. Formulations differ, and there is no one required blend for Choti Diwali.

Mix only what you need

As a sample household recipe, mix one tablespoon of fine gram flour with a tiny pinch of turmeric and add enough plain water to create a smooth paste. This serves as a cultural recipe example and is not intended as a remedy for any skin condition. Ensure utensils are clean and prepare a small fresh portion.

Choose simplicity over novelty

Sandalwood, rose water, and dairy components are found in certain family recipes, but including additional ingredients doesn’t necessarily improve the paste. Scents, spices, and food elements may irritate the skin. If an ingredient has previously triggered a reaction, omit it instead of blindly following the recipe.

Apply without scrubbing

Those opting to use ubtan should avoid contact with the eyes and broken skin, refrain from harsh scrubbing, and rinse it off gently. Discontinue use if irritation arises. A gentle cleanser and simple bath are suitable alternatives, particularly for individuals with sensitive skin.

Preserve the family recipe thoughtfully

Document the person who shared the recipe, its ingredients, and the significance associated with it. Maintain modesty in cosmetic claims: appreciating a ritual differs from demonstrating that a paste detoxifies, whitens, or medically enhances the skin.

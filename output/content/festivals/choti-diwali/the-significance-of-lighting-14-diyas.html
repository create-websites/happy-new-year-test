The Significance of Lighting 14 Diyas

Fourteen lamps hold a special significance in Bengali Bhoot Chaturdashi rituals. Families often associate this number with honoring their ancestors.

Article 5 of 50 · 2 min read · Prashant Dhingra

Choti Diwali/Stories & significance/Article 5

Fourteen lamps hold a special significance in Bengali Bhoot Chaturdashi rituals. Families often associate this number with honoring their ancestors. Other Choti Diwali households light different numbers, so fourteen diyas should not be presented as a universal requirement across India.

Give each lamp a thoughtful place

An entrance, a tidy courtyard border, a window sill meant for a lamp, and a monitored puja spot can create a soft path of light. Plan the layout with a sketch before placing the diyas. Avoid unstable railings, curtains, stair edges, and locations where a lamp could easily be tipped over.

Remember people rather than counting anxiously

Encourage relatives to identify an ancestor, recount a generous deed, or share a family story. Some families trace back fourteen generations; others interpret the custom differently. The key editorial difference lies between a meaningful tradition and a mandatory formula applied to every household.

Adapt the display to the home

A small apartment might not accommodate fourteen appropriate flame spots. Use a supervised cluster of clay lamps in a single secure area and battery-powered lights in other places. Avoid placing lamps near rubbish or flammable storage, despite older advice suggesting lighting overlooked corners.

Make the evening participatory

Have children organize unlit lamps or create a placement diagram while an adult manages the lighting. Take a photo of the completed setup before anyone starts moving around the area.

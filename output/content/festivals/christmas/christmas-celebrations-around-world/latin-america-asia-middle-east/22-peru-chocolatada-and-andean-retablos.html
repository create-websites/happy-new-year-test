Peru: Chocolatada and Andean Retablos

Peru's Christmas season blends generosity, craft and the high Andes. Chocolatadas bring communities together around sweet hot drinks, while miniature carved Nativity scenes and retablos carry intricate local artistic traditions.

🇵🇪 PeruChristmas Eve🎄 Christmas traditions

How Christmas is celebrated

A chocolatada often involves sharing hot chocolate and panetón with children or neighbors, sometimes organized by schools, churches or charitable groups. Andean retablos: painted boxes with figures: can portray Nativity stories and wider community life, linking religious narratives with regional craft.

What is on the festive table?

Panetón, Peru's popular version of panettone, appears in homes and gift baskets. Hot chocolate is especially symbolic even where the December climate is not cold.

Where to experience the season

Cusco's Plaza de Armas is a dramatic place to experience the season, and the Santurantikuy Christmas Eve artisan fair is traditionally associated with its historic center. Explore stalls for handmade figures and regional Christmas artistry.

Planning a Christmas visit

Cusco sits at high altitude, so acclimatize and plan calm first days. Fair layouts and access rules change; respect religious processions and seek permission before photographing individual artisans.

Local context matters

Chocolatadas are community activities with many organizers, not one centrally scheduled attraction.

Before you make travel plans

Public markets, parades, worship services and exhibitions change their dates, entry policies and scale from one season to the next. Check the current official event calendar, applicable travel advisories and local cultural guidance before booking. This guide describes traditions, not a promise that a particular event will run every year.

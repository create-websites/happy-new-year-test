Spain: The Three Kings and Sweet Turrón

Spanish Christmas is not confined to 25 December. The season builds from family meals and twinkling city streets toward the arrival of the Three Kings, whose parades on 5 January and gift-giving on 6 January are central to many households.

🇪🇸 Spain25 December & 6 January🎄 Christmas traditions

How Christmas is celebrated

The Reyes Magos: Melchior, Gaspar and Balthazar: feature prominently in the holiday imagination. Children often watch a cabalgata procession on the eve of Epiphany. Some families also exchange presents on Christmas Day, so the old and newer customs coexist rather than exclude one another.

What is on the festive table?

Turrón, a nougat of almonds, honey and sugar, belongs on many festive tables alongside polvorones and roscón de Reyes, the ring-shaped cake associated with January's Three Kings celebrations.

Where to experience the season

Madrid's illuminated shopping streets and seasonal markets make an engaging winter base. Barcelona offers a different blend of Catalan customs, markets and Gothic Quarter walking routes; the two cities are best explored on separate stays.

Planning a Christmas visit

To experience the Three Kings, plan for 5–6 January rather than just Christmas week. Parade routes and street closures can change, and January 6 is a public holiday with altered opening hours.

Local context matters

Catalan traditions such as the caga tió add another layer; Christmas customs in Spain differ considerably by autonomous community.

Before you make travel plans

Public markets, parades, worship services and exhibitions change their dates, entry policies and scale from one season to the next. Check the current official event calendar, applicable travel advisories and local cultural guidance before booking. This guide describes traditions, not a promise that a particular event will run every year.

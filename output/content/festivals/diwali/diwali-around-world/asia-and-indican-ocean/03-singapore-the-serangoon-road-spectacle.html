Singapore: The Serangoon Road Spectacle

Singapore's Little India turns Deepavali into a neighborhood-wide festival of light, commerce and shared cultural space. Overhead ornaments stretch across Serangoon Road, while Campbell Lane and surrounding streets offer food, textiles, flower garlands and seasonal activities.

SingaporeLittle India light-upSerangoon Road and Campbell Lane

The story behind the celebration

Deepavali is a public holiday in Singapore, and Hindu households mark it with prayer, festive cleaning and visits. The large public light-up is a different kind of celebration: it makes the festival visible to people of many backgrounds. Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple and other religious sites offer an important reminder that the lights have a spiritual meaning beyond decoration.

What makes this Diwali celebration distinctive?

Walk the neighborhood at dusk, when the arches and lights come into their own. The Tekka Centre area and the Indian Heritage Centre add food and historical context. Seasonal bazaars often include clothing, henna and sweets, though individual vendors and activity dates change every year.

Food, family and community

Jangiri, murukku, payasam and other South Asian treats may be sold in Little India. The district is busy year-round, so make time to visit independent traders and learn about Singapore's diverse Tamil, North Indian and other Indian communities.

Where to experience it

Use Little India or Jalan Besar MRT rather than driving into the busiest streets. A gentle route runs through Serangoon Road, Campbell Lane and Tekka; add an indoor museum visit during the hottest part of the day.

How to plan responsibly

For 2026, local organizers listed a street light-up and Festival Village ahead of Deepavali on 8 November. Verify exact operating hours and access on the official event site before arriving, particularly if visiting on a weekend evening.

Important cultural context

The public decorations represent the local festival; the provided Diwali artwork in this guide is illustrative and is not a photograph of Serangoon Road.

Diwali across Asia and the Indian Ocean

From the animal-honoring days of Tihar to Singapore's Little India and Indo-Fijian family traditions, this wave explores celebrations shaped by neighboring histories and island communities.

Nepal, Southeast Asia, Fiji, Mauritius and Reunion. Explore the traditions, communities and travel considerations without assuming that all festivals operate on the same calendar.

Wave 1 · Article 01

Nepal's Tihar: Worshipping Crows, Dogs, and Cows

In Nepal, the festival of lights is called Tihar, and its five-day sequence tells a story about gratitude as well as illumination. Crows, dogs and cows are honored on different days, while household Lakshmi worship and the final bond between siblings make the celebration distinctly Nepali.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 02

Malaysia: Hari Diwali in Kuala Lumpur

Kuala Lumpur marks Deepavali with glowing kolam patterns, temple visits and family open houses. Brickfields, widely known as Little India, becomes a particularly lively neighborhood as shoppers look for new clothes, flowers, sweets and festive decorations.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 03

Singapore: The Serangoon Road Spectacle

Singapore's Little India turns Deepavali into a neighborhood-wide festival of light, commerce and shared cultural space. Overhead ornaments stretch across Serangoon Road, while Campbell Lane and surrounding streets offer food, textiles, flower garlands and seasonal activities.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 04

Sri Lanka: Tamil Traditions in the Island Nation

Among Sri Lanka's Tamil Hindu communities, Deepavali is woven into the rhythm of family, temple and neighborhood life. The day is often more intimate than a large street spectacle, with prayers and visits framed by meals, new clothing and the sharing of sweets.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 05

Fiji: A National Holiday Across the Pacific

In Fiji, Diwali is part of the country's shared public calendar, reflecting generations of Indo-Fijian history. Clay lamps, illuminated homes and visits with neighbors give the Festival of Lights a special place in a Pacific island society.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 06

Mauritius: Beachfront Illuminations and Rangoli

Mauritius celebrates Divali with rows of tiny flames outside homes, colorful rangoli and the generous exchange of homemade sweets. Across the island, familiar South Asian rituals have taken root in a distinctly Mauritian landscape and multicultural society.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 07

Indonesia: Balinese Hinduism and Diwali

Diwali is observed by some Indian families and community organizations in Indonesia, but it is not a central island-wide Balinese Hindu holiday. Understanding that distinction makes this destination more interesting, not less: two living religious traditions share ideas of light and dharma without identical calendars or rituals.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 08

Thailand: The Loi Krathong Connection

Bangkok offers a revealing meeting point between Indian Diwali customs and Thailand's own rich festival traditions. Indian community celebrations bring prayer, sweets and family gatherings, while the famous Loi Krathong festival illustrates how different cultures honor light for different reasons.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 09

Myanmar (Burma): Lighting Up Yangon

Yangon's Hindu temples and long-established Indian-origin communities preserve a lesser-known chapter in the global story of Diwali. Celebrations are generally rooted in households and religious spaces rather than in a nationwide public festival.

Read the guide →
Wave 1 · Article 10

Reunion Island: Dipavali with a French Twist

On Reunion, a French overseas department in the Indian Ocean, Dipavali reflects the island's Tamil-descended communities and a broader Creole cultural setting. The festival can be public, artistic and deeply personal at once, with lights, dance and shared celebrations.

Read the guide →

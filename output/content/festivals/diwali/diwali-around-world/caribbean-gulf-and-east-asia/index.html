Diwali in the Caribbean, Gulf and East Asia

Here the story moves from illuminated Guyanese motorcades and Divali Nagar to merchant history, neighborhood temples and expatriate festivities across Asia.

Trinidad, Guyana, Suriname, the Gulf, Japan and Hong Kong. Explore the traditions, communities and travel considerations without assuming that all festivals operate on the same calendar.

Trinidad and Tobago: The Magic of Divali Nagar

Divali in Trinidad and Tobago is both a national holiday and a defining expression of Indo-Caribbean culture. The celebration brings devotional light to homes and public spaces, while Divali Nagar creates a major gathering point for music, dance, education and community identity.

Wave 3 · Article 22

Guyana: The Spectacular Diwali Motorcades

Guyana's Diwali motorcades transform the festival of lights into a moving public artwork. Vehicles and decorated floats carry complex illuminated designs through local streets, while households and mandirs continue the quieter observances of prayer and hospitality.

Read the guide →
Wave 3 · Article 23

Suriname: Deepavali in South America

Suriname celebrates Deepavali through the traditions of its Hindustani communities and the wider country's multicultural life. Diyas, devotional music, family gatherings and public cultural programs make the day a recognizable national observance in South America.

Read the guide →
Wave 3 · Article 24

Jamaica: Blending Caribbean Vibe with Indian Tradition

Diwali in Jamaica is often celebrated on a smaller scale than in Trinidad or Guyana. That intimacy is part of the story: Indian-descended families and community groups maintain relationships to the festival within a broader Jamaican cultural landscape.

Read the guide →
Wave 3 · Article 25

United Arab Emirates: The Dubai Gold Souk Rush

Dubai's Diwali season moves between glittering retail districts and the private rituals of a large Indian expatriate community. The Gold Souk attracts particular interest around Dhanteras, while homes and community venues host prayers, dinners and festive gatherings.

Read the guide →
Wave 3 · Article 26

Oman: Muscat's Illuminated Balconies

Oman's Diwali traditions reflect longstanding connections between the Arabian Peninsula and traders from western India. In Muscat, homes and businesses may glow with lamps, while religious observances and family visits generally take place within the Indian expatriate community.

Read the guide →
Wave 3 · Article 27

Qatar and Bahrain: Middle Eastern Festivities

Qatar and Bahrain share deep commercial and social ties with Indian communities, yet Diwali takes different public forms in each country. Families and colleagues may celebrate in homes and community venues; access to formal religious spaces and large gatherings varies by location.

Read the guide →
Wave 3 · Article 28

Japan: Diwali in Tokyo and Yokohama

In Japan, Diwali is celebrated by Indian families, students and professionals whose growing communities create pockets of light and color within the urban landscape. Tokyo's Edogawa area and Yokohama are often discussed in connection with Indian cultural gatherings.

Read the guide →
Wave 3 · Article 29

Hong Kong: Chungking Mansions and Tsim Sha Tsui

Hong Kong's Diwali celebrations sit at the intersection of Indian merchant history, global finance and busy neighborhood life. The festival can be seen in restaurants, shops and family gatherings, particularly around the city's longstanding South Asian networks.

Read the guide →
Wave 3 · Article 30

Philippines: The Manila Diaspora

In metropolitan Manila, Diwali's warmth is found largely in the Indian diaspora's family and community circles. The holiday reflects longstanding merchant networks as well as newer professionals and students who maintain the festival far from home.

Read the guide →

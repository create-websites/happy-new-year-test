United Kingdom: Diwali at Trafalgar Square, London

Diwali on the Square brings the Festival of Lights into London's civic heart. Trafalgar Square fills with music, dance, food and activities created by communities who celebrate Diwali, turning a religiously rooted holiday into an opportunity for respectful public exchange.

London, United KingdomDiwali on the SquareTrafalgar Square cultural festival

The story behind the celebration

The event has highlighted Hindu, Sikh and Jain voices, each with its own relationship to the season. Traditional and contemporary dances, family workshops, clothing demonstrations and vegetarian food express a broad public festival rather than a single household ritual.

What makes this Diwali celebration distinctive?

The backdrop of the National Gallery and the square's open terraces gives the event a uniquely London setting. Visitors can watch performances, learn about symbolic lamps and attend hands-on workshops. Activities and the opening sequence vary by year, and the best approach is to follow the official program.

Food, family and community

Food stalls are a welcoming entry point: ask vendors about regional dishes, ingredients and festival significance. Londoners from many communities join the celebrations, while family pujas and other observances take place separately across the city.

Where to experience it

Use Underground or rail to reach the West End, allowing extra time for security screening and crowds. Trafalgar Square is close to cultural institutions that can deepen the day's experience.

How to plan responsibly

The Mayor of London's 2026 Diwali on the Square is advertised for Sunday 25 October, 1pm-7pm, free to attend. Check the City Hall page for accessibility facilities, transport information and any changes before traveling.

Important cultural context

A free public event does not represent every tradition behind Diwali; its strength is bringing several communities into a shared civic space.

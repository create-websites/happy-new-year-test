Diwali in Europe, North America, Australia and Africa

Major public festivals, local diaspora communities and historic Indian Ocean connections show how Diwali travels between civic squares and family homes.

Leicester, London, North America, Australasia and East Africa. Explore the traditions, communities and travel considerations without assuming that all festivals operate on the same calendar.

United Kingdom: The Leicester Mega-Celebration

Leicester's Golden Mile has long made Diwali visible in public life, with Belgrave Road's elaborate lights, family shopping and neighborhood restaurants. The celebration is one of the city's signature cultural events, though the program has changed in recent years for crowd safety.

Wave 2 · Article 12

United Kingdom: Diwali at Trafalgar Square, London

Diwali on the Square brings the Festival of Lights into London's civic heart. Trafalgar Square fills with music, dance, food and activities created by communities who celebrate Diwali, turning a religiously rooted holiday into an opportunity for respectful public exchange.

Wave 2 · Article 13

USA: The White House and Times Square

Diwali has become increasingly visible in American civic life, from ceremonies hosted by public officials in Washington to large cultural gatherings in New York. Yet the festival's deepest meaning still lives in family prayer, neighborhood networks and community organizations.

Read the guide →
Wave 2 · Article 14

USA: Diwali in Silicon Valley

In Silicon Valley, Diwali moves between homes, community halls, temples, schools and workplaces. Families who arrived from different parts of India bring distinct foods and traditions, while younger generations reinterpret the festival through music, cultural fairs and neighborhood invitations.

Read the guide →
Wave 2 · Article 15

Canada: Brampton's Fireworks and Festivities

In Brampton and the wider Greater Toronto Area, Diwali reaches beyond private family homes into markets, community centers and an exceptionally busy festive shopping season. The scale of local South Asian communities makes the holiday visible throughout neighborhoods and businesses.

Read the guide →
Wave 2 · Article 16

Australia: Lighting the Sydney Opera House

Diwali arrives during Australian spring, creating a striking contrast with the autumn celebrations familiar in the Northern Hemisphere. From Sydney's waterfront to Melbourne's city squares, multicultural festivals and symbolic landmark lighting bring the holiday into public view.

Read the guide →
Wave 2 · Article 17

New Zealand: The Auckland Diwali Festival

Auckland makes Diwali a major part of the city's public cultural calendar through a multi-day festival of music, dance, arts and vegetarian food. Visitors can enjoy a spirited downtown gathering while learning that the celebration has a deeper spiritual meaning in many family homes.

Read the guide →
Wave 2 · Article 18

South Africa: Deepavali in Durban

Durban's Deepavali traditions are inseparable from the history of Indian South Africans and generations of cultural continuity. Lighted homes, prayer services and sweet-making connect contemporary families to a story that survived colonialism and apartheid.

Read the guide →
Wave 2 · Article 19

Kenya: East African Deepavali

In Kenya, Diwali is celebrated by Indian-origin families whose connections to East Africa span generations. Nairobi and Mombasa each offer a different environment for temple worship, family gatherings and the sharing of lights and sweets.

Read the guide →
Wave 2 · Article 20

Tanzania: Dar es Salaam's Festive Nights

Dar es Salaam's Deepavali evenings reflect a long Indian Ocean history of Gujarati trading networks, religious institutions and multicultural city life. The festival often unfolds in temples, businesses and homes rather than at one central public countdown.

Read the guide →

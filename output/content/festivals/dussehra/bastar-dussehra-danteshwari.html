Bastar Dussehra: The 75-Day Festival of Goddess Danteshwari

Discover the Bastar Dussehra festival, a 75-day tribal celebration focused on Goddess Danteshwari, local customs, and community involvement, as outlined in the editorial brief.

Dussehra Hub
Why this guide matters

Bastar Dussehra defies the notion that every Dussehra is centered around Rama, Ravana, and burning effigies. Instead, it focuses on Goddess Danteshwari and the rich ritual traditions of Bastar.

India’s unusually long Dussehra cycle

The editorial plan outlines Bastar Dussehra as a 75-day celebration, ranking it among the lengthiest festival cycles linked to Dussehra. The rituals progress gradually instead of being condensed into a single evening.

The elongated timeframe mirrors a sacred calendar unique to the region, with its own set of principles and communal duties.

Goddess Danteshwari at the center

In contrast to the Rama-Ravana narrative of North India, Bastar Dussehra honors Goddess Danteshwari, reflecting the region's rich goddess traditions, tribal involvement, and historical significance.

Visitors must steer clear of regarding it as just another form of Ravana Dahan.

Community labor and ritual participation

The success of large public festivals hinges on various contributions such as processional tasks, ceremonial roles, music, crafts, and group coordination. Bastar's festivities exemplify the significance of community involvement evolving into a sacred tradition.

The event highlights the preservation of regional identity through recurring public ceremonies.

How to approach as a visitor

Visitors should prioritize cultural sensitivity, seek guidance from locals, and obtain consent before taking photographs. Ritual spaces should not be treated as performances for outsiders, even when they draw in travelers.

A mindful visit prioritizes listening and understanding over simply gathering experiences.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Is Bastar Dussehra about Ravana Dahan?

The festival revolves around Goddess Danteshwari and the traditional customs of Bastar.

Why is it described as 75 days?

The editorial brief describes it as a lengthy ritual cycle spanning approximately 75 days.

Where is it celebrated?

It is associated with the Bastar region of Chhattisgarh.

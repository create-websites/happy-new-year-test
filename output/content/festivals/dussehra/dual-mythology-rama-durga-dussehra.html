Cultural & Mythological Origins

The Dual Mythology of Dussehra: Rama and Durga

Examine the convergence of Rama's triumph over Ravana and Durga's vanquishing of Mahishasura during Vijayadashami, highlighting the essence of righteousness and rejuvenation in both traditions.

Dussehra Hub
Why this guide matters

Dussehra is not centered on a single narrative. The variations in the festival's celebration across different regions can be attributed to the incorporation of both the Ramayana and Shakta traditions.

Two victory stories, one festival

In north India, Dussehra marks the end of the Ramayana cycle as Lord Rama triumphs over Ravana. Similarly, in eastern and Shakta traditions, Vijayadashami concludes Durga Puja by honoring Goddess Durga's victory over Mahishasura. Both stories highlight the festival as a moment of restoring balance and defeating chaos.

The distinct stories, rather than being interchangeable, serve as a useful reminder that Indian festivals often bring together various regional memories on the same sacred calendar day.

Rama, Ravana and the public drama of Dussehra

The Ramlila performances depict scenes from the Ramayana before Dussehra, culminating in the burning of Ravana effigies to symbolize the downfall of unchecked power.

In families, the focus is usually not on rejoicing in the downfall of an enemy, but rather on contemplating courage, loyalty, duty, and the repercussions of pride.

Durga, Mahishasura and Vijayadashami

In Bengal and other eastern areas, the emotional journey varies. Durga Puja honors the Goddess as a nurturing and protective figure, while Vijayadashami signifies both triumph and bidding farewell as the deity's image is readied for immersion.

The triumph over Mahishasura is not only a celebration of strength, but also a recognition of tenderness, community, homecoming, departure, and the promise of reunion.

What unites the traditions

The common theme is the revival of dharma. Evil is depicted not just as an external threat; the tales prompt contemplation on ego, rage, unfairness, dread, and abuse of authority.

This is why Vijayadashami is able to evolve with modern life, as it can be both spiritual and communal, while also being personal and theatrical.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Are Dussehra and Vijayadashami the same festival?

Regional customs and names may vary, but they typically all refer to the same tenth-day culmination.

Why are both Rama and Durga associated with the day?

Various regional customs associate Vijayadashami with distinct sacred stories of triumph and rebirth.

Is Ravana Dahan practiced everywhere?

No, it is a significant aspect of various north Indian festivities, with other areas focusing on Durga immersion, processions, Shastra Puja, or worship of local deities.

Cultural & Mythological Origins

The Dual Mythology of Dussehra: Rama and Durga

Explore how Vijayadashami brings together Rama’s victory over Ravana and Durga’s defeat of Mahishasura in two powerful traditions of dharma and renewal.

Dussehra Hub
Why this guide matters

Dussehra is not built around one story alone. Understanding both the Ramayana and Shakta traditions explains why the festival can look very different from one region to another.

Two victory stories, one festival

Across much of north India, Dussehra culminates the Ramayana cycle with Lord Rama’s victory over Ravana. In many eastern and Shakta traditions, Vijayadashami closes Durga Puja with the remembrance of Goddess Durga defeating Mahishasura. Both narratives place the festival at the point where disorder is overcome and moral or cosmic balance is restored.

The stories are distinct rather than interchangeable. Their coexistence is a useful reminder that Indian festivals often gather multiple regional memories around the same sacred calendar day.

Rama, Ravana and the public drama of Dussehra

Ramlila performances retell episodes from the Ramayana in the days leading to Dussehra. The burning of Ravana effigies turns the final confrontation into a public visual symbol: power without restraint ultimately destroys itself.

For families, the story is often less about celebrating an enemy’s destruction and more about reflecting on courage, loyalty, duty and the consequences of pride.

Durga, Mahishasura and Vijayadashami

In Bengal and other eastern regions, the emotional arc is different. Durga Puja celebrates the Goddess as a protective and maternal presence, and Vijayadashami marks both victory and farewell as the image of the Goddess is prepared for immersion.

The victory over Mahishasura is therefore paired with tenderness: strength is celebrated, but so are community, homecoming, departure and the hope of return.

What unites the traditions

The shared thread is the restoration of dharma. Evil is not presented only as an external monster; the stories invite reflection on ego, anger, injustice, fear and the misuse of power.

That is why Vijayadashami remains adaptable to modern life. The festival can be devotional, theatrical, communal and deeply personal at the same time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Are Dussehra and Vijayadashami the same festival?

They usually refer to the same tenth-day culmination, though regional customs and names differ.

Why are both Rama and Durga associated with the day?

Different regional traditions connect Vijayadashami with different sacred narratives of victory and renewal.

Is Ravana Dahan practiced everywhere?

No. It is prominent in many north Indian celebrations, while other regions emphasize Durga immersion, processions, Shastra Puja or local deities.

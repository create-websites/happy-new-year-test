How to Plan a Fair and Fun Easter Egg Hunt

Organize an egg hunt suitable for all ages, featuring fair guidelines, imaginative hints, allergy-conscious treats, and secure hiding locations.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCrafts, Decor & DIY

Separate age groups when possible

Young children and older kids search at varying paces, so consider creating separate zones - one easy and visible for toddlers and another more challenging for older children, or run two quick rounds.

Count every egg before hiding

Maintain a written tally and, for bigger hunts, make a mental note of the general area where each batch of eggs is concealed to avoid discovering a misplaced chocolate egg weeks later and to track the progress of the hunt.

Balance candy with tokens and clues

Combine candy with stickers, small puzzles, coupons, coins, or color-coded tokens that can be exchanged for bigger rewards, which can be helpful for kids with various dietary requirements.

Choose safe hiding places

Steer clear of roads, grills, prickly plants, ponds, tool sheds, and areas that involve climbing. Before visiting public parks, be sure to check the local regulations and have a well-defined adult-to-child supervision strategy in place.

Frequently asked questions

How many eggs should each child get?

Select a target without a set number and implement color coding or limits to ensure inclusion of younger or slower children.

How can I make a hunt fair for different ages?

Use separate zones, staggered start times or assigned egg colors.

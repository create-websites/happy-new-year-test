The Easter Bunny and Painted Eggs: Folklore Explained

Trace the rabbit, decorated egg and gift-bringing traditions that shaped the modern Easter basket.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterHistory, Folklore & Spiritual Origins

Why eggs became an Easter symbol

Eggs have long carried associations with life and renewal, making them a natural seasonal symbol. In Christian Europe, eggs also fit the calendar practically: fasting traditions could restrict certain foods during Lent, making decorated or gifted eggs especially meaningful when the fast ended.

Different regions developed their own dyeing, scratching, waxing and painting techniques, so there is no single origin for all Easter eggs.

From hares to a gift-bringing bunny

German-speaking traditions helped popularize stories of an Easter hare—often called the Osterhase—who judged children and delivered colored eggs. Immigrants carried versions of these customs to North America, where the hare gradually became the softer, child-friendly Easter Bunny.

Nests evolved into baskets, and eggs were joined by candy, toys and small spring gifts.

Why the custom endured

The tradition is adaptable. Families can make the bunny a playful character, a scavenger-hunt host, a decorative motif or skip it entirely. The egg itself can remain religious, artistic, culinary or simply seasonal depending on the household.

Build a tradition with context

If you celebrate with children, pair the fun with a short explanation: some Easter customs come from church practice, some from folklore, and some from modern commercial culture. That gives children a richer sense of how traditions evolve.

Frequently asked questions

Did the Easter Bunny begin in Germany?

Gift-bringing hare traditions are strongly associated with German-speaking Europe and later spread through migration and popular culture.

Why are eggs decorated at Easter?

Egg decorating reflects a mix of seasonal symbolism, Christian fasting customs and regional folk art traditions.

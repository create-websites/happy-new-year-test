Easter Ham vs. Lamb: Choosing the Holiday Centerpiece

Compare two classic Easter centerpieces by flavor, symbolism, cooking style, guest preference and leftovers.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCulinary & Baking Trends

Why families choose ham

Ham is popular in many North American Easter meals because it feeds a crowd, can be prepared ahead and pairs easily with sweet or tangy glazes. A bone-in ham also creates useful leftovers for sandwiches, soups and breakfast dishes.

Why families choose lamb

Lamb has deep symbolic and culinary associations with Passover and Easter in many cultures. Roasted leg of lamb or shoulder works well with garlic, rosemary, lemon and herbs, while smaller households may prefer chops.

Choose based on your table, not a rule

Think about guest preferences, oven space, budget and the side dishes you want to serve. A mixed table can even offer a smaller roast alongside a vegetarian centerpiece rather than choosing one large meat.

Plan leftovers before you cook

Ham can become biscuits, strata or bean soup; lamb can become sandwiches, grain bowls or shepherd’s-pie-style bakes. Cooling and refrigerating leftovers promptly is more important than squeezing one more meal out of them.

Frequently asked questions

Is lamb more traditional than ham for Easter?

Lamb has older religious symbolism, while ham became strongly associated with Easter in many North American households. Both are established traditions.

Which is easier for a large group?

Ham is often simpler because it can be heated and glazed with less last-minute cooking.

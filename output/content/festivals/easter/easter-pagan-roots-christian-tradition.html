Easter Origins: Spring Rites and Christian Tradition

Explore what historians can and cannot say about spring customs, the name Easter, and the Christian celebration of the resurrection.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterHistory, Folklore & Spiritual Origins

Start with the central Christian meaning

For Christians, Easter celebrates the resurrection of Jesus after his crucifixion, the culmination of Holy Week and the heart of the Paschal season. The feast developed within the early Christian calendar in close relationship to Passover and was known in many languages by names derived from Pascha.

That religious core is older and more specific than many modern claims that Easter is simply a renamed spring fertility festival.

Where spring customs enter the story

Easter is celebrated in the Northern Hemisphere during spring, so seasonal imagery—new growth, light, flowers, eggs and young animals—naturally became attached to the holiday in many places. Some customs may preserve older regional habits, but not every bunny, egg or flower can be traced to a single pre-Christian ritual.

The English word Easter is unusual because many other languages use forms related to Pascha. Scholars have debated its linguistic history, but popular internet claims often go further than the evidence.

A better way to understand the blend

Think of Easter as a layered holiday. Theology, liturgy, local folklore, family foodways and springtime symbolism accumulated over centuries. A church sunrise service and a neighborhood egg hunt can coexist without having the same historical origin.

That layered view is more accurate—and more interesting—than trying to force every custom into one origin story.

How to talk about the history respectfully

When discussing Easter with children or mixed-faith groups, distinguish belief from historical reconstruction. Christians may describe resurrection as a matter of faith; historians can describe how the feast, calendar and customs developed. Keeping those categories clear prevents both religious meaning and historical evidence from being flattened.

Frequently asked questions

Is Easter simply a pagan festival renamed by Christians?

No. Easter is a Christian resurrection feast with ancient roots in the Paschal calendar, while some later seasonal customs may reflect broader European spring traditions.

Why is Easter associated with spring?

Its movable date falls in spring in the Northern Hemisphere, and many local customs adopted seasonal imagery such as flowers, eggs and new life.

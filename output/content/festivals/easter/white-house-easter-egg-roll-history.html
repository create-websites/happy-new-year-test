White House Easter Egg Roll: History and Traditions

Follow the South Lawn tradition starting in 1878 with music, egg races, souvenir eggs, and contemporary family activities.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterFamily, Community & Modern Traditions

Why the White House lawn became the setting

Egg rolling had become a beloved Easter tradition in Washington when President Rutherford B. Hayes decided to allow children to use the White House grounds for the annual event in 1878, following restrictions on the U.S. Capitol grounds by Congress.

A tradition that kept evolving

Throughout the nineteenth century, the U.S. Marine Band joined the festivities, and egg-roll races became a popular activity over time, alongside music, games, and public appearances.

Souvenir eggs and family programming

In 1981, the Reagan White House launched a search for wooden eggs autographed by famous individuals. Decorated wooden eggs became cherished mementos, with contemporary initiatives including storytelling, live performances, and interactive stations.

Why the event matters beyond politics

The Egg Roll, a civic family tradition held at a political landmark, has remained popular for generations due to children coming together on the South Lawn for spring games, despite disruptions from war, renovations, weather, and public-health crises.

Frequently asked questions

When was the first annual White House Easter Egg Roll?

The inaugural White House Easter Egg Roll was held on April 22, 1878, as confirmed by The White House Historical Association.

Who opened the White House grounds to egg rollers?

In 1878, President Rutherford B. Hayes allowed children to visit the grounds.

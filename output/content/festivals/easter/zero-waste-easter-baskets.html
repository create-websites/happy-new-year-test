Zero-Waste Easter Baskets: Practical Eco-Friendly Ideas

Create Easter baskets using reusable containers, practical presents, and compostable filler instead of disposable plastic.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCrafts, Decor & DIY

Start with the container

Utilize a basket, storage bin, tote, garden pot, or fabric bag that you already have on hand, giving it a second life after Easter. Reusing the container each year makes a meaningful impact as it is typically the largest and most durable part of the gift.

Replace plastic grass

Shredded recycled paper, a scarf, cloth napkins, or crinkle-cut kraft paper can be used as eco-friendly alternatives to plastic packaging materials for cushioning gifts. Additionally, using fabric can make unpacking gifts easier, especially around pets and toddlers.

Choose gifts that get used

Instead of novelty plastic, consider purchasing books, art supplies, seed packets, sidewalk chalk, reusable water bottles, gardening tools, and experience coupons. When choosing treats, opt for favorites instead of a large assortment that may not be fully enjoyed.

Reuse the decor next year

Keep ribbons, wooden eggs, fabric bunting, and sturdy baskets stored together post-holiday in a labeled Easter box to help curb the urge to purchase new decorations every spring.

Frequently asked questions

What can replace plastic Easter grass?

Shredded paper, fabric scraps, cloth napkins, or reusable scarves are all effective options.

Can a zero-waste basket still include candy?

Yes. The aim is to reduce waste, not strive for perfection: select treats that people will truly appreciate and cut down on unnecessary packaging when possible.

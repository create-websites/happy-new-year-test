Authentic Ukadiche Modak Step-by-Step

Ukadiche modak are steamed dumplings with a tender rice-flour shell and a fragrant coconut-jaggery center. The recipe is simple in ingredients but rewards patient technique: cook the dough well, keep it warm, prepare a filling that is moist but not runny, and shape the pleats before the shell dries.

Key takeaways

  • Cook the rice flour in hot water rather than mixing it with cold water; the cooked dough is what gives the shell flexibility.
  • Cool the jaggery-coconut filling before stuffing so steam and excess moisture do not tear the shell.
  • Keep the dough covered while shaping and steam only until the shell looks glossy and cooked.
1

Ingredients for about 10–12 modaks

For the shellFor the filling
1 cup fine rice flour
1 to 1¼ cups water as needed
1 tsp ghee
Pinch of salt		1 cup freshly grated coconut
¾ cup grated jaggery, adjusted to taste
½ tsp cardamom powder
Optional pinch of nutmeg
1 tsp ghee
2

Step 1: prepare the filling

Warm a pan on low heat and add a teaspoon of ghee. Add coconut and jaggery. Stir gently as the jaggery melts. Cook only until the mixture comes together and excess liquid has mostly evaporated. Overcooking can make the filling hard after cooling.

Turn off the heat, add cardamom and optional nutmeg, then spread the mixture on a plate to cool completely.

3

Step 2: make the ukad

Bring about one cup of water to a boil with ghee and salt. Lower the heat and add the rice flour in one go. Stir quickly until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest for a few minutes so the flour hydrates.

When cool enough to handle but still warm, knead the dough thoroughly. Wet or grease your palms lightly. The goal is a smooth dough without cracks. Add tiny splashes of hot water if it feels dry.

4

Step 3: shape the pleats

Take a small lemon-sized ball of dough and keep the rest covered. Flatten it into a cup with thin edges and a slightly thicker base. Rotate the cup while pinching 8 to 12 narrow pleats around the rim.

Add a spoonful of cooled filling. Bring the pleats together and pinch the top to seal. If using a mold, grease it lightly and press a thin layer of dough against the sides before filling and sealing.

5

Step 4: steam gently

Line a steamer plate with a banana leaf or lightly grease it. Arrange the modaks with space between them. Steam over medium heat for roughly 10 to 12 minutes, or until the shells look slightly glossy and set. Cooking time varies with size and steamer.

Rest for a minute before moving them. Serve warm, traditionally with a small spoon of ghee.

6

Troubleshooting

  • Cracks while shaping: the dough is too dry or too cool. Knead with a few drops of hot water.
  • Shell breaks during steaming: it may be too thin, overfilled, or poorly sealed.
  • Filling leaks: cook the jaggery-coconut mixture slightly longer next time and cool it fully.
  • Hard shell: avoid excessive steaming and use finely milled rice flour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Practical answers for readers exploring this Ganesh Chaturthi topic.

Can I make the filling ahead?

Yes. Refrigerate it in a covered container and bring it close to room temperature before shaping.

Can I use desiccated coconut?

Fresh coconut gives the most traditional texture, but hydrated desiccated coconut can work in a pinch.

Why does my dough crack?

Usually because it is too dry, undercooked, or has cooled. Knead it warm with tiny amounts of hot water.

How to Prepare and Hoist a Gudi at Home

A handy, family-oriented manual for putting together, adorning, and securely showcasing a classic Gudi in your home for Gudi Padwa.

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

The festival is made unforgettable by actively participating in traditions such as raising the Gudi, listening to the panchang, sharing in ritual foods, creating rangoli, and including children. These instructions emphasize both significance and practicality.

What you need

Get a bamboo or sturdy pole, a colorful cloth, neem or mango leaves, a flower garland, a sugar garland if desired, and a light metal vessel to place at the top. Don't forget strong cotton string or secure tie.

Select items that reflect your family traditions. Purchasing extravagant materials is unnecessary when simpler, reusable ones already hold sentimental value in your household.

Step 1: prepare the space

Prepare the entrance, balcony, terrace, or window area for displaying the Gudi. It is common for families to create a rangoli and place a small puja thali with kumkum, turmeric, flowers, rice, and a lamp nearby.

Before assembling the Gudi on a balcony, carefully choose the exact location for tying it. It is important to prioritize safety by planning ahead, rather than improvising once the structure is already built.

Step 2: dress the pole

Hang the bright cloth near the top of the bamboo like a banner, securing it tightly and neatly. Adorn the upper section with neem or mango leaves, as well as a flower garland.

When using a sugar garland, position it to be visible without adding too much weight to the top.

Step 3: crown the Gudi

Position the metal vessel upside down on top of the pole, ensuring it is securely in place and cannot slip off. Opt for a lighter vessel over a heavier one, especially when on higher levels or in breezy weather.

When finished, the Gudi should appear tall and festive while still being sturdy enough to be lifted without shaking.

Step 4: raise, secure and worship

Erect the Gudi near the entrance or in a prominent location, securing it tightly to a railing or stable support. It is customary for the Gudi to be displayed at a height where it can be easily seen.

Families may present flowers, kumkum, akshata, incense, or a lamp before conducting a brief prayer or aarti as per their household customs. It is important to always keep open flames away from flammable materials, such as cloth, leaves, and windy balconies.

A simple child-friendly version

Kids can create a mini Gudi for their tabletop with a small wooden stick, reusable cloth, paper or fabric flowers, and a light cup. This is perfect for apartments, classrooms, and diaspora gatherings where a full-size bamboo Gudi isn't feasible.

The importance of the teaching opportunity outweighs the size: involve the child in understanding and organizing each component.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Where should a Gudi be placed?

Usually, it is prominently placed near the entrance, window, balcony, or terrace, based on the family's custom and home layout.

Does the vessel have to be copper?

Numerous families opt for a copper or metal vessel, however, preferences differ. Choose a secure, lightweight alternative that aligns with your customs.

Can apartment residents make a small Gudi?

Indeed, a compact or tabletop Gudi can maintain its symbolic significance in situations where space or building regulations restrict a full-height display.

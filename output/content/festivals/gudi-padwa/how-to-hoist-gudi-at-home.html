How to Prepare and Hoist a Gudi at Home

A practical, family-friendly guide to assembling, decorating and safely displaying a traditional Gudi at home for Gudi Padwa.

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

The festival becomes memorable through action: raising the Gudi, listening to the panchang, sharing a ritual taste, making rangoli and involving children. These guides focus on meaning as well as practical steps.

What you need

Prepare a clean bamboo or sturdy pole, a festive cloth, neem or mango leaves, a flower garland, a sugar garland if your family uses one, and a lightweight metal vessel to place upside down at the top. You will also need strong cotton string or another secure tie.

Choose items that match your family custom. There is no benefit in buying elaborate materials if simpler reusable ones already carry meaning in your home.

Step 1: prepare the space

Clean the entrance, balcony, terrace or window area where the Gudi will be displayed. Many families make a rangoli nearby and place a small puja thali with kumkum, turmeric, flowers, rice and a lamp.

If the Gudi is going on a balcony, decide exactly where it will be tied before assembling it. Safety should be planned, not improvised after the structure is complete.

Step 2: dress the pole

Near the upper end of the bamboo, drape the bright cloth so that it hangs like a banner. Tie it firmly but neatly. Add neem or mango leaves and the flower garland around the same upper section.

If using a sugar garland, place it so that it is visible without making the top excessively heavy.

Step 3: crown the Gudi

Place the metal vessel upside down over the top of the pole. Check that it sits securely and cannot slide off. A light vessel is safer than a heavy one, especially on upper floors or in windy conditions.

The completed Gudi should look tall and celebratory but remain stable enough to be raised without wobbling.

Step 4: raise, secure and worship

Raise the Gudi upright near the entrance or another prominent place and tie it firmly to a railing or stable support. Traditionally, the Gudi is displayed high enough to be visible.

Families may offer flowers, kumkum, akshata, incense or a lamp and then perform a short prayer or aarti according to household tradition. Keep open flames away from cloth, leaves and windy balconies.

A simple child-friendly version

Children can make a tabletop Gudi using a short wooden stick, reusable cloth, paper or fabric flowers and a lightweight cup. This is useful for apartments, classrooms and diaspora celebrations where a full-height bamboo structure may not be practical.

The teaching opportunity matters more than scale: explain what each element means and let the child participate in arranging it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Where should a Gudi be placed?

Traditionally it is displayed prominently near the entrance, window, balcony or terrace, depending on the home and family custom.

Does the vessel have to be copper?

Many families use a copper or metal vessel, but practices vary. Use a secure, lightweight option that fits your tradition.

Can apartment residents make a small Gudi?

Yes. A compact or tabletop Gudi can preserve the symbolism when space or building rules limit a full-height display.

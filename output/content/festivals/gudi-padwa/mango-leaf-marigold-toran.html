How to Make a Mango Leaf and Marigold Toran

Make a new, classic toran for Gudi Padwa using mango leaves and marigold flowers, along with helpful advice on how to tie and maintain it.

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

Clothing and home decorations transform Gudi Padwa into a vibrant celebration. These tips emphasize easy-to-achieve styling and festive decor that is effortlessly executed.

Why the doorway matters

Gudi Padwa starts at home with cleaning, decorating, rangoli, and raising the Gudi, while a toran at the entrance signals the auspicious occasion.

Mango leaves and marigolds are favored for their unmistakably festive shapes and colors, complementing the Gudi perfectly.

Materials

Collect new mango leaves, marigold blossoms, sturdy cotton thread or twine, a needle appropriate for making garlands, and non-damaging hooks or ties for the doorway.

In the absence of fresh mango leaves, opt for locally sourced leaves that are safe, strong, and culturally suitable instead of using plastic alternatives.

Prepare the leaves and flowers

Gently wipe leaves, removing any damaged ones, and sort by size to create a balanced toran. Select firm marigold blooms that will remain intact for hours.

Store materials in a cool, shaded area until assembly to maintain freshness.

Two easy assembly styles

  • Alternating style: Connect groups of leaves together with a marigold placed between each cluster.
  • Garland style: Start by creating a marigold garland and then add mango leaves evenly spaced along the top.

No matter which method you select, make sure to leave sufficient thread at each end for safe hanging.

Hang it safely

Secure the toran with sturdy hooks or ties at a height that prevents tall guests from brushing against it, and refrain from placing it near open flames from lamps or candles.

To keep the entrance clean and safe, sweep up any fallen petals from the flowers regularly to prevent slipping.

A reusable modern version

In apartments or areas with limited access to fresh materials, create a fabric toran using cloth leaves and hand-stitched marigold-colored fabric flowers. Remember to store it properly for future use.

A family's handmade, reusable toran can hold memories and traditions while maintaining its traditional color and design.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

How long will a fresh toran last?

The freshness of leaves and flowers is greatly influenced by the heat and humidity levels, with proximity to the festival site playing a key role in prolonging their vibrancy.

Can I make a toran without mango leaves?

If you can't find mango leaves, opt for a mindful local substitute or a reusable cloth option instead of using unsafe or inappropriate plants.

Where should the toran be placed?

Traditionally, it is hung over the main doorway or another notable entrance to the house.

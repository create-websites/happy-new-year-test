The Role of Panchang Shravan on Gudi Padwa

Why many families begin the New Year by listening to the panchang—and what tithi, nakshatra, yoga and yearly forecasts mean in practice.

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

The festival becomes memorable through action: raising the Gudi, listening to the panchang, sharing a ritual taste, making rangoli and involving children. These guides focus on meaning as well as practical steps.

Beginning the year by listening to time

Panchang Shravan literally centers on hearing or reviewing the panchang, the traditional Hindu almanac. On Gudi Padwa, this practice gives the New Year a calendrical and reflective beginning: families listen to important features of the coming year and note auspicious dates or religious observances.

Historically, the panchang served practical as well as ritual needs. It helped organize festivals, fasts, ceremonies and seasonal time.

The five limbs of a panchang

The word panchang refers to five calendrical elements: tithi, vara, nakshatra, yoga and karana. A full almanac includes much more information, but these five form the conceptual core.

You do not need to master technical astrology to appreciate the tradition. Learning what a tithi is and why a lunar day differs from a civil date is already a meaningful step.

What is heard on Gudi Padwa

Depending on region and tradition, a priest, elder, astrologer or knowledgeable family member may read the year name, important planetary or calendrical information, festival dates, seasonal notes and general forecasts.

The emphasis can vary from devotional listening to detailed astrological interpretation. Families should follow the level of practice that is meaningful to them.

A modern household version

If no one at home can read a traditional panchang, use a reputable regional almanac and focus on practical information: the New Year tithi, major festivals, ekadashi dates if relevant, family observances, and temple events.

Create a shared family calendar after the reading. That converts Panchang Shravan from a passive ritual into useful yearly planning.

Reflection matters as much as prediction

For many people, the deeper value of the practice is not forecasting every event. It is the act of pausing at the threshold of a year and recognizing that time has structure, seasons and sacred markers.

Pair the panchang with a short family reflection: What should we continue? What should we leave behind? Which festivals or family gatherings do we want to protect time for this year?

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

What is Panchang Shravan?

It is the traditional practice of hearing or reviewing the new almanac and important calendrical information at the start of the year.

Do I need to understand astrology to participate?

No. You can focus on the calendar, tithis, festivals and family observances without making detailed astrological interpretations.

What are the five parts of a panchang?

Traditionally they are tithi, vara, nakshatra, yoga and karana.

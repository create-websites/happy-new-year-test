Mastering Puran Poli for Gudi Padwa

Master the essential techniques behind soft, thin puran poli—from cooking the chana dal filling to rolling without tearing.

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

Maharashtrian New Year food balances ritual, hospitality and regional taste. These guides move from iconic puran poli to a complete meal, lesser-known dishes and gifts that travel well.

The two-part challenge

Puran poli looks simple—a sweet filling inside flatbread dough—but success depends on getting two textures right at the same time. The filling must be smooth, cohesive and not watery; the outer dough must be soft and elastic enough to stretch around it.

If either part is too dry, the poli cracks. If either is too wet, it becomes hard to roll.

Cook the dal until tender, not mushy

Chana dal should cook fully while still being drainable. Excess water is the enemy of a smooth filling, so drain well before adding jaggery according to your recipe.

Once jaggery melts, the mixture loosens again. Continue cooking patiently until it thickens enough to hold shape. Stir frequently to prevent scorching.

Make the puran silky

Traditionally, the cooked filling may be passed through a puran yantra or similar tool for an even texture. A suitable kitchen method that produces a smooth, lump-free filling can also work.

Flavor with cardamom and, if your family likes it, a small amount of nutmeg. Let the filling cool before stuffing the dough.

Use a soft, rested dough

The dough should be softer than a typical roti dough. Some families use wheat flour, some combine flours, and regional methods vary. Resting allows hydration to even out and makes stretching easier.

Oil or ghee may be used according to the recipe. The goal is elasticity without stickiness.

Stuff generously, roll gently

A good puran poli usually contains a generous amount of filling. Flatten the dough, place a smooth ball of puran inside, seal carefully, then roll with light pressure. Rotate frequently rather than forcing the pin across one direction.

Use only as much dusting flour as needed; excess flour can dry the surface.

Cook until spotted and fragrant

Cook on a properly heated tawa, turning when the surface develops bubbles and light brown spots. Avoid overcooking, which makes the poli leathery. Serve warm.

The first one or two may not be perfect. Treat them as calibration: adjust heat, filling consistency or dough softness before making the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Why does puran poli tear while rolling?

Common causes are dry dough, lumpy filling, filling that is too wet, or heavy rolling pressure.

Can puran be made ahead?

Yes. The filling can often be prepared ahead, cooled and refrigerated, then brought to a workable temperature before use.

Why is my puran poli hard?

Overcooking, a stiff dough or too much dusting flour can make it hard rather than soft.

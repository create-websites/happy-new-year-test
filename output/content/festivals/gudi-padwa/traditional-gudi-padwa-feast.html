The Ultimate Gudi Padwa Feast

Organize a traditional Maharashtrian New Year feast featuring puran poli, shrikhand, batata bhaji, rice, dal, veggies, papad, chutneys, and seasonal flavors

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

Traditional Maharashtrian New Year cuisine offers a harmonious blend of ritual, hospitality, and local flavors. From the classic puran poli to a wide array of lesser-known dishes and portable gifts, these culinary traditions span a complete meal that delights the senses.

Build the meal around balance

A Gudi Padwa celebration can be elaborate without being overwhelming. Simply select a main sweet, a savory dish, rice or bread, a dal or amti, a vegetable, accompaniments, and a refreshing dessert or dairy dish.

This provides diversity in color, texture, and flavor without the need for an extensive restaurant menu.

Puran poli: the festive centerpiece

Puran poli is a popular dish in Maharashtrian festive cuisine, consisting of a soft flatbread filled with a sweet mixture of cooked chana dal and jaggery, flavored with cardamom and occasionally nutmeg.

Enjoy it hot with ghee if it's part of your household's cooking. This recipe benefits from being prepared in advance, as the filling can be made ahead and chilled before putting it all together.

Shrikhand and cooling sweetness

Shrikhand provides a refreshing, creamy complement to a warm dish. This rich strained yogurt is sweetened, flavored with cardamom and saffron, and topped with nuts.

If you're serving puran poli, go easy on the shrikhand. A festive thali should feel abundant, not overwhelming.

Batata bhaji and the savory plate

A basic Maharashtrian potato dish is a good choice as it is comforting, familiar, and goes well with puri or poli. Serve with dal, amti or usal, a seasonal vegetable, rice, papad, pickle, and koshimbir based on your family's taste preferences.

The savory dishes in the thali enhance the festive feel of the sweet dishes without being overpowering.

A practical cooking timeline

The day before the festival: prepare chana dal for puran, strain yogurt for shrikhand, make dry snacks, and get garnishes ready. On the morning of the festival: complete the puran, knead dough, cook vegetables, make rice and dal, and prepare puran poli just before serving.

Ensure that at least one dish is kept intentionally simple so that the host can still participate in the puja and family celebration.

A sample menu

  • Neem-jaggery ritual taste
  • Puran poli with ghee
  • Batata bhaji
  • Varan or amti with rice
  • Seasonal vegetable or usal
  • Koshimbir, papad and pickle
  • Shrikhand or fruit shrikhand
Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

What is the most iconic Gudi Padwa dish?

Puran poli is a popular Maharashtrian dish often enjoyed during festive occasions.

Can I prepare the feast partly in advance?

Indeed, preparing puran filling, shrikhand, snacks, and completing some tasks in advance can help alleviate stress on the day of the festival.

Do I need to make every traditional dish?

A concise menu featuring select family favorites is perfectly suitable.

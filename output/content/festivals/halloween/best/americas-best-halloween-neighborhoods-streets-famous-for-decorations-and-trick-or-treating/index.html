America's Best Halloween Neighborhoods: Streets Famous for Decorations and Trick-or-Treating

Every city has a few streets where Halloween goes into overdrive : full-size candy bars, theatrical porches and thousands of costumed visitors. Here's what makes them special and how to find them.

By Prashant Dhingra · October 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Great Halloween streets are walkable, dense, well-lit and have neighbors who coordinate.
  • Famous examples: West 69th Street (Manhattan), Park Slope (Brooklyn), New Orleans' Garden District and Salem's historic district.
  • Find local gems via news lists, neighborhood groups and community candy maps.
  • Visiting? Arrive early, park legally, bring your own light and say thank you.

On Halloween night, some streets stand quiet with a few porch lights glowing. Others become destinations : packed sidewalks, theatrical decorations, neighbors in costume handing out candy from elaborate stations, and families who've driven in from across town. These neighborhoods have turned Halloween into a shared community performance.

Famous Halloween streets and neighborhoods

West 69th Street, Manhattan

On the Upper West Side, the block of West 69th Street between Central Park West and Columbus Avenue has a long-standing reputation as one of New York City's great Halloween blocks. Brownstone stoops become stages, and the block has typically been closed to traffic for the evening as thousands of children parade through.

Park Slope, Brooklyn

Park Slope's tree-lined streets of brownstones are famous for elaborate stoop decorations, and the neighborhood's long-running Halloween parade fills Seventh Avenue with costumed children and families. It's a model of a whole neighborhood celebrating together.

New Orleans: Garden District and Uptown

Grand historic mansions, oak-lined streets and a city that loves costumes make Uptown and the Garden District one of the most atmospheric trick-or-treat experiences in the country. See New Orleans at Halloween.

Salem, Massachusetts

Beyond the downtown crowds, Salem's McIntire Historic District, including Chestnut Street, offers Federal-style mansions dressed for the Witch City's biggest night. More in Halloween in Salem, Massachusetts.

Savannah's historic district

Moss-draped squares and historic homes make Savannah a beautiful place to trick-or-treat; many residents decorate elaborately. See Savannah, Georgia at Halloween.

The Pumpkin House, Kenova, West Virginia

A single home that became a regional legend: each October thousands of carved jack-o'-lanterns cover the house, fences and yard of the Pumpkin House in Kenova, drawing visitors from across the tri-state area.

Anoka, Minnesota

The self-styled Halloween Capital of the World pairs parades and community events with classic Midwestern trick-or-treating. See Halloween in the Midwest.

Your city's hidden gems

Almost every metro area has at least one "Halloween street" : often an older neighborhood with sidewalks and a few superfan households that set the tone. Local news outlets publish annual lists, and many neighbors now share interactive Halloween maps.

How to find great streets near you

  1. Search local news for "best trick-or-treat neighborhoods" in your city.
  2. Check neighborhood social-media groups and community apps for maps and street closures.
  3. Look for crowd-sourced decoration maps, which grow every year.
  4. Ask teachers and librarians : they know where families go.
  5. Drive by in daylight the week before to spot decorated blocks.

Visiting a destination neighborhood: etiquette

  • Arrive early : the best streets are packed by 6:30 p.m.
  • Park legally and well away from the busiest blocks; walk in.
  • Stay on sidewalks and keep moving so others can reach the door.
  • Take one or two treats : residents are buying candy for hundreds or thousands of children.
  • Skip houses with lights off.
  • Say thank you : and compliment the decorations.
  • Take your trash with you.
  • Bring your own light : flashlights and reflective gear.

For residents of destination streets

If you live on a famous Halloween block, coordination makes the night safe and fun:

  • Buy candy in bulk : far more than you think. Some residents budget for 1,000+ visitors.
  • Coordinate with neighbors on themes, timing and a "lights out" signal when supplies run out.
  • Request a street closure or traffic help from your city if crowds are large.
  • Use a candy station at the sidewalk to keep porches clear.
  • Light the path brightly and keep it clear of cords and props.

Start a tradition on your street

Every legendary Halloween block started with a few enthusiastic neighbors. Talk to the people next door, agree on a start time, share a theme, maybe close the street : and see how quickly the word spreads. Questions & answers

What makes a good trick-or-treat neighborhood?

Good trick-or-treat neighborhoods have sidewalks, houses close together, good street lighting, low traffic, many participating households and a community tradition of decorating. Some cities also close streets to cars on Halloween evening.

Is it OK to trick-or-treat in a different neighborhood?

Many families visit famous Halloween streets, and most residents expect it. Be respectful: park legally, stay on sidewalks, keep groups moving, take only one or two treats, and avoid houses with lights off.

How do I find the best Halloween houses near me?

Search local news sites for annual 'best trick-or-treat neighborhoods' lists, check neighborhood social media groups and community apps, look for crowd-sourced Halloween decoration maps, and ask at local schools and libraries.

What is the Pumpkin House in Kenova?

The Pumpkin House in Kenova, West Virginia, is a famous Halloween display where thousands of carved jack-o'-lanterns cover a family home and its grounds, drawing visitors from across the region each October.

Neighborhood traditions are run by residents, not official organizers, and can change from year to year. Check local news and community groups for the current year's details and any street closures.

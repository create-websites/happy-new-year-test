DIY Halloween Costumes: Creative Last-Minute Looks Using Things You Already Own

It's 5 p.m. on Halloween and you have nothing to wear. Don't panic : your closet, your recycling bin and a roll of tape can do a lot in an hour.

By Prashant Dhingra · October 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Every great DIY costume has one signature element : ears, a hat, a prop : that makes it readable instantly.
  • Start from a base outfit (all black, all white, sweats, formal wear) and add one or two pieces.
  • Cardboard, felt, safety pins and tape solve almost every construction problem.
  • Makeup finishes a simple costume and costs little : see the makeup guide.

Store-bought costumes are convenient, but homemade ones are often more fun, more original and much cheaper. The trick is to think like a costume designer: start with a simple base outfit and add one signature element that tells everyone instantly who you are.

The base-outfit method

Most last-minute costumes start from one of five bases you probably already own:

Base outfit Easy costumes
All black Cat, bat, witch, ninja, shadow, burglar, mime (with stripes)
All white Ghost, angel, chef, scientist, mummy (with gauze)
Sweatsuit or hoodie Animal, crayon, athlete, superhero, astronaut
Formal wear Vampire, secret agent, ghost bride/groom, magician
Denim and flannel Lumberjack, farmer, scarecrow, cowboy

Then add the signature piece : ears, a cape, a hat, a prop or makeup.

30 ideas from what you already have

From a sheet or fabric

  1. Classic ghost : white sheet, eyeholes, done. Add sunglasses for "cool ghost."
  2. Toga : a bedsheet draped and pinned at one shoulder, plus a leaf crown.
  3. Mummy : white clothes wrapped in strips of old sheet or gauze.
  4. Superhero cape : a tablecloth or pillowcase, with a felt emblem.
  5. Wizard : a bathrobe, a paper cone hat with stars and a stick wand.

From black clothes

  1. Black cat : headband ears, eyeliner whiskers and a tail made from a black sock stuffed with tissue.
  2. Bat : black umbrella fabric or an old cape spread into wings.
  3. Witch : black dress or clothes, a broom and a paper cone hat.
  4. Burglar : black clothes, striped shirt, a beanie and a bag marked "$".
  5. Shadow : all black with a white chalk outline traced around you.

From a hoodie

  1. Bear, fox or wolf : felt ears and a felt belly panel.
  2. Dinosaur : felt spikes glued down the hood and back.
  3. Monster : fuzzy fabric, googly eyes and felt teeth.
  4. Astronaut : white or gray hoodie, foil-tape details and a cardboard control panel.

From cardboard

  1. Robot : a box body painted silver, bottle caps for dials.
  2. Crayon : cardboard cone hat on a single-color outfit with a printed label.
  3. Toy building brick : a painted box with paper-cup studs on top.
  4. Dice : two boxes with dots for a couple.
  5. Vending machine or TV : a box with a cut window framing your face.

Puns and wordplay

  1. Smart cookie : glasses plus cookies pinned to a shirt.
  2. Cereal killer : cereal boxes stabbed with plastic knives.
  3. Social butterfly : cardboard wings covered in hand-drawn app icons.
  4. Hot mess : red/orange clothes covered in random items.
  5. Couch potato : a brown sack, a remote and a couch cushion.

From formal wear

  1. Vampire : black suit or dress, a cape and slicked-back hair.
  2. Zombie prom date : old formal clothes, torn and dusted with makeup.
  3. Magician : a black outfit, a paper top hat and a stuffed rabbit.
  4. Secret agent : a suit, sunglasses and a coiled earpiece wire.

From everyday occupations

  1. Chef : white shirt, apron, paper hat and a wooden spoon.
  2. Tourist : Hawaiian shirt, camera, fanny pack, sunscreen on the nose and a map.

Construction cheat sheet

Problem Quick solution
Attach fabric without sewing Safety pins, fabric glue, iron-on hemming tape
Make ears Felt or card glued to a headband
Make a hat Paper or card rolled into a cone; craft foam for structure
Make armor or wings Cardboard or craft foam, painted
Make a tail Stuffed sock or knee-high, pinned on
Add sparkle Glitter glue, sequins, metallic paint
Make it glow Glow sticks or battery fairy lights tucked into seams

Finish with makeup and hair

A simple costume becomes convincing with the right face: whiskers for a cat, pale skin and dark eyes for a vampire, stitches for a monster, smudged gray for a zombie. Halloween Makeup Ideas has step-by-step looks for every skill level. Slick, tease or spray-color your hair to match.

DIY costumes for families and groups

DIY shines for groups because you can coordinate cheaply:

  • Box of crayons : each person in a different colored sweatshirt with a paper label.
  • Farm : overalls, flannel, a scarecrow, and a toddler as a pumpkin in an orange shirt.
  • Pack of animals : matching hoodies with different felt ears.
  • Ghost family : sheets in graduated sizes.

For more ideas, see Best Halloween Costume Ideas.

Comfort and safety

DIY costumes can be the safest of all because you control the design:

  • Make sure eyeholes are big enough for full vision, or skip masks in favor of makeup.
  • Hem sheets and capes so nobody trips.
  • Add reflective tape to dark costumes for trick-or-treating.
  • Choose layers for cold or wet weather.

Halloween Costume Safety covers visibility, masks and makeup in more detail.

Be sustainable

Homemade costumes reuse what you have, and the materials go back into the closet or recycling afterward. Organize a costume swap with friends or neighbors for next year : Eco-Friendly Halloween explains how.

Questions & answers

What is the easiest homemade Halloween costume?

A classic sheet ghost, a black cat (black clothes, ears and eyeliner whiskers), a vampire (dark clothes, a cape and slicked hair) and a 'tourist' (Hawaiian shirt, camera, sunscreen on the nose) are among the easiest.

How can I make a costume without sewing?

Use safety pins, fabric glue, hot glue, iron-on hemming tape, double-sided tape and felt, which doesn't fray. Cardboard and craft foam can be cut and taped into hats, armor and props.

What can I use to make animal ears?

Cut ear shapes from felt or card, glue or tape them to a plain headband, or fold paper into ears and pin them to a hood. A hoodie with felt ears sewn or glued on is one of the most versatile bases.

How do I make a costume look less homemade?

Commit to one strong silhouette, keep the color palette consistent, add one well-made prop, and complete the look with makeup and hair. Confidence sells any costume.

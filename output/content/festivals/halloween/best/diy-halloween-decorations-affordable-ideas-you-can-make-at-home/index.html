DIY Halloween Decorations: Affordable Ideas You Can Make at Home

The best Halloween decorations are often the ones made at the kitchen table from cardboard, paper, jars and thrift-store finds. Here are projects for every budget and skill.

Key takeaways

  • Black card stock, tape and a craft knife make window silhouettes and paper bats : the cheapest high-impact décor there is.
  • Recycled jars, bottles and milk jugs become lanterns, potions and glowing ghosts.
  • A can of matte black spray paint turns thrift-store candlesticks, frames and plastic toys into gothic décor.
  • Use LED lights in all paper and fabric projects for safety.

Halloween decorations don't need to come from a big-box store. Some of the most memorable porches and parties are built from cardboard, paint and imagination : and they cost a fraction of the price. These projects use materials you probably already have, plus a few dollars of craft supplies.

The DIY toolkit

Stock up once and you can make almost everything below:

  • Black card stock and white printer paper
  • Craft knife, scissors and a cutting mat
  • Painter's tape and double-sided tape
  • Hot glue gun
  • Matte black, white and metallic spray paint
  • Battery tea lights and LED fairy lights
  • Cheesecloth or cheap gauze
  • Twine, wire and zip ties
  • Recycled glass jars, bottles and plastic milk jugs

1. Window silhouettes

Nothing beats silhouettes for impact per dollar. Print or draw shapes : witches on broomsticks, black cats, bats, a crooked tree, a haunted house skyline : on black card stock and cut them out. Tape them inside your windows. At night, with lights on indoors, they read crisply from the street.

Tip: Add a sheet of parchment or tissue paper behind them for a glowing amber backdrop.

2. A swarm of paper bats

Cut 30–50 bats in two or three sizes from black card stock. Fold each down the middle so the wings lift, then tape them in a flowing path : up a staircase wall, around a mirror or bursting out of a fireplace. Arrange them from small to large to suggest movement.

3. Milk-jug ghosts

Rinse plastic milk jugs and draw ghost faces on them with a black marker. Cut a small hole in the back and insert a string of LED lights. Line them along a path or porch step for a glowing ghost parade. This is a perfect kids' project.

4. Floating cheesecloth ghosts

Inflate a balloon and set it on top of a tall bottle. Drape cheesecloth over it and soak it with fabric stiffener (or a 50/50 mix of white glue and water). Let it dry overnight, pop the balloon, and you have a free-standing ghost. Hang several from a porch ceiling with fishing line for a floating effect. Keep these under cover; rain will soften them.

5. Potion bottles

Collect old glass bottles of different shapes. Remove labels, then fill with colored water, dried herbs or nothing at all. Print vintage-style labels : Bat Wings, Eye of Newt, Moonlight Tonic : and age them by dabbing with cold tea and singeing the edges carefully. Top with corks and twine. Group them on a shelf or mantel for an apothecary look.

6. Mason-jar lanterns

Paint jars with orange or white glass paint, or wrap them in gauze, then add googly eyes for mummies or cut black paper faces for mini jack-o'-lanterns. Drop in an LED tea light. These look wonderful lining a table or steps.

7. Toilet-roll glowing eyes

Cut eye shapes into cardboard tubes and fill them with glow sticks. Hide them in bushes, hedges or under the porch for creatures watching from the dark. Kids love making these.

8. Branch wreath with crows

Gather a bundle of bare twigs, bind them into a loose circle with wire and spray-paint them matte black. Add faux crows, a few dried leaves or a small skull. It's dramatic, natural and nearly free.

9. Cardboard tombstones

Cut tombstone shapes from large cardboard boxes or foam insulation board. Paint gray, add texture by dabbing with a sponge, then letter funny epitaphs with a black paint pen. Prop them up with garden stakes. For outdoor durability, use foam board and seal with spray varnish.

10. Thrift-store gothic makeover

Visit a thrift shop for mismatched candlesticks, picture frames, plastic animals, vases and old books. One coat of matte black spray paint unifies everything instantly. Add black taper candles and old portraits in black frames for a haunted-manor look.

11. Spooky book stack

Wrap old hardcovers in kraft paper, then hand-letter titles such as Spells for Beginners or A History of Haunted Houses. Stack them with a candle or skull on top.

12. Paper lanterns you design yourself

For a centerpiece that glows, try a paper luminary. The free Desk Lantern Designer lets you choose silhouettes : cats, bats, ghosts, a haunted skyline : preview the lantern in 3D, then print a cut-and-fold template that builds into a real lantern over a battery tea light.

Safety notes

  • Use LED lights, not candles, in anything paper, fabric or plastic.
  • Keep spray painting outdoors and away from kids.
  • Hot glue guns and craft knives are adult jobs; give kids scissors and tape.
  • Glow sticks shouldn't be cut open : the liquid is irritating.

Making it sustainable

DIY decorations are naturally greener: they reuse packaging and last for years if stored well. Pack paper pieces flat between cardboard sheets, and label bins clearly. For more ideas, see Eco-Friendly Halloween.

Pulling it together

Choose a theme so your projects feel connected, then arrange them using the zone approach in The Ultimate Halloween Home Decorating Guide. A handful of well-placed handmade pieces : a silhouette in the window, a bat swarm by the door, glowing ghosts on the steps : often outshines a yard full of store-bought props.

Questions & answers

What are the cheapest Halloween decorations to make?

Paper bat swarms, window silhouettes, toilet-paper-roll glowing eyes, milk-jug ghosts and labeled potion bottles from recycled glass cost almost nothing and use materials most homes already have.

How do you make a cheesecloth ghost?

Drape cheesecloth over a form such as a balloon on a bottle, soak it with fabric stiffener or a glue-and-water mix, let it dry completely, then remove the form. The stiffened cloth holds its ghostly shape and can be displayed indoors or under a covered porch.

How can I make outdoor DIY decorations weatherproof?

Use plastic, foam board and outdoor paint rather than paper or cardboard, seal painted items with clear spray sealer, and add weight or stakes so pieces don't blow away.

What Halloween crafts are good for kids?

Paper bats, painted pumpkins, toilet-roll monsters, handprint spiders, milk-jug ghosts and decorated paper lanterns are easy and safe for young children with adult help on cutting.

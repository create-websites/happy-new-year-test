Halloween in Salem, Massachusetts: Witch History, October Events and Visitor Guide

Salem's Halloween fame rests on a tragedy. Understanding what really happened in 1692 makes a visit to the Witch City : especially in its 400th year : far more meaningful.

Key takeaways

  • In 1692 more than 200 people were accused of witchcraft; 20 were executed : 19 hanged and one pressed to death.
  • Must-see history: the Witch Trials Memorial, Proctor's Ledge, the Witch House and the Old Burying Point.
  • Haunted Happenings draws 1 million+ visitors; 2026 adds 400th-anniversary events and a 400-drone Halloween show.
  • Arrive by train or ferry, reserve tours ahead and visit midweek if you can.

Every October, Salem, Massachusetts, transforms into the most famous Halloween town in America. Witches in pointed hats walk the brick streets, psychics read palms on Essex Street, and costumed crowds pack the waterfront. But beneath the festivity lies a real and sobering history. To experience Salem properly : especially in 2026, the city's 400th anniversary year : it helps to know what happened here.

What really happened in 1692

In early 1692, in Salem Village (today's Danvers, a few miles from downtown Salem), a group of girls began having fits that local doctors could not explain. Under pressure, they accused neighbors of bewitching them. Accusations spread rapidly through the region.

Over the following months:

  • More than 200 people were accused of witchcraft.
  • A special Court of Oyer and Terminer was established to hear the cases, relying heavily on "spectral evidence" : testimony that the accused's spirit had appeared to victims in dreams or visions.
  • Nineteen people were hanged.
  • Giles Corey, an elderly farmer, was pressed to death under heavy stones for refusing to enter a plea.
  • Several more people, including infants, died in jail.

By 1693 doubts had grown, spectral evidence was rejected and the remaining prisoners were released. In the years that followed, jurors and judges apologized, and the colony eventually compensated some families. In 2022 Massachusetts formally exonerated the last of the convicted, Elizabeth Johnson Jr.

Myth check: Nobody was burned at the stake in Salem. Burning was a European punishment; in Salem the condemned were hanged.

Historians still debate the causes : religious tension, frontier wars, land disputes, community rivalries and mass panic have all been proposed. What is clear is that the victims were ordinary people caught up in fear.

How Salem became the Witch City

For more than two centuries Salem was better known as a wealthy seaport and the home of writer Nathaniel Hawthorne, a descendant of trial judge John Hathorne. Interest in the trials grew in the 20th century, especially after Arthur Miller's play The Crucible (1953). The 1970 television series Bewitched filmed episodes here, and in 1972 the Salem Witch Museum opened. In 1982 the city launched Haunted Happenings, and the Witch City brand was born.

Historic sites to visit

Site Why it matters
Salem Witch Trials Memorial Stone benches inscribed with the names of the 20 executed, beside the Old Burying Point; dedicated in 1992
Proctor's Ledge Memorial The confirmed site of the hangings, identified by researchers in 2016
The Witch House Home of trial judge Jonathan Corwin : the only structure in Salem with direct ties to the trials
Old Burying Point (Charter Street Cemetery) One of the oldest burial grounds in the U.S.; trial judge John Hathorne is buried here
Salem Witch Museum Dramatic presentation of the trials and an exhibit on the evolving image of witches
Rebecca Nurse Homestead (Danvers) Home of one of the most respected victims
Peabody Essex Museum World-class art museum that holds original trial documents (not always on display)
The House of the Seven Gables The mansion that inspired Hawthorne's novel

Salem Haunted Happenings 2026

Salem Haunted Happenings runs throughout October. Highlights announced for 2026 include:

  • Thursday, October 1 : The Haunted Happenings Grand Parade from Shetland Park to Salem Common.
  • Saturday, October 10 : The Howl-o-ween Pet Parade.
  • October 16–17 and 23–24 : Salem Night Faire at Pioneer Village.
  • Saturday, October 17 : Witch Hats on the Wharf, a community photo and world-record attempt on Derby Wharf for the 400th anniversary.
  • Saturday, October 24 : Haunted Speakeasy at Hamilton Hall and Wicked Night on the Wharf.
  • Saturday, October 31 : Live music downtown from noon, ending with a 400-drone show over the North River at 10 p.m.

Earlier in the season the city also hosted a food-truck festival and the Salem Glass Pumpkin Patch on Salem Common.

Visitor guide

Getting there

The city strongly encourages visitors not to drive in October. Instead:

  • Train: The MBTA commuter rail runs from Boston's North Station to Salem in about 30 minutes.
  • Ferry: A seasonal ferry connects Boston with Salem's waterfront.
  • Park and ride: Satellite parking lots with shuttles operate on busy weekends.

Where to start

Download the Destination Salem app for maps, parking and events, and start at the Visitor Information Center at 245 Derby Street.

When to go

  • Midweek in early October : the best balance of atmosphere and crowds.
  • Weekends : lively, crowded and expensive.
  • Halloween itself : a citywide street party; spectacular but very crowded, especially on a Saturday.

Book ahead

Reserve museums, ghost tours, restaurants and hotels well in advance. Many sell out weeks before October weekends.

Visiting respectfully

Salem balances commerce and commemoration. Enjoy the costumes and fun, but remember:

  • The memorials honor real victims : keep voices low and don't sit on the memorial stones for photos.
  • Cemeteries are fragile; follow posted rules and opening hours.
  • Salem has a large modern witch and pagan community; it's a living faith for many residents, not just a costume.

Questions & answers

Why is Salem famous for Halloween?

Salem is famous for the witch trials of 1692, when more than 200 people were accused of witchcraft and 20 were executed. In the 20th century the city leaned into its witch associations, and since 1982 it has hosted Salem Haunted Happenings, a month-long October festival.

Were any witches burned in Salem?

No. Nineteen people convicted in the Salem trials were hanged, one man, Giles Corey, was pressed to death with heavy stones for refusing to enter a plea, and several others died in jail. Burning witches was a European practice, not an American one.

Where were the Salem witches hanged?

In 2016 researchers confirmed that the executions took place at Proctor's Ledge, a rocky outcrop below Gallows Hill. A memorial was dedicated there in 2017.

What is happening in Salem for Halloween 2026?

Highlights of Salem Haunted Happenings 2026 include the Grand Parade on October 1, the Howl-o-ween Pet Parade on October 10, Witch Hats on the Wharf on October 17, the Salem Night Faire, and a Halloween night finale with a 400-drone show over the North River marking the city's 400th anniversary.

Event dates are from Salem's official 2026 Haunted Happenings announcements. Some had already taken place by the publication date. Confirm details at hauntedhappenings.org and salem.org before visiting.

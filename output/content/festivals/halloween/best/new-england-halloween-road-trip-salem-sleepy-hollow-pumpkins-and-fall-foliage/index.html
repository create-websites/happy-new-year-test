New England Halloween Road Trip: Salem, Sleepy Hollow, Pumpkins and Fall Foliage

Witch trials, headless horsemen, red maples and cider donuts. A week-long loop through America's most atmospheric Halloween country : timed to the foliage.

By Prashant Dhingra · October 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Loop from Sleepy Hollow through the Berkshires, Vermont and New Hampshire to Salem in about a week.
  • Peak foliage moves south: northern VT/NH in late Sept–early Oct, southern New England mid–late Oct.
  • Don't miss Concord's Sleepy Hollow Cemetery, the Kancamagus Highway and Salem.
  • Book Salem and Sleepy Hollow stays early; drive north first to catch the color.

There is no better place for an autumn road trip than New England in October. Maples blaze red and gold, farm stands overflow with pumpkins, white church steeples rise over village greens : and the region's history of witch trials, literary ghosts and old burying grounds gives every drive a delicious shiver. This seven-day loop combines the best of Halloween and fall foliage.

The route at a glance

Day Route Highlights
1 New York City → Sleepy Hollow, NY Old Dutch Church, Sleepy Hollow Cemetery, Jack O'Lantern Blaze
2 Hudson Valley → the Berkshires, MA Orchards, farm stands, Lenox and Stockbridge
3 Berkshires → southern Vermont Covered bridges, village greens, cider
4 Central and northern Vermont Foliage drives, cider mill, maple everything
5 Vermont → White Mountains, NH Kancamagus Highway, waterfalls, mountain views
6 White Mountains → Concord, MA → Salem Authors' Ridge, then the Witch City
7 Salem → Providence or Fall River Lovecraft, jack-o'-lanterns or Lizzie Borden

Total driving: roughly 700–800 miles, with no single day much over four hours.

Why this direction? Foliage peaks first in the north and at elevation, so heading north early in the week and finishing on the coast keeps you near peak color.

Day 1: Sleepy Hollow, New York

Start in the village that Washington Irving made famous. Visit the Old Dutch Church and wander Sleepy Hollow Cemetery, where Irving is buried. In the evening, see Historic Hudson Valley's Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze : thousands of carved, glowing pumpkins : which runs September 18 to November 8, 2026. Book timed tickets well in advance. Full guide: Sleepy Hollow, New York.

Day 2: The Hudson Valley to the Berkshires

Drive north through Hudson Valley apple country, stopping at orchards and farm stands. Cross into western Massachusetts to the Berkshires, where hillside towns like Lenox and Stockbridge look straight out of a Norman Rockwell painting (his museum is in Stockbridge). Stay overnight in an old inn.

Day 3: Into southern Vermont

Head north into Vermont on scenic back roads. Look for covered bridges, steepled villages and roadside stands selling cider, pumpkins and maple syrup. Towns like Manchester and Woodstock make perfect overnight stops.

Day 4: Heart of Vermont

Spend the day on foliage drives : scenic routes through the Green Mountains are spectacular in early October. Stop at a cider mill for fresh cider and cider donuts, and look for pumpkin displays on village greens. Overnight near Stowe or Waterbury.

Day 5: New Hampshire's White Mountains

Cross into New Hampshire and drive the Kancamagus Highway, one of America's most celebrated foliage routes, winding about 34 miles through the White Mountain National Forest past waterfalls, overlooks and rivers. Pull over at scenic overlooks and short trails.

Day 6: Concord and Salem

Head south to Concord, Massachusetts, and its other Sleepy Hollow: Sleepy Hollow Cemetery, where Authors' Ridge holds the graves of Emerson, Thoreau, Louisa May Alcott and Nathaniel Hawthorne. It's a quiet, beautiful place on an autumn afternoon.

Then drive to Salem, the Witch City. Walk the Witch Trials Memorial, the Old Burying Point and the Witch House, then take an evening ghost tour. In 2026 Salem celebrates its 400th anniversary during Haunted Happenings. Book lodging months ahead, or stay in Boston and take the train. Read Halloween in Salem, Massachusetts and Salem at Halloween: Complete October Travel Guide.

Day 7: Choose your finale

Option A: Providence, Rhode Island

Providence was home to horror writer H. P. Lovecraft, buried in Swan Point Cemetery. In autumn, the Jack-O-Lantern Spectacular at Roger Williams Park Zoo displays thousands of artistically carved pumpkins along an illuminated trail : check current-year dates and tickets.

Option B: Fall River, Massachusetts

Visit the Lizzie Borden House, scene of the infamous 1892 murders, now a museum and bed-and-breakfast offering tours and overnight stays for the brave.

Option C: More Salem

Spend another day in Salem and nearby Marblehead, a harbor town with colonial streets and an old burial hill overlooking the sea.

Seasonal food to try

  • Cider donuts from orchard bakeries
  • Fresh-pressed apple cider
  • Maple creemees (soft-serve) in Vermont
  • Clam chowder and lobster rolls on the coast
  • Pumpkin bread and pie from farm stands
  • Apple crisp in old inns

Practical tips

  1. Book lodging early : foliage season is New England's busiest time, and Salem in October is extremely popular.
  2. Follow state foliage reports for real-time color.
  3. Avoid weekend traffic on the most famous routes; drive them early in the morning.
  4. Pack layers : mornings can be near freezing in the mountains.
  5. Carry cash for farm stands.
  6. Allow extra time : you'll stop constantly for photos.

Shorter versions

  • Long weekend: Salem + Concord + Boston.
  • Four days: Sleepy Hollow + the Berkshires + Salem.
  • Foliage focus: Vermont + New Hampshire loop, ending in Salem.

Questions & answers

When is the best time for a New England Halloween road trip?

Early to mid-October is ideal, combining good foliage with Halloween events. Northern Vermont and New Hampshire usually peak in late September or early October, while southern New England and the coast peak in mid- to late October.

How many days do you need for a New England fall road trip?

Five to seven days allows a relaxed loop including the Hudson Valley, the Berkshires, Vermont, New Hampshire's White Mountains and Salem. A long weekend can cover Salem plus one foliage region.

Are there two Sleepy Hollows?

Yes. Sleepy Hollow, New York, is the village made famous by Washington Irving's story. Sleepy Hollow Cemetery in Concord, Massachusetts, is a separate place, famous for Authors' Ridge, where Ralph Waldo Emerson, Henry David Thoreau, Louisa May Alcott and Nathaniel Hawthorne are buried.

Can you visit the Lizzie Borden house?

Yes. The house in Fall River, Massachusetts, where Andrew and Abby Borden were killed in 1892, operates as a museum and bed-and-breakfast offering tours and overnight stays.

Foliage timing varies by elevation and weather each year; follow state foliage reports. Event dates and attraction hours should be confirmed before travel.

Planning a Haunted Attraction Road Trip: How to Build the Ultimate Halloween Weekend

One haunt is a night out. Three in a weekend is a pilgrimage. Here's how to build the ultimate Halloween road trip around America's scariest attractions.

By Prashant Dhingra · October 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Choose a region with several great haunts within about two hours of each other.
  • Plan no more than two haunts per night : most run roughly from dusk to midnight.
  • Buy timed or fast-pass tickets ahead and keep a weather backup plan.
  • Fill daytime with ghost tours, farms or history, and never drive drowsy after a late haunt.

Haunt fans know the feeling: you finish one great haunted house and immediately want another. A haunted attraction road trip turns that craving into an adventure : a weekend or week spent driving between some of America's most ambitious scare attractions, with ghost tours, pumpkin farms and diner breakfasts in between.

Step 1: Choose a region

The best haunt road trips focus on a cluster of attractions within about one to two hours' drive of each other. Some strong regions:

Region Why it works
Philadelphia – Lancaster – Hudson Valley Dense with renowned haunts plus colonial history and fall foliage
Southern California Knott's Scary Farm, Halloween Horror Nights Hollywood and Disneyland's Halloween season close together
Central Florida Halloween Horror Nights in Orlando, Howl-O-Scream in Tampa and ghost tours in St. Augustine
St. Louis – Chicago corridor Big indoor haunts in both cities and Midwest farm country between
Deep South Haunts in Atlanta and Baton Rouge plus ghost cities like Savannah and New Orleans

See America's Best Haunted Houses for attraction details and Halloween at Amusement Parks for theme-park options.

Step 2: Set your schedule

Timing rules

  • Most haunts open around dusk and close near midnight, with some later on weekends.
  • Two haunts per night is realistic; a major theme-park event usually takes a whole night.
  • Arrive at opening for the shortest lines.
  • Weeknights and early-season dates are calmer; the final weekend before Halloween is the busiest. In 2026 that's October 30–31.

Daytime fillers

Your days are free : use them:

  • Ghost tours and historic sites : Salem, Sleepy Hollow, Philadelphia, Savannah, St. Augustine
  • Pumpkin patches and corn mazes : see America's Best Pumpkin Patches and The Ultimate Corn Maze Guide
  • Cemetery walks in historic towns
  • Sleep : seriously; late nights add up

Step 3: Book smart

  1. Buy timed tickets online : many haunts sell out on weekends.
  2. Consider fast passes for the most popular attractions.
  3. Book lodging early, ideally between two haunts so drives stay short.
  4. Check refund and weather policies : outdoor haunts may close for storms.
  5. Keep a backup : an indoor haunt nearby in case of rain.

Step 4: Budget

Expense Notes
Haunt tickets Vary by attraction and night; fast passes add more
Theme-park scare events Usually the priciest single tickets
Lodging Cheaper outside city centers and midweek
Fuel and tolls Plan routes to avoid backtracking
Food Late-night options are limited : stock the car
Parking Many haunts charge, often cash only

Step 5: Stay safe

  • Don't drive drowsy. Haunts end late; plan short drives afterward or stay nearby.
  • Rotate drivers if you can.
  • Eat real meals : adrenaline and candy aren't dinner.
  • Know each haunt's safe word or exit procedure.
  • Mind health warnings : strobes, fog and tight spaces.
  • Keep valuables locked away : many haunts don't allow bags.

Sample weekend routes

Northeast: "Colonial Chills" (3 nights)

  • Day 1: Philadelphia history by day; Eastern State Penitentiary's Halloween Nights at night.
  • Day 2: Drive west to Lancaster County farms; Field of Screams at night. Or head south-west of Philly for Bates Motel.
  • Day 3: Drive to the Hudson Valley (about 3 hours); explore Sleepy Hollow by day (Sleepy Hollow, New York); evening at the Headless Horseman Hayrides & Haunted Houses or the Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze.
  • Extension: Continue to Salem : see the New England Halloween Road Trip.

West: "SoCal Screams" (3 nights)

  • Night 1: Knott's Scary Farm in Buena Park.
  • Night 2: Halloween Horror Nights at Universal Studios Hollywood.
  • Night 3: Something gentler : Disneyland's Halloween Time or the Oogie Boogie Bash.
  • Days: Beaches, pumpkin patches or a drive along the coast. See the California Halloween Road Trip.

Florida: "Sunshine Scares" (3 nights)

  • Night 1: Halloween Horror Nights in Orlando.
  • Night 2: Howl-O-Scream at Busch Gardens Tampa Bay.
  • Day 3: Drive to St. Augustine for history; lantern ghost tour at night (St. Augustine, Florida).

Midwest: "Heartland Haunts" (2–3 nights)

  • Night 1: The Darkness in St. Louis.
  • Day 2: Drive north through Illinois farm country : pumpkin patches around Morton, the self-styled Pumpkin Capital.
  • Night 2: A Chicagoland haunt such as Statesville Haunted Prison.
  • Extension: Richardson Adventure Farm's giant corn maze. See Halloween in the Midwest.

Packing list

  • Closed-toe, comfortable shoes
  • Warm layers and a rain jacket
  • Phone chargers and a car power bank
  • Downloaded tickets and screenshots
  • Cash for parking and food stands
  • Snacks and water for the car
  • A small flashlight
  • Earplugs and an eye mask for daytime sleep

Make memories, not just screams

Keep a running "scare ranking" with your crew, collect wristbands and tickets, and take photos at each entrance (not inside). A well-planned haunt road trip gives you stories for years : and maybe a few nightmares.

Questions & answers

How many haunted houses can you do in one night?

Two is realistic for most attractions. Most open around dusk and close near midnight, and lines can be long. Large multi-attraction parks or theme-park events may take a whole evening on their own.

When is the best time for a haunted house road trip?

Late September to mid-October offers the best balance: all attractions are open and lines are shorter than on the final weekend before Halloween. Weeknights and Thursday or Sunday nights are often quieter.

How much does a haunted attraction road trip cost?

Costs vary by region. Budget for haunt tickets (often more with fast passes), fuel, lodging, food and parking. Choosing weeknights, booking lodging early and staying slightly outside major cities helps.

What should I pack for a haunted road trip?

Comfortable closed-toe shoes, warm layers, a rain jacket, phone chargers, snacks and water for the car, printed or downloaded tickets, cash for parking, and a small flashlight.

Attraction schedules and operating status change each year. Verify every stop's dates, hours and ticket policies before you commit to a route.

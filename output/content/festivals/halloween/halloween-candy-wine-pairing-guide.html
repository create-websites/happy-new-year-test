Pairing candy and wine is most successful when approached as a fun tasting experience rather than following strict guidelines. Key factors to consider are sweetness, acidity, chocolate level, saltiness, and texture. Opt for small portions and focus on combinations that bring pleasure instead of worrying about being "right."

Match sweetness before complexity

Dry wines can taste harsh when paired with sugary candy, while dessert wines, off-dry styles, and fruit-forward wines are better suited for sweet pairings compared to lean, tannic reds.

Chocolate likes different partners

Milk chocolate pairs well with softer, fruitier wines, while dark chocolate complements deeper flavors but may clash with high tannins. White chocolate's rich sweetness makes it a great match for aromatic or lightly sparkling styles.

Caramel, nuts and salt change the equation

Try pairing a single candy with both caramel and peanut flavors with a touch of salt to experience how the wine's taste is transformed.

Keep the tasting clearly adult

This activity is restricted to adults who are of legal drinking age. Be sure to offer water, food, and non-alcoholic options, and avoid turning a fun pairing into an obligation to drink.

Frequently asked questions

Is there one best wine for Halloween candy?

Different candy recipes and individual preferences can differ. Instead of strict rules, consider sweetness, acidity, and intensity as helpful guidelines.

Can I run the same tasting without alcohol?

Sure. Experiment with sparkling grape juice, tea, cold brew, spiced cider, or botanical non-alcoholic beverages to see how their sweetness and acidity complement different types of candy.

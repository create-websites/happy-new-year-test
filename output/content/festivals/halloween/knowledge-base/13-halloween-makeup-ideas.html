Halloween Makeup Ideas

A good face can carry the simplest costume. These looks run from five-minute whiskers to full skull and special-effects wounds, with the products and techniques to pull them off safely.

At a glance

Halloween makeup ranges from simple face paint for children to dramatic special-effects looks. Popular looks include the black cat, vampire, witch, skeleton or skull, zombie, clown and stitched monster. Use face paints labeled for the intended skin area, patch-test 24 hours in advance, prime the skin, build color in layers, set with powder, and remove gently with oil-based cleanser. Avoid getting glitter or paint near the eyes and never use craft paints or markers on skin.

Makeup is the fastest way to transform a costume : or to become the costume. A sheet and a pair of sunglasses is a joke; a sheet with a painted gaunt skull face underneath is a ghost story. Whether you want five-minute whiskers or a full special-effects wound, these looks will get you there.

Key takeaways

  • Use face paint labeled for skin and the intended application area; follow its directions, test it before use, and never use craft paint, markers or acrylics.
  • Prime, layer and set: thin layers of color, sealed with powder or setting spray, last all night.
  • Easy wins: cat whiskers, vampire, witch, sugar-skull-inspired florals and half-face skulls.
  • Remove with an oil-based cleanser and moisturize; never scrub.

Never use craft paint, acrylics, permanent markers or school markers on skin. They can cause rashes and are hard to remove.

Before you start: safety first

  • Patch-test each product on your inner arm 24 hours before Halloween.
  • Choose products labeled for the area where you will use them, and follow their directions. “Cosmetic grade” alone does not establish suitability near the eyes or lips; consult FDA novelty-makeup guidance.
  • Keep glitter and loose pigment away from eyes.
  • Avoid sharing sponges and brushes to prevent infections.
  • Use decorative contact lenses only with a valid prescription and professional fitting; never buy or wear them as an unprescribed costume accessory.

Easy looks (5–15 minutes)

Black cat

  1. Wing out black eyeliner past the outer corner of each eye.
  2. Paint the tip of the nose black, extending a thin line down to the top lip.
  3. Add three or four whisker lines on each cheek, plus small dots at their base.
  4. Optional: dark lipstick and a touch of gray shading on the cheeks.

Vampire

  1. Use foundation one or two shades lighter than your skin, or a light dusting of white powder.
  2. Contour cheekbones with gray or purple shadow.
  3. Smoky dark eyes; red or deep plum lips.
  4. A single drip of fake blood from the corner of the mouth.

Witch

  1. Choose a green, gray or natural base.
  2. Dramatic dark eyes and brows.
  3. Dark lips : black, plum or deep green.
  4. Add a fake wart with a dot of brown makeup or a small bit of scar wax.

Friendly face paint for little ones

Pumpkin cheeks, a bat across the forehead, a spider web from one eye, or a small rainbow. Keep it to the cheeks and forehead for comfort.

Intermediate looks (20–40 minutes)

Half-face skull

  1. Paint one half of the face white with a sponge.
  2. Outline the eye socket in black and fill it in, softening the edge with gray.
  3. Paint a black upside-down heart shape for the nose cavity.
  4. Draw teeth extending across the lips onto the cheek: a horizontal line with vertical lines and a little gray shading.
  5. Shade the cheekbone hollow with gray and black.

Full skeleton

Extend the skull across the whole face and continue with neck vertebrae and collarbones if your costume reveals them.

Floral skull (Día de los Muertos-inspired)

Inspired by the calaveras of Mexico's Day of the Dead. Use a white base, black eye sockets ringed with colorful petals, a black nose and stitched lips, with flowers, dots and swirls in bright colors. If you choose this look, treat it with respect for its cultural meaning : see Halloween Around the World.

Zombie

  1. Apply a gray-green base unevenly, heavier around the eyes and under cheekbones.
  2. Add purple-red shadow around the eyes and in the creases.
  3. Dab dark brown around the hairline and jaw for "dirt."
  4. Add scratches and a few drips of fake blood.

Advanced: special effects (45 minutes+)

Stitched wound

  1. Draw a jagged line with dark red liner.
  2. Surround it with purple and red bruising, blended out.
  3. Draw black "X" or short line stitches across.
  4. Add a little fake blood along the cut.

3D cut with scar wax

  1. Warm scar wax between your fingers and press it onto the skin, feathering the edges until they disappear.
  2. Cover with foundation to match your skin.
  3. Shape a groove in the wax with a blunt makeup spatula, keeping the tool away from the eyes and applying no pressure to the skin.
  4. Fill the cut with dark red cream, then fake blood.

Liquid latex textures

Layer liquid latex with tissue to create peeling or decaying skin. Always patch-test : some people are allergic to latex : and never apply near eyebrows without a barrier (it pulls hair on removal).

Make it last

  1. Start with clean, moisturized skin.
  2. Apply a primer.
  3. Build color in thin layers, letting each dry.
  4. Set with translucent powder after every major layer.
  5. Finish with a setting spray.

Removal

Massage in an oil-based cleanser or balm, let it dissolve the paint, wipe gently with a warm washcloth, then cleanse and moisturize. Don't scrub. Use the dedicated remover for spirit gum or latex.

Pairing makeup with costumes

Makeup turns budget costumes into showstoppers. Pair these looks with the closet-based ideas in DIY Halloween Costumes and the popular characters in Best Halloween Costume Ideas. For trick-or-treating kids, makeup is often a safer choice than masks : find out why in Halloween Costume Safety.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest Halloween makeup look?

A black cat is the easiest: a black nose dot, a few whiskers, winged eyeliner and optional dark lips. A vampire with pale foundation, dark eyes, red lips and a drip of fake blood is nearly as simple.

Is Halloween face paint safe for kids?

Choose face paint intended for skin and follow the label’s directions and age guidance. Check whether it is permitted near the eyes or lips, test a small area before use, avoid shared applicators, and remove it as directed. A cosmetic-grade label alone is not a guarantee against irritation.

How do I make Halloween makeup last all night?

Start with clean, moisturized skin and a primer, apply color in thin layers, set each layer with translucent powder, and finish with a setting spray. Avoid touching your face.

How do I remove heavy Halloween makeup?

Massage in an oil-based cleanser or cleansing balm to break down the paint, wipe gently with a soft cloth, then wash with a mild cleanser and moisturize. For spirit gum or prosthetics, use the matching remover.

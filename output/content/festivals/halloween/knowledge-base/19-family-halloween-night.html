Family Halloween Night

Not every Halloween has to be a big party. A night at home with a movie, a game, a craft and a bowl of popcorn can become the tradition your kids remember most.

At a glance

A family Halloween night combines a themed dinner, a craft or carving session, games such as mummy wrap, Halloween bingo and flashlight hide-and-seek, storytelling, and a movie chosen by scare level, from gentle picks like It's the Great Pumpkin, Charlie Brown to mildly spooky family films like Hocus Pocus, Coraline and The Nightmare Before Christmas. Build the evening around trick-or-treating hours, keep scares age-appropriate and end with a cozy wind-down.

The biggest Halloween memories aren't always made at parties or haunted houses. Often they're made at home: the year everyone carved pumpkins at the kitchen table, the night Grandpa told the ghost story, the movie you watch every October under the same blanket. Here's how to build a family Halloween night that works for every age.

Key takeaways

  • Build the evening around trick-or-treat hours : dinner first, then out, then the movie.
  • Choose movies by scare level, not just age rating.
  • Mix one active game, one craft and one quiet activity like storytelling.
  • End with a cozy wind-down so little ones aren't too wired to sleep.

Plan the evening around trick-or-treating

In 2026 Halloween falls on a Saturday, which leaves plenty of room for an all-day celebration. A sample schedule:

Time Activity
Afternoon Craft or pumpkin carving; bake treats
5:00 p.m. Early Halloween dinner
5:45 p.m. Costumes on, photos on the porch
6:00–7:30 p.m. Trick-or-treating (or hand out candy)
7:30 p.m. Candy sorting and trading
8:00 p.m. Movie with popcorn
Bedtime Cozy story and lights out

For more new-style family rituals, see Halloween for Modern Families. If your family doesn't trick-or-treat, hand out candy together : kids love being the ones at the door : or attend a community event like a trunk-or-treat (see Trunk-or-Treat Explained).

Halloween dinner

Keep dinner quick, warm and fun: mummy hot dogs, jack-o'-lantern quesadillas, "witches' brew" soup or a chili cauldron. A big meal before trick-or-treating also slows the candy rush. The Ultimate Halloween Food Guide has plenty of ideas, and Halloween Desserts for Kids covers the sweet side.

Movies by scare level

Ratings tell you about content, but not how scary something feels to your child. Group your options by scare level instead.

Level 1: Not scary at all (preschoolers)

  • It's the Great Pumpkin, Charlie Brown (1966)
  • Room on the Broom (animated short)
  • Spookley the Square Pumpkin
  • Classic Halloween cartoon shorts

Level 2: A little spooky (early elementary)

  • Casper (1995)
  • Hotel Transylvania (2012)
  • The Addams Family (1991 or animated versions)
  • Halloweentown (1998)
  • Hocus Pocus (1993)

Level 3: Genuinely spooky (older kids)

  • The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993)
  • Monster House (2006)
  • ParaNorman (2012)
  • Coraline (2009)
  • Frankenweenie (2012)

Level 4: Classic chills (tweens and teens with parents)

  • Ghostbusters (1984)
  • Beetlejuice (1988)
  • The Witches (1990)

Every child is different : some six-year-olds laugh at Coraline, and some ten-year-olds hide from Casper. Preview anything you're unsure about.

Games for all ages

Mummy wrap race

Teams wrap one person in toilet paper. Fastest complete mummy wins. Great for photos.

Halloween bingo

Print bingo cards with Halloween pictures : pumpkin, bat, ghost, witch : and call them out with candy corn as markers.

Pumpkin bowling

Use small pumpkins as bowling balls and plastic bottles (or toilet-paper rolls painted as ghosts) as pins.

Flashlight hide-and-seek

Lights off, one seeker with a flashlight. Spooky without being frightening.

Monster freeze dance

Dance to spooky songs and freeze like a monster when the music stops.

"What's in the box?"

Peeled grapes for eyeballs, cold spaghetti for worms, a wet sponge for a brain. Players reach in blindfolded and guess. Shrieks guaranteed.

Spooky scavenger hunt

Hide small treats or clues around the house or yard with riddles leading to the next spot. See Halloween Activities for Kids for printable-style ideas.

Crafts

  • Carve or paint pumpkins : younger children paint or use stickers, older kids carve with supervision. The Pumpkin Carving Masterclass has tips; everyone can sketch faces on paper first.
  • Paper lanterns : draw and fold a simple paper luminary with adult help, then light it with a battery tea light; see DIY Halloween Decorations.
  • Paper bats : a quick craft that decorates the whole house; see DIY Halloween Decorations.
  • Decorate treat bags with markers and stickers before trick-or-treating.

Storytelling by flashlight

Turn off the lights, gather around a single lantern and tell stories. Ideas:

  • Read Washington Irving's The Legend of Sleepy Hollow (simplified for younger kids).
  • Tell the Irish legend of Stingy Jack : see The Story of the Jack-o'-Lantern.
  • Play "one sentence at a time," where each person adds a line to a spooky story.
  • Share family stories : the funniest costume, the scariest Halloween.

Keep scares in the fun zone

  • Let kids choose their level of scary.
  • Talk about how costumes, makeup and props work : knowing the trick takes the fear away.
  • Give children a "pause" signal they can use any time.
  • Avoid gory displays and jump-scare houses for younger children.

The wind-down

After a big night, children need time to settle. A warm bath, pajamas, a mug of warm milk or hot cocoa and a gentle Halloween picture book help. Let them pick two or three candies for "tomorrow" and put the rest away.

Make it a tradition

Families who repeat the same small rituals each year : the same movie, the same dinner, the same porch photo : often find those become the most treasured parts of Halloween. Write down what you did this year and do it again next year. That's how traditions are made.

Frequently asked questions

What are good Halloween movies for young kids?

Gentle picks include It's the Great Pumpkin, Charlie Brown (1966), Room on the Broom, Spookley the Square Pumpkin, and Winnie the Pooh's Halloween specials. Classic animated Halloween shorts are also a safe start.

What Halloween movies are good for the whole family?

Family favorites include Hocus Pocus, The Nightmare Before Christmas, Casper, Hotel Transylvania, Monster House, The Addams Family, Coraline and ParaNorman. Check ratings and reviews, as several are scarier than they look for young children.

What Halloween games can all ages play?

Mummy wrap races, Halloween bingo, pumpkin bowling, flashlight hide-and-seek, monster freeze dance, a spooky scavenger hunt and 'guess what's in the box' are fun for toddlers through grandparents.

How do I keep Halloween from being too scary for my kids?

Let children choose their own level of scary, preview movies and attractions, talk about how costumes and decorations work, avoid gory displays, and give them a safe signal to stop. Many kids enjoy spooky more when they feel in control.

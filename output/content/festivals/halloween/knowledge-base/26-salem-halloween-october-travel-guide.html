Salem at Halloween: Complete October Travel Guide

A practical playbook for visiting America's Witch City in its busiest month : and its 400th-anniversary year : without spending the whole trip in traffic or lines.

At a glance

Visiting Salem, Massachusetts, in October requires planning: book lodging months ahead, visit midweek in early or mid-October if possible, and arrive by MBTA commuter rail from Boston's North Station (about 30 minutes) or by seasonal ferry rather than driving. Base yourself downtown or in Boston, reserve museums and ghost tours in advance, and combine the historic trial sites with Essex Street, the waterfront, the Peabody Essex Museum and side trips to Marblehead and Danvers. Salem Haunted Happenings draws more than a million visitors each October.

Salem is the most famous Halloween destination in the United States, and in October it feels like the whole world has the same idea. More than a million people visit during Salem Haunted Happenings, and 2026 brings extra attention as the city celebrates its 400th anniversary. With the right plan, though, it's one of the most memorable autumn trips in America.

For the history behind the Witch City and this year's event calendar, start with Halloween in Salem, Massachusetts. This guide is about logistics.

Key takeaways

  • Book lodging early : October weekends can sell out months in advance.
  • Don't drive. Take the MBTA commuter rail (about 30 minutes from North Station) or the seasonal ferry.
  • Visit midweek in early or mid-October for the best atmosphere-to-crowd ratio.
  • Reserve museums, ghost tours and dinner tables ahead, especially for weekends.

Getting there

Salem is about 16 miles northeast of Boston. In October, do not plan to drive if you can avoid it : traffic backs up for miles and parking is scarce and expensive.

  • Commuter rail. The MBTA's Newburyport/Rockport Line runs from North Station in Boston to Salem in about 30 minutes. Trains are crowded on October weekends; buy tickets via the MBTA app.
  • Ferry. A seasonal ferry runs between downtown Boston and Salem's waterfront : scenic and stress-free.
  • Satellite parking. On peak weekends Salem operates park-and-ride lots with shuttles.
  • From the airport. Boston Logan is about 30–45 minutes away by car without traffic.

Where to stay

In Salem

Staying in town lets you enjoy evening ghost tours and the quieter streets after day-trippers leave. Options include historic inns, B&Bs and a handful of hotels near the Common and the waterfront : the grand Hawthorne Hotel on Salem Common is the classic choice. Rooms for October weekends often book up many months in advance and command premium rates.

In nearby towns

Beverly, Danvers, Peabody and Marblehead offer more choice and lower prices, a short drive or ride away.

In Boston

Many visitors stay in Boston and take the train. It's often cheaper, offers more rooms, and lets you combine Salem with a Boston city break.

Getting around

Downtown Salem is compact and best explored on foot. The main historic sites, Essex Street pedestrian mall, waterfront and museums are within about 15 minutes of each other. Wear comfortable shoes : the brick sidewalks are uneven.

What to see

Witch-trial history

  • Salem Witch Trials Memorial and the adjacent Old Burying Point
  • Proctor's Ledge Memorial, the confirmed execution site
  • The Witch House, home of trial judge Jonathan Corwin
  • Salem Witch Museum

Beyond the witches

  • Peabody Essex Museum : one of the oldest continuously operating museums in the U.S., with world-class maritime, Asian and American art collections. Allow at least two hours.
  • The House of the Seven Gables : the 17th-century mansion that inspired Hawthorne's novel.
  • Salem's waterfront : Derby Wharf, the historic Custom House where Hawthorne worked, and the replica tall ship Friendship.
  • Chestnut Street : a beautiful street of Federal-style mansions, lovely in autumn light.

Halloween fun

  • Essex Street : psychics, witch shops, street performers and costumed crowds.
  • Haunted houses and wax museums : plenty of family-friendly scares near the Common.
  • Ghost tours : a range of walking tours run every evening in October; book ahead.

Sample itineraries

One day

  • Morning: Witch Trials Memorial, Old Burying Point, Witch House.
  • Midday: Lunch near Essex Street; browse the shops.
  • Afternoon: Salem Witch Museum or Peabody Essex Museum; waterfront walk.
  • Evening: Dinner, then a lantern-lit ghost tour.

Two days

  • Day 1: As above.
  • Day 2: House of the Seven Gables, Proctor's Ledge, Chestnut Street, then a side trip to Marblehead for its harbor and historic Old Town, or to Danvers for the Rebecca Nurse Homestead.

Eating and drinking

Salem has a surprisingly strong restaurant scene, from seafood to cocktail bars. In October:

  • Reserve dinner a week or more ahead for weekends.
  • Eat early or late to avoid peak waits.
  • Grab lunch outside the core : a few blocks from Essex Street, lines shrink.

Budget tips

  • Visit midweek for lower lodging prices.
  • Stay in Boston or neighboring towns.
  • Combine paid museums with free sites : the memorials, cemeteries and waterfront are free to visit.
  • Bring snacks and water; lines can be long.

What to pack

  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Warm layers and a rain jacket : New England October can swing from mild to cold
  • A costume or witch hat (you won't be the only one)
  • A phone charger for long days of photos and maps

Crowd strategy for Halloween week

  • Arrive early in the morning, before the trains fill.
  • Visit the main memorials first, before midday crowds.
  • Schedule ticketed attractions with timed entry.
  • Pick a meeting point if your group gets separated.
  • Plan your train home; the last trains on Halloween are packed.

Make it a bigger trip

Salem fits perfectly into a New England autumn journey with Boston, Sleepy Hollow and the foliage of Vermont and New Hampshire. See the New England Halloween Road Trip, or compare Salem with other haunted towns in America's Best Halloween Destinations.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Salem?

One full day covers the main historic sites and downtown. Two days lets you add the Peabody Essex Museum, a ghost tour, the waterfront and a side trip to Marblehead or Danvers without rushing.

Is it better to stay in Salem or Boston?

Staying in Salem lets you enjoy evening tours and the town after day-trippers leave, but October rates are high and rooms scarce. Staying in Boston is often easier and cheaper, with a short commuter-rail ride to Salem.

How crowded is Salem on Halloween?

Extremely. Salem draws more than a million visitors in October, and Halloween itself is the peak, especially when it falls on a Saturday as it does in 2026. Streets close to cars, trains are packed and many attractions sell out.

How do you get from Boston to Salem?

The MBTA commuter rail runs from Boston's North Station to Salem in about 30 minutes. A seasonal ferry also connects Boston's waterfront with Salem. Driving is discouraged in October because of traffic and very limited parking.

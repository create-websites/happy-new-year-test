Sleepy Hollow, New York

Washington Irving gave this Hudson River village a headless horseman two centuries ago. Every October, Sleepy Hollow invites the whole country to come and look for him.

At a glance

Sleepy Hollow, New York, a Hudson River village about 30 miles north of Manhattan, is the setting of Washington Irving's 1820 tale The Legend of Sleepy Hollow. Key sites include the Old Dutch Church, Sleepy Hollow Cemetery where Irving is buried, Philipsburg Manor and Irving's home Sunnyside. Historic Hudson Valley's Halloween season, including the Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze with thousands of hand-carved pumpkins and dramatic readings of Irving's Legend, runs September 18 to November 8, 2026. Tickets are timed and sell out; Metro-North trains reach nearby Tarrytown.

Few places on earth are as perfectly tied to a single story as Sleepy Hollow. In 1820 Washington Irving published The Legend of Sleepy Hollow, the tale of lanky schoolmaster Ichabod Crane and the Headless Horseman who chases him through the woods one autumn night. Two centuries later, the village on the Hudson River has become one of America's essential Halloween destinations.

Key takeaways

  • Irving's The Legend of Sleepy Hollow (1820) made this village America's original Halloween setting.
  • Don't miss the Old Dutch Church and Sleepy Hollow Cemetery, where Irving is buried.
  • The Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze and Halloween in Sleepy Hollow events run Sept 18–Nov 8, 2026.
  • Buy timed tickets early and consider Metro-North to Tarrytown instead of driving.

The legend in brief

Irving set his story in the Dutch farming community of Sleepy Hollow, a drowsy valley "under the sway of some witching power." Its most famous ghost is the Headless Horseman, said to be a Hessian trooper whose head was carried away by a cannonball during the Revolutionary War, and who rides each night in search of it.

Ichabod Crane, courting the wealthy Katrina Van Tassel, leaves a harvest party late one night and is pursued by the Horseman to the bridge by the church. The next morning, his horse is found, his hat lies near the stream : and beside it, a shattered pumpkin. Ichabod is never seen in Sleepy Hollow again.

The story's mix of humor, harvest imagery and ghostly dread made it one of the first great American works of fiction, and helped tie pumpkins, autumn and ghosts together in the American imagination (see The Story of the Jack-o'-Lantern).

A village that took the name

For most of its history the village was called North Tarrytown. In 1996 residents voted to rename it Sleepy Hollow, embracing the legend that already drew visitors. Today you'll find Headless Horseman imagery everywhere, from the village seal to street signs and a dramatic Horseman sculpture.

Places to visit

Old Dutch Church

Built in the late 1600s, this stone church is one of the oldest churches in New York State and appears in Irving's tale as the place near which the Horseman tethers his steed. Its burying ground is said to be where the Horseman rests.

Sleepy Hollow Cemetery

Next to the church, this vast, hilly 19th-century cemetery is the resting place of Washington Irving himself, as well as industrialists and notable New Yorkers. Seasonal lantern tours explore its history and legends. Visit by day to wander its winding paths and Gothic mausoleums.

Philipsburg Manor

A restored colonial-era mill and farm interpreting the lives of the people, including enslaved Africans, who lived and worked here in the 1700s. It's also a key venue for Historic Hudson Valley's Halloween programming.

Sunnyside

Washington Irving's picturesque home, a short drive south in Tarrytown, wrapped in wisteria and overlooking the Hudson. Seasonal "Legendary" tours focus on Irving and his tales.

Patriot's Park and the bridge

The village commemorates the Revolutionary War history that inspired the Hessian ghost. Pause at the stream crossings and imagine Ichabod's desperate ride.

The Headless Horseman sculpture

A striking metal sculpture of the Horseman on the main road makes a classic photo stop.

Halloween in Sleepy Hollow 2026

Historic Hudson Valley runs an extensive Halloween season. For 2026, the Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze and the Halloween in Sleepy Hollow events run from September 18 to November 8. Programs listed for 2026 include:

  • The Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze : a walk-through path of thousands of hand-carved, glowing jack-o'-lanterns arranged into giant sculptures and scenes.
  • Irving's Legend : dramatic performances of the tale.
  • An Audience with Poe : a theatrical evening with Edgar Allan Poe's dark tales.
  • Headless Horseman Files and Spirits of Sleepy Hollow Country : immersive, story-driven experiences.
  • Twilight Village : evening programming in a historic setting.
  • Legendary Tours at Washington Irving's Sunnyside.

Events are timed-entry and frequently sell out on weekends. Historic Hudson Valley advertised lower-priced tickets on Monday–Thursday nights after 8 p.m. through October 29.

Getting there

  • By train: Metro-North's Hudson Line runs from Grand Central Terminal to Tarrytown or Philipse Manor in under an hour. From the station, use taxis, rideshares or event shuttles where offered.
  • By car: About 30 miles north of Manhattan. Expect heavy weekend traffic; follow event parking instructions.
  • By foot: Within the village, many sites are walkable.

Tips for visiting

  1. Buy tickets early, especially for Fridays and Saturdays.
  2. Go on a weeknight for smaller crowds.
  3. Dress warmly : Hudson Valley evenings get cold.
  4. Visit the cemetery by day, then return for evening events.
  5. Read or listen to the story before you go; it makes every stop more vivid.
  6. Eat in Tarrytown, which has a lively main street of restaurants.

Make it part of a bigger trip

Sleepy Hollow pairs beautifully with apple-picking and pumpkin farms across the Hudson Valley and with Salem in Massachusetts. See the New England Halloween Road Trip, America's Best Pumpkin Patches and America's Best Halloween Destinations.

Frequently asked questions

Is Sleepy Hollow a real place?

Yes. Sleepy Hollow is a village in Westchester County, New York, on the Hudson River. Formerly known as North Tarrytown, it officially adopted the name Sleepy Hollow in 1996 in honor of Washington Irving's story.

Is the Headless Horseman real?

The Headless Horseman is a fictional character from Washington Irving's 1820 story, said to be the ghost of a Hessian soldier who lost his head to a cannonball during the Revolutionary War. Irving drew on local Dutch folklore and European tales of headless riders.

What is the Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze?

The Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze is a walk-through Halloween display by Historic Hudson Valley featuring thousands of hand-carved, illuminated jack-o'-lanterns arranged into sculptures and scenes. In 2026 it runs from September 18 to November 8.

Where is Washington Irving buried?

Washington Irving is buried in Sleepy Hollow Cemetery, adjacent to the Old Dutch Church, which appears in The Legend of Sleepy Hollow.

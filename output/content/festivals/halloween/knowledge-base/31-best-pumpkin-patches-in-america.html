Pumpkin Patches & Farms

America's Best Pumpkin Patches

A good pumpkin patch is part farm, part festival and part photo studio. Here's how to find the great ones : and a regional starter list from coast to coast.

4 min readGuide 1 of 4 in this topic

Planning note. Farms change attractions, ticketing and hours every year, and some require advance reservations. Always check each farm's website before visiting.

At a glance

The best U.S. pumpkin patches combine real fields where visitors pick pumpkins off the vine with fall attractions such as hayrides, corn mazes, petting farms and cider. Notable examples by region include Bishop's Pumpkin Farm and Half Moon Bay farms in California; Roloff Farms, The Pumpkin Patch on Sauvie Island and Remlinger Farms in the Pacific Northwest; Schnepf Farms in Arizona; Dewberry Farm and the Dallas Arboretum's pumpkin village in Texas; Richardson Adventure Farm and Sever's in the Midwest; and Cox Farms, Linvilla Orchards and Burt's Pumpkin Farm in the East and South. Prices, tickets and hours change every season.

There's something deeply satisfying about wandering a field on a crisp autumn day to choose the perfect pumpkin. Pumpkin patches have become one of the most beloved Halloween traditions in America : part farm visit, part festival, part photo shoot. The best combine genuine agriculture with old-fashioned fun.

Key takeaways

  • The best patches let you pick pumpkins in a real field, not just from a pile.
  • Look for hayrides, corn mazes, animals and fresh cider : and check whether entry is ticketed.
  • Go early in the season and on weekdays for the best pumpkins and the shortest lines.
  • Regional favorites range from Half Moon Bay and Sauvie Island to Cox Farms and Burt's.

What makes a great pumpkin patch?

Feature Why it matters
Real field picking Cutting a pumpkin off the vine is the heart of the experience

Hayrides A ride out to the field is half the fun
Corn maze The perfect companion attraction

Food Cider donuts, kettle corn, caramel apples, fresh cider

Many big patches now run as ticketed fall festivals with timed entry on weekends. Smaller farms may be free to enter. Both have their charms : the right choice depends on your family.

West Coast

California

  • Bishop's Pumpkin Farm (Wheatland) : A Sacramento Valley classic with fields, a train, animals and family attractions.
  • Half Moon Bay farms : The coastal town south of San Francisco is famous for pumpkins; family-run farms line Highway 92 and Highway 1. Half Moon Bay's 54th Art & Pumpkin Festival took place October 17–18, 2026, alongside a world-championship pumpkin weigh-off.
  • Southern California : Seasonal patches pop up across Los Angeles, Orange and San Diego counties, along with farms in the Central Valley.

Oregon

  • Roloff Farms (Hillsboro) : Known from TV and popular for its pumpkin season.
  • The Pumpkin Patch on Sauvie Island (Portland) : A beloved farm on an island in the Columbia River, with fields, a corn maze and hayrides.

Washington

  • Remlinger Farms (Carnation) : A Snoqualmie Valley farm with a fall festival, rides and U-pick pumpkins.
  • Snohomish County farms : A cluster of popular fall farms north of Seattle.

More on the region in Halloween in the Pacific Northwest.

Southwest

  • Schnepf Farms (Queen Creek, Arizona) : A long-running family farm with a big fall festival.
  • Dewberry Farm (Brookshire, Texas) : West of Houston, known for its fall festival, corn maze and pumpkin patch.
  • Dallas Arboretum (Dallas, Texas) : Its autumn celebration features a "pumpkin village" built from tens of thousands of pumpkins, squash and gourds : more garden spectacle than picking patch, and spectacular for photos.

Midwest

  • Richardson Adventure Farm (Spring Grove, Illinois) : Famous for one of the largest corn mazes in the world, plus pumpkins and fall activities.
  • Kuipers Family Farm (Maple Park, Illinois) : Fall festival with pumpkins, apples and a corn maze.
  • Sever's Fall Festival (Shakopee, Minnesota) : A Twin Cities favorite with a corn maze and pumpkin patch.
  • Uncle John's Cider Mill (St. Johns, Michigan) : A Michigan cider tradition with fall fun.

Illinois grows more pumpkins than any other state, most of them for canned pumpkin. See Halloween in the Midwest.

East Coast

  • Cox Farms (Centreville, Virginia) : A legendary Northern Virginia fall festival with slides, hayrides and pumpkins.
  • Linvilla Orchards (Media, Pennsylvania) : A large family farm near Philadelphia with pick-your-own pumpkins and apples.
  • Hudson Valley farms (New York) : Dozens of orchards and pumpkin farms pair perfectly with a trip to Sleepy Hollow (Sleepy Hollow, New York).
  • New England farms : From Connecticut to Vermont, pumpkins meet peak fall foliage. See the New England Halloween Road Trip.

South

  • Burt's Pumpkin Farm (Dawsonville, Georgia) : A North Georgia mountain tradition with hayrides and colorful pumpkins.
  • Tennessee and the Carolinas : Mountain farms in the Smokies and Blue Ridge combine pumpkins with fall color.

How to choose a patch near you

  1. Search your county's agritourism listings or state agriculture department's farm directory.
  2. Read recent reviews : patches change from year to year.
  3. Check the website for tickets, hours, weather closures and what's included.
  4. Match attractions to your kids' ages : toddlers love animals and small hay mazes; teens want big mazes and haunted attractions.
  5. Ask about pricing : per pumpkin, by weight or by size.

Make the most of your visit

  • Go on a weekday or right at opening on weekends.
  • Bring a wagon and wear boots : fields can be muddy.
  • Carry pumpkins from the bottom, never by the stem.
  • Bring cash for small farm stands.
  • Plan a full day using How to Plan the Perfect Family Pumpkin-Patch Day.

Back home

Store uncarved pumpkins in a cool, dry place out of direct sun. Carve them a few days before Halloween : the Pumpkin Carving Masterclass explains how : and sketch designs on paper first. And to understand how your pumpkin grew, read From Pumpkin Patch to Jack-o'-Lantern.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to go to a pumpkin patch?

Late September through mid-October offers the best selection and weather. Weekday mornings are least crowded. Pumpkins picked in early October will last until Halloween if left uncarved and kept cool and dry.

How much does a pumpkin patch cost?

It varies widely. Some small farms charge nothing to enter and only sell pumpkins by weight or size. Larger 'fall festival' farms charge admission, often higher on weekends, which may include hayrides, mazes and play areas.

What should I look for in a good pumpkin patch?

A real growing field, clean facilities, reasonable crowds, clear pricing, activities for your kids' ages, food and cider, and good reviews from recent seasons. Ticketing and parking information on the farm's site is a good sign of organization.

What state grows the most pumpkins?

Illinois grows the most pumpkins in the United States, largely for processing into canned pumpkin. Many other states, including California, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Texas and Washington, are major producers of ornamental and carving pumpkins.

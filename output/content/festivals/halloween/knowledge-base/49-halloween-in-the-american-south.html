Halloween in the American South

Live oaks, Spanish moss, centuries-old cemeteries and stories told on wide porches. The American South may be the country's richest region for ghost lore : and October is the perfect time to explore it.

4 min readGuide 12 of 13 in this topic

Planning note. Many haunted sites are private homes, working inns or fragile historic places with set tour hours. Confirm access and schedules before visiting, and treat cemeteries and sites tied to slavery with respect.

At a glance

The American South is famous for Halloween-season ghost tourism. A coastal route links Charleston (founded 1670), Savannah (1733) and St. Augustine (1565), with New Orleans as a separate highlight. Historic cemeteries include Savannah's Bonaventure and Colonial Park, Charleston's churchyards, and New Orleans' St. Louis Cemetery No. 1. Southern legends include Tennessee's Bell Witch, South Carolina's Gray Man of Pawleys Island, and reputedly haunted sites such as the Myrtles in Louisiana, Waverly Hills Sanatorium in Kentucky and Sloss Furnaces in Alabama, which hosts a Halloween haunted attraction. October's milder weather makes evening walking tours comfortable.

In the American South, ghost stories aren't just Halloween entertainment : they're part of how history is told. Centuries of colonial conflict, plagues, war, slavery, storms and fires have left layers of memory in old port cities and country towns. Add live oaks dripping with moss, iron gates and gas-lit streets, and you have the most atmospheric Halloween destination region in America.

Key takeaways

  • A coastal ghost-city route: Charleston → Savannah → St. Augustine, plus New Orleans.
  • Historic cemeteries: Bonaventure, Colonial Park, Charleston's churchyards and St. Louis No. 1.
  • Legends to know: the Bell Witch (TN), the Gray Man (SC) and the Myrtles (LA).
  • October brings milder evenings : ideal for lantern walking tours.

The coastal ghost-city route

Three of the oldest cities in the United States sit within a few hours' drive of each other along the Atlantic coast.

Charleston, South Carolina

Founded in 1670, Charleston has cobblestone lanes, grand antebellum homes and some of the most beautiful churchyards in the country. Ghost tours explore:

  • Historic churchyards : such as those of the Unitarian Church and St. Philip's, among the oldest burial grounds in the city
  • The Old City Jail, used from the early 1800s until 1939
  • The Old Exchange and Provost Dungeon, where colonial-era prisoners were held
  • The Battery and White Point Garden, where pirates were once hanged

Savannah, Georgia

Founded in 1733, Savannah's 22 squares, Bonaventure Cemetery and Colonial Park Cemetery make it one of the most popular ghost-tour cities in America. Full guide: Savannah, Georgia at Halloween.

St. Augustine, Florida

Founded in 1565, the oldest continuously inhabited European-founded city in the continental U.S. offers ghost tours of its colonial quarter, lighthouse and Old Jail, plus the Castillo de San Marcos. Full guide: St. Augustine, Florida.

Driving times: Charleston to Savannah is about 2 hours; Savannah to St. Augustine about 3 hours.

New Orleans: the essential detour

New Orleans is farther west : roughly 9 to 10 hours' drive from Savannah : so many travelers fly or make it a separate trip. It's worth it: French Quarter ghost and vampire tours, above-ground "cities of the dead," Voodoo history and the Krewe of BOO! parade (October 24, 2026). Full guide: New Orleans at Halloween.

Southern legends and haunted places

The Bell Witch (Adams, Tennessee)

One of America's most famous ghost stories tells of a spirit that tormented the Bell family around 1817–1821. Visitors can tour the Bell Witch Cave north of Nashville, and the legend inspired films and books.

The Gray Man (Pawleys Island, South Carolina)

A ghostly figure in gray is said to walk the beach before major hurricanes, warning residents to leave. Those who see him, the story goes, find their homes spared.

The Myrtles (St. Francisville, Louisiana)

A historic plantation house, now an inn, often called one of America's most haunted homes. Its ghost stories have been retold for decades : and historians have challenged many of them. Visits should acknowledge the real history of the enslaved people who lived and worked there.

Waverly Hills Sanatorium (Louisville, Kentucky)

A massive former tuberculosis hospital, famous among paranormal enthusiasts, offering historical and paranormal tours.

Sloss Furnaces (Birmingham, Alabama)

A National Historic Landmark ironworks, preserved as a museum, that hosts a popular Halloween haunted attraction each fall.

Grove Park Inn (Asheville, North Carolina)

A grand 1913 mountain resort known for its legendary "Pink Lady" ghost : combine it with Blue Ridge fall foliage.

The Menger Hotel (San Antonio, Texas)

Next to the Alamo, this 19th-century hotel is famous for ghost lore : pair it with a visit to the Alamo itself.

Sample 7-day itinerary

  1. Charleston : historic district walk by day, ghost tour by night.
  2. Charleston : plantations and history tours with honest interpretation of slavery; Magnolia Cemetery.
  3. Drive to Savannah : squares, Forsyth Park, evening tour.
  4. Savannah : Bonaventure Cemetery, Telfair Museums, haunted pub crawl (21+).
  5. Drive to St. Augustine : Castillo de San Marcos, lighthouse.
  6. St. Augustine : Old Jail, ghost tour, beaches.
  7. Fly or drive to New Orleans for an extended stay, or head home.

Southern food for the season

  • Shrimp and grits, gumbo, red beans and rice
  • Pralines and benne wafers
  • Boiled peanuts from roadside stands
  • Pecan and sweet potato pie
  • Beignets in New Orleans

Practical tips

  1. October weather is usually pleasant, but it can still be warm : choose breathable costumes.
  2. Hurricane season runs through November; buy refundable bookings and watch forecasts.
  3. Bug spray for evening tours near marshes and moss.
  4. Book Halloween weekend lodging early in popular cities.
  5. Respect history : many sites involve slavery, war and disease; good tours treat these stories seriously.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most haunted city in the South?

Savannah, New Orleans, Charleston and St. Augustine are each frequently called one of America's most haunted cities, thanks to their long histories, old buildings and cemeteries and thriving ghost-tour industries.

What is the Bell Witch legend?

The Bell Witch is a Tennessee legend about a spirit said to have tormented the family of John Bell in Adams, Tennessee, between about 1817 and 1821. It is one of the most famous American ghost stories, and visitors can tour the Bell Witch Cave.

Who is the Gray Man of Pawleys Island?

The Gray Man is a South Carolina legend about a ghostly figure seen walking the beach at Pawleys Island before major hurricanes. Residents who see him are said to be spared from the storm's damage.

Is October a good time to visit the South?

Yes. October usually brings lower humidity and comfortable evenings across much of the South, which is ideal for walking ghost tours. Hurricane season technically runs through November, so watch forecasts and buy refundable bookings.

Sustainable Spookiness: An Eco-Friendly Halloween Guide

Halloween is a great time to get creative and find ways to minimize waste. The most eco-friendly decorations and costumes are often made from items you already have, borrowed, repaired, or can reuse in the future.

Start with what already exists

Consider shopping your closet, swapping with friends, visiting a thrift store, or repurposing pieces from previous years before purchasing a new costume. By utilizing a basic black outfit, old sheet, cardboard, scarves, and accessories, you can easily transform them into numerous recognizable costumes with some creative styling.

Decorate with reusable and compostable materials

Opt for fabric bunting, paper silhouettes, natural branches, pumpkins, gourds, and reusable lights instead of disposable plastics. Organize and store these items in labeled bins to create a reusable annual collection rather than a one-time purchase.

Make pumpkins part of the food cycle

If a carving pumpkin is in good condition and safe for consumption, utilize the flesh or seeds as needed; otherwise dispose of it in accordance with local regulations. Make sure to remove any candles, paint, glitter, or artificial decorations before composting.

Rethink treats and energy use

Purchase candy in quantities that match the anticipated number of visitors to avoid excess, consider adding a variety of non-edible treats, and employ LED lights with timers for a more sustainable approach. Consistency in these practices allows for easy repetition annually.

Frequently asked questions

Are artificial decorations always worse than natural ones?

It's not always the case. Opting for a long-lasting decoration that can be used year after year may be a better choice than constantly purchasing disposable options. The durability factor is important.

Can painted pumpkins be composted?

It varies based on the type of paint and the composting regulations in your area. Opt for plain, untreated pumpkin material for the safest composting option.

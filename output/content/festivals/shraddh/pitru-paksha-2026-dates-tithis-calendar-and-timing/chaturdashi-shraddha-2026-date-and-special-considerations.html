Chaturdashi Shraddha 2026: Date and Special Considerations

For New Delhi, Chaturdashi Shraddha 2026 falls on October 9, 2026. Use a Panchang set to the city where the observance is performed before using clock timings.

Location and tradition note

Core dates on this page use a New Delhi reference unless a different city is named. Tithi boundaries, Kutup/Rohina/Aparahna windows and sometimes the civil observance date can differ by city. Family, regional and sampradaya practice can also vary.

Chaturdashi Shraddha 2026 date in 2026

For the New Delhi reference calendar, Chaturdashi Shraddha 2026 falls on October 9, 2026. This is an observance date, not a claim that the tithi runs from midnight to midnight.

Many Panchang traditions apply special rules to Chaturdashi Shraddha, particularly for some categories of untimely death. Confirm the applicable family or priestly rule rather than applying a generic article universally.

How to use this date

Use the date only after confirming that the ancestor’s death tithi matches this Shraddha and that the local Panchang for the ceremony location assigns the same civil date. Clock timings should always be taken from the local city calculation.

Frequently asked questions

When does Pitru Paksha 2026 start and end in New Delhi?

The main Pratipada-to-Amavasya sequence runs from September 27 through October 10, 2026. Purnima Shraddha on September 26 is commonly listed immediately before it.

Why do some 2026 calendars show different dates outside India?

Because a tithi can cross local daytime at different clock times. For example, Eastern North American reference calendars can place the opening and closing Shraddha dates one civil day earlier than New Delhi.

Why are Chaturthi and Panchami both on September 30, 2026?

In the New Delhi reference, both tithis intersect the relevant afternoon date-selection period on September 30, so both Shraddha observances are assigned to that civil date.

Should I use these pages as a universal ritual instruction?

No. These pages are calendar and planning guides. Family, regional and sampradaya practice can vary, and final ritual/timing decisions should use the local Panchang and trusted family or priestly guidance.

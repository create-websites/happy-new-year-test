The Psychology of Shraddh: Grief, Memory and Family History

Discover the benefits of incorporating structured ancestral remembrance to aid in grief processing, family storytelling, and maintaining a sense of continuity alongside mental-health care.

Shraddh Hub
Why this guide matters

Although Shraddh is a religious observance, it shares common traits with modern grief research, such as maintaining connections with the deceased, engaging in rituals to honor their memory, sharing family stories, receiving social support, and setting aside dedicated time to process loss.

Ritual gives grief a container

Grief is often unpredictable, but a recurring remembrance day can provide structure and closure.

That framework can help the family navigate challenging emotions by providing clear steps: prepare, gather, remember, offer, and conclude.

Continuing bonds rather than “moving on”

Current views on grief acknowledge that it is possible to mourn in a healthy way without needing to completely let go of the relationship with the deceased. Connections can be maintained through various means such as stories, values, recipes, photos, and commemorations.

Shraddh establishes a lasting connection in a religious and familial context.

Family history as identity

Learning about our ancestors can uncover migration stories, occupations, languages, conflicts, and acts of resilience, all of which contribute to our shared identity across generations. Sharing and preserving these details can help younger family members gain a deeper understanding of their heritage.

The ritual can serve as a catalyst for oral history, rather than just a mere ceremony.

Know the limits

While rituals can provide comfort, they should not be considered a substitute for professional help in dealing with prolonged grief, depression, trauma, or family violence.

A balanced approach encourages the coexistence of spiritual practice and mental health support without requiring each to justify the other's focus.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Can Shraddh help with grief?

While individual experiences may vary, it can offer structure, memories, and family backing.

Is Shraddh a form of therapy?

No, it is a religious ceremony, despite sharing some similarities with healthy mourning practices.

When should someone seek professional help?

If feelings of grief, depression, trauma, or anxiety start to greatly interfere with daily life or safety, seeking professional help is recommended.

Why 16 Days? The Lunar and Spiritual Logic of Pitru Paksha

Learn why Pitru Paksha spans the waning lunar fortnight, how tithis shape ancestral observance, and why the period is commonly described as sixteen days.

Shraddh Hub
Why this guide matters

Pitru Paksha unfolds during the dark or waning fortnight of the lunar month. The exact civil dates vary each year because Hindu observances follow lunar tithis rather than a fixed Gregorian schedule. The period is popularly described as sixteen days, culminating in Sarvapitri Amavasya.

The rhythm of the waning moon

The waning fortnight moves from fullness toward the new moon. In Hindu ritual symbolism, this quieter phase is often associated with inwardness, remembrance and release. Pitru Paksha uses that lunar rhythm to create a dedicated season for ancestors.

Because tithis do not map perfectly onto 24-hour civil days, calendar counts can look different depending on location and year.

Why death tithi matters

Many families perform Shraddh on the tithi corresponding to an ancestor’s death rather than on a fixed Gregorian anniversary. This makes the ritual calendar relational: different days of the fortnight can become especially significant for different branches of a family.

When the exact tithi is unknown, Sarvapitri Amavasya is traditionally used as an inclusive day of remembrance.

Why the period is called sixteen days

Popular explanations count the full ancestral fortnight together with the concluding new-moon observance. The number should be understood within the lunar calendar system rather than as a rigid promise that every local calendar will display exactly sixteen midnight-to-midnight dates.

A local panchang is the best guide for precise observance.

The spiritual purpose of a defined season

Setting aside a limited period gives remembrance structure. Instead of grief and ancestry being abstract ideas, households receive a calendar window for ritual, stories, food, charity and reflection.

That rhythm can also prevent ancestral practice from becoming constant fear: Pitru Paksha is a focused season, after which the festive cycle moves forward.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Is Pitru Paksha always exactly 16 calendar days?

It is traditionally described as sixteen days, but local tithi calculations can make civil-date spans appear different.

Why is the waning moon important?

The waning fortnight provides a traditional symbolic setting for introspection, remembrance and ancestral rites.

What if I do not know an ancestor’s death tithi?

Sarvapitri Amavasya is traditionally used for ancestors whose specific tithi is unknown or whose rites were missed.

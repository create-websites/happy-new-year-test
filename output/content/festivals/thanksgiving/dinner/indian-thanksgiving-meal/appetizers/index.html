Thanksgiving Appetizers: Chaat Bar, Samosas and Pakoras for Arriving Guests

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

Thanksgiving dinner often runs late, and Indian guests rarely arrive on time : which is exactly why the appetizer table matters. A good spread of chaat, samosas and chai keeps everyone happy while the turkey rests or the biryani finishes.

Key takeaways

  • A self-serve chaat bar is interactive, mostly make-ahead and works for all ages.
  • Samosas reheat well in the oven; avoid microwaving them.
  • Keep appetizers lighter than usual : the main meal is large.
  • Offer a hot drink such as masala chai or a non-alcoholic drink like jaljeera or mango lassi.
  • Plan two to three pieces of each snack per guest if dinner follows.

Appetizers for a long evening

Thanksgiving appetizers have one job: keep guests comfortable without spoiling their appetite. Indian snacks are perfect for this, but they are also filling, so choose two or three and keep portions small. Mini samosas instead of large ones, a single pakora variety instead of three, and plenty of fresh elements.

Set up a self-serve chaat bar

A chaat bar turns appetizers into an activity. Lay out bowls in order:

  1. Bases: papdi, crushed samosas, aloo tikki, roasted sweet potato cubes, boiled chickpeas.
  2. Yogurt: lightly sweetened, whisked smooth, kept chilled.
  3. Chutneys: green chutney, tamarind-date chutney and the cranberry chutney from dinner.
  4. Crunch and colour: sev, chopped onion, coriander, pomegranate seeds.
  5. Seasoning: chaat masala, roasted cumin, chilli powder.

Everything except the tikki can be prepared ahead. Assemble only at the moment of eating so the crisp parts stay crisp. See chaat varieties and pani puri for more ideas.

Samosas: make, freeze, reheat

Samosas are the easiest crowd snack to plan. Make and freeze them unfried, or buy good frozen or bakery samosas. Bake or fry from frozen and reheat on a rack in a hot oven so the pastry stays crisp. A Thanksgiving variation: fill with spiced potato, peas and a little roasted pumpkin, or with leftover turkey keema on the following days. See samosa.

Pakoras and tikkas

Pakoras are best hot, so fry in small batches as guests arrive, or choose a baked option. Onion bhaji and paneer pakora are dependable; sliced sweet potato or butternut squash pakoras fit the season. Pakora varieties covers batter consistency.

Paneer tikka skewers can be marinated the night before and roasted in one tray shortly before guests arrive. For mixed groups, make chicken tikka on a separate tray.

Drinks to pass around

  • Masala chai in a large pot or thermos : welcome on a cold November evening.
  • Mango lassi or sweet lassi for children.
  • Jaljeera or nimbu pani as a tangy, non-alcoholic appetite opener.
  • Spiced apple cider with cardamom and ginger as a fusion option.

How much to prepare

When a full dinner follows, two to three pieces of each snack per guest is a sensible starting estimate, with two or three snack types in total. If appetizers are the main event : an open house or an afternoon gathering : double it. See tea-time snacks for lighter options such as dhokla and roasted chana.

Frequently asked questions

What Indian appetizers are best for Thanksgiving?

Mini samosas, a self-serve chaat bar, paneer tikka, pakoras and dhokla, served with masala chai or lassi.

How do I keep samosas crisp for a party?

Reheat them on a rack in a hot oven rather than the microwave, and keep chutneys in separate bowls.

How many appetizers per guest before Thanksgiving dinner?

About two to three pieces of each snack per guest, with two or three snack types, when a full dinner follows.

The Indian Thanksgiving Bread Basket: Naan, Kulcha and Pooris Instead of Dinner Rolls

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

Dinner rolls exist to mop up gravy. Indian breads do the same job and bring their own personality : garlic naan with makhani gravy, puris with kaddu sabzi, kulcha with chole.

Key takeaways

  • Naan and kulcha reheat well; puris and bhature are best fried close to serving.
  • For big parties, buy good restaurant or grocery naan and reheat it wrapped in foil.
  • Match the bread to the dish: puri with dry sabzi and halwa, naan with rich gravies, roti with dal.
  • Plan roughly two pieces of bread per adult when rice is also served.
  • Keep breads warm in a cloth-lined basket or covered insulated container.

Naan for a crowd

Home ovens can't match a tandoor, and making naan for twenty people while also hosting is a lot to ask. Most hosts buy naan from a restaurant or Indian grocery and finish it at home: brush with melted butter or garlic butter, sprinkle with coriander, stack in foil and warm in the oven. If you make your own, bake earlier in the day and reheat the same way. See naan varieties.

Puris: the festive choice

Puffed puris are the bread of celebration in many North Indian and Gujarati homes, paired with potato or pumpkin sabzi and halwa. They are best eaten soon after frying. At a big gathering, the practical options are to roll the dough balls ahead and fry in batches while one family member keeps the kadai going, or to serve puris early as part of the first wave of food. See poori and bhatura.

Kulcha and stuffed breads

Stuffed kulcha with potato, paneer or onion is substantial enough that guests eat fewer pieces, which helps with planning. Amritsari-style kulcha with chole makes a complete potluck contribution on its own. See kulcha.

Keeping breads warm and soft

  • Wrap stacks of naan or roti in foil and hold in a low oven.
  • Line a basket with a clean cloth or napkin and fold it over the breads.
  • An insulated casserole carrier keeps rotis soft for potlucks.
  • Refill the basket in small batches rather than putting out everything at once.

How many breads to plan

A practical starting estimate is two pieces of naan, kulcha or roti per adult when rice is also on the table, and three to four puris per adult if puris are the main bread. Children typically eat about half. Leftover naan freezes well and becomes excellent next-day turkey or paneer wraps.

Frequently asked questions

Can I serve naan instead of dinner rolls at Thanksgiving?

Yes. Naan does the same job : mopping up gravy : and pairs especially well with paneer, makhani gravy and tandoori turkey.

How do I keep naan warm for a party?

Brush with butter, wrap in foil and hold in a low oven, then serve from a cloth-lined basket in small batches.

How many naans per person for a party?

Plan about two pieces per adult when rice is also served, and about half that for children.

Indian Thanksgiving Cooking Timeline: A Make-Ahead Plan from One Week Out

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

Indian festive cooking has a reputation for keeping the host in the kitchen all day. It doesn't have to. Many Indian dishes : gravies, dals, chutneys, halwa : taste better made ahead, which makes them ideal for a holiday.

Key takeaways

  • Gravies, dals, chutneys and many sweets improve when made a day or two ahead.
  • Breads, fried snacks, rice and final tadkas are best done on the day.
  • A large frozen turkey can need four to six days to thaw in the refrigerator.
  • Clear refrigerator space before you start; Indian menus use a lot of containers.
  • Write a serving-time schedule and post it on the refrigerator.

One week before (Thursday–Friday)

  • Finalise guest count, dietary needs and menu.
  • Make a shopping list split by store: Indian grocery (paneer, spices, basmati, frozen samosas, naan), supermarket (turkey, produce, dairy), warehouse store (bulk rice, oil, disposables).
  • Order restaurant naan or catering items for larger events.
  • Check that you have large pots, hotel pans, a food thermometer and enough serving spoons.

Five to six days before (Friday–Saturday)

  • Buy a frozen turkey and start thawing it in the refrigerator. USDA and CDC guidance is about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds; a 20-pound bird needs about five days.
  • Shop for spices and pantry items.
  • Make and freeze samosas or koftas if making from scratch.

Two to three days before (Monday–Tuesday)

  • Make cranberry chutney and tamarind chutney.
  • Cook dal makhani, chole or rajma; cool quickly and refrigerate.
  • Make gravy bases for paneer butter masala and malai kofta.
  • Prepare halwa or kheer if serving cold or reheated.
  • Make ginger-garlic paste and roast and grind spice blends.

The day before (Wednesday)

  • Marinate the turkey or turkey parts (second stage) and paneer for tikka.
  • Chop vegetables for sides; store in containers.
  • Make mint-coriander chutney.
  • Prepare dough for puris or the filling for stuffed pumpkin.
  • Make gulab jamun and keep in syrup.
  • Set the table, label serving dishes and print dish cards.

Refrigerator and freezer tips

Clear space a few days before. Use shallow containers so cooked food cools quickly, stack flat, and keep raw turkey on the bottom shelf on a tray. A cooler with ice can hold drinks and raita overflow. Label every container with the dish name and date.

Frequently asked questions

Which Indian dishes can be made ahead for Thanksgiving?

Dals, chole, rajma, gravy bases, cooked chutneys, halwa, kheer and gulab jamun can be made one to three days ahead; samosas and koftas can be frozen.

How early should I thaw my turkey?

Allow about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds in the refrigerator; a 20-pound turkey needs about five days.

What should I cook on Thanksgiving Day itself?

Breads, fried snacks, rice dishes, roasting, final tadkas, garnishes and raita assembly.

Cranberry Chutney and Indian Condiments for the Thanksgiving Table

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

Cranberry sauce is the one Thanksgiving dish that already thinks like an Indian chutney: sweet, sour and meant to cut through rich food. Lean into that and build a whole condiment tray around it.

Key takeaways

  • Cook cranberries with jaggery or sugar, ginger, red chilli and a mustard-seed tempering for a true chutney.
  • A condiment tray of three chutneys, one raita and one pickle lifts every dish on the table.
  • Most chutneys can be made several days ahead; herb chutneys are best within a day or two.
  • Offer chilli heat through condiments so main dishes can stay mild.
  • Small labelled bowls keep a buffet tidy and help guests with allergies.

Why cranberries make a great chutney

Indian kitchens have long made sweet-sour chutneys from tart fruits : raw mango, tomato, amla, tamarind. Fresh cranberries have the same sharp acidity and natural pectin, so they cook down into a glossy, spoonable chutney in about fifteen minutes. The only difference from American cranberry sauce is the seasoning.

Cranberry-ginger chutney

Heat a little oil, crackle mustard seeds with a pinch of nigella or fennel seeds and a dried red chilli. Add grated ginger, then fresh or frozen cranberries, jaggery or sugar, a pinch of salt and a splash of water or orange juice. Simmer until most berries burst and the mixture thickens. Finish with Kashmiri chilli powder for colour and roasted cumin. It keeps well refrigerated for several days and tastes even better on day two.

Variations: add chopped dates or raisins for a Bengali-style sweet chutney, or orange zest and cinnamon for a gentler, kid-friendly version.

What about gravy?

Brown turkey gravy can stay for traditionalists, but Indian tables have plenty of options that do the same job. A makhani gravy (butter-tomato), a light kadhi, or simply the gravies of the mains : dal makhani, chole, kofta : moisten rice and turkey just as well. If you make a turkey gravy, a little garam masala and ginger in the roux connects it to the rest of the menu.

Serving tips

  • Use small bowls with their own spoons so chutneys are not mixed : important for guests avoiding garlic, onion or nuts.
  • Label each chutney with its heat level.
  • Keep dairy-based raita chilled until serving and refill from the refrigerator rather than leaving one large bowl out all evening.
  • Put a cranberry chutney jar in take-home bags; it makes a thoughtful host gift.

Frequently asked questions

What is cranberry chutney?

Cranberries cooked with jaggery or sugar, ginger and chilli, often with a tempering of mustard seeds : a spiced, Indian-style version of cranberry sauce.

How far ahead can I make cranberry chutney?

Several days ahead if refrigerated in a clean, covered container; the flavour improves after a day.

Which chutneys go with Thanksgiving dishes?

Cranberry-ginger chutney for turkey and paneer, mint-coriander chutney for tandoori dishes and snacks, and tamarind-date chutney for chaat and sweet potato.

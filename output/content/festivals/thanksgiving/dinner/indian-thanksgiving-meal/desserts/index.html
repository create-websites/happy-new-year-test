Indian Thanksgiving Desserts: Pumpkin Halwa, Sweet Potato Kheer and Mithai

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

Pumpkin pie and Indian halwa share the same soul: a humble vegetable slow-cooked with sugar, fat and warm spices into something celebratory. Thanksgiving dessert is where Indian and American traditions blend most naturally.

Key takeaways

  • Kaddu (pumpkin) halwa is the Indian cousin of pumpkin pie and needs no oven.
  • Swap pumpkin pie spice for cardamom, nutmeg and a little ginger, or use masala chai spices.
  • Sweet potato kheer and gajar halwa are seasonal, make-ahead and crowd-friendly.
  • Gulab jamun with vanilla ice cream is a reliable hit with children and non-Indian guests.
  • Diwali sweets from earlier in November can become a Thanksgiving mithai platter if stored properly.

Pumpkin (kaddu) halwa

Grate peeled pumpkin or butternut squash and cook it in ghee until it softens and the moisture evaporates. Add milk and cook down, then sugar or jaggery, cardamom and a pinch of nutmeg. Finish with toasted cashews, almonds or pecans and a few saffron strands. The texture sits between a pie filling and gajar halwa. Serve warm in small cups, or spoon over vanilla ice cream.

Cardamom or chai-spiced pumpkin pie

Keep the pie, change the spice. Replace most of the cinnamon in a standard pumpkin pie with ground cardamom, add fresh ginger, a pinch of black pepper and clove, and a touch of nutmeg : essentially masala chai spices. A pecan or pistachio crumble on top and a dollop of cardamom whipped cream or rabri complete the fusion.

Sweet potato kheer and other puddings

Shakarkandi kheer is made by simmering grated or mashed sweet potato in milk with sugar, cardamom and saffron, finished with nuts. It is naturally gluten-free and can be made a day ahead; it thickens in the refrigerator, so loosen with a little warm milk before serving. Rice kheer, seviyan and gajar halwa are equally good make-ahead options. Carrot halwa in particular is a North Indian winter dessert that suits late November perfectly.

Gulab jamun and fried sweets

Warm gulab jamun with a scoop of vanilla or cinnamon ice cream is the dessert that converts first-time guests. Make the jamuns earlier in the day and hold them in syrup, then warm gently before serving. A popular fusion idea is a gulab jamun cheesecake or trifle, layered with cream and crushed pistachios. See gulab jamun.

The post-Diwali mithai platter

With Diwali on November 8 in 2026, many households still have boxes of mithai in late November. Dry sweets such as kaju katli, besan ladoo and soan papdi keep reasonably well in airtight containers, while fresh milk-based sweets such as rasgulla, kalakand and many barfis need refrigeration and are best eaten within a few days. Check each sweet before serving and use only what is fresh. Arrange small portions on a platter with dates and spiced nuts. See Indian sweets.

Planning the dessert table

  • Choose one warm dessert (halwa or gulab jamun), one cold dessert (kheer or ice cream) and one sliceable dessert (pie or barfi).
  • Serve in small portions : dessert follows a very large meal.
  • Label nuts clearly; pistachios, cashews and almonds appear in most Indian sweets.
  • Offer masala chai or filter coffee alongside.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Indian version of pumpkin pie?

Kaddu (pumpkin) halwa : grated pumpkin cooked with ghee, milk, sugar and cardamom : is the closest Indian equivalent.

Can I put Indian spices in pumpkin pie?

Yes. Cardamom, ginger, clove, nutmeg and a pinch of black pepper give a masala chai-style pumpkin pie.

How long do Diwali sweets last?

Dry sweets such as kaju katli and besan ladoo keep longer in airtight containers; milk-based sweets such as rasgulla and kalakand need refrigeration and should be eaten within a few days.

Thanksgiving Leftovers, Indian Style: Turkey Keema, Paratha Wraps and Day-After Brunch

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

In Indian households, leftovers are never a problem : they are the start of tomorrow's menu. Thanksgiving's extra turkey, mash and vegetables are ideal raw material for parathas, rolls and curries.

Key takeaways

  • Refrigerate leftovers within two hours in shallow containers.
  • Use refrigerated leftovers within three to four days, or freeze them.
  • Reheat leftovers to 165°F or until steaming hot.
  • Turkey keema and turkey kathi rolls are the fastest Indian leftover meals.
  • Masala mash and dry vegetables make excellent paratha or tikki filling.

Leftover safety first

Thanksgiving buffets sit out for hours, so leftover handling matters. US food-safety guidance is to refrigerate leftovers within two hours of cooking, store them in shallow containers so they cool quickly, use refrigerated leftovers within three to four days, and reheat them to 165°F. Leftovers can also be frozen for longer storage. Discard anything that sat out longer than two hours : including dairy-heavy dishes like raita and kheer.

Leftover turkey, five ways

  • Turkey keema: shred or finely chop turkey, add to an onion-tomato-ginger masala with peas and garam masala. Serve with pav or roti, or stuff into samosas.
  • Turkey curry: simmer chunks briefly in leftover makhani gravy or a fresh Chettinad-style pepper masala.
  • Turkey biryani or pulao: layer turkey with fresh rice, fried onions and mint.
  • Kathi rolls: wrap spiced turkey, sliced onion, green chutney and a little egg (optional) in leftover naan or paratha.
  • Turkey soup: boil the carcass with ginger, pepper, cinnamon and bay leaf for a mulligatawny-style broth with lentils and rice.

Vegetarian leftovers

  • Masala mash parathas: leftover spiced mashed potatoes are almost exactly aloo paratha filling. See stuffed parathas.
  • Aloo tikki: shape mash with breadcrumbs and peas, pan-fry, and top with leftover chutneys and yogurt for chaat.
  • Paneer or kofta gravy: thin with a little water and serve over rice, or use as a pav bhaji-style dip.
  • Dal: leftover dal makhani often tastes even better on day two; or knead it into dough for dal parathas.
  • Biryani: reheat covered with a splash of water, or turn into a fried-rice style dish.
  • Stuffed pumpkin: scoop out the filling and the pumpkin flesh, mash, and make kaddu parathas or pumpkin soup.

A day-after brunch for houseguests

Thanksgiving weekend often means relatives staying over. A South Indian or Maharashtrian brunch feels like a reset after a heavy meal:

  • Dosa or uttapam with leftover cranberry chutney alongside coconut chutney and sambar. Buy batter ahead from an Indian grocery. See dosa and uttapam.
  • Poha : light, quick and ready in 15 minutes. See Maharashtrian food.
  • Idli with sambar for a gentle breakfast. See idli and vada.
  • Masala chai or filter coffee for everyone.

Sharing leftovers with guests

Have take-home containers ready and offer them as guests leave, especially to students, singles and people far from family. Pack hot and cold foods separately and remind guests to refrigerate them promptly.

Frequently asked questions

What can I make with leftover turkey Indian style?

Turkey keema, turkey curry, turkey biryani, kathi rolls in leftover naan, or a mulligatawny-style soup from the carcass.

How long are Thanksgiving leftovers safe?

Refrigerate within two hours, use within three to four days, and reheat to 165°F; freeze for longer storage.

What is a good Indian breakfast after Thanksgiving?

Dosa or uttapam with chutneys and sambar, poha, or idli, with masala chai or filter coffee.

Hosting a Mixed Thanksgiving Table: Vegetarian, Jain, Non-Vegetarian and Non-Indian Guests

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

An Indian American Thanksgiving table can hold a grandparent who is strictly vegetarian, a Jain family friend, a cousin who wants turkey, a colleague trying Indian food for the first time and a child with a nut allergy. Good hosting makes sure every one of them eats a full, festive meal.

Key takeaways

  • Design the menu so a vegetarian guest can build a complete plate, then add non-vegetarian dishes.
  • Use separate tables or clearly separated sections, with dedicated serving spoons.
  • Jain-friendly dishes generally avoid onion, garlic and root vegetables such as potatoes and carrots; ask guests about their own practice.
  • Nuts, especially cashews, hide in many gravies and sweets : label them.
  • Cook mains at medium heat and serve chilli on the side for guests new to Indian food.

Ask before you plan

A short message a week ahead saves a lot of anxiety: "Any dietary needs I should plan for?" Indian dietary practices are personal and vary even within families : some vegetarians eat eggs, some do not; some avoid onion and garlic only on certain days; some Jain guests follow stricter rules than others. Asking respectfully is always better than guessing.

Vegetarian-first menu design

Build the menu so that a vegetarian guest can fill a plate from every layer: a substantial main, rice, dal, vegetables, bread, chutneys and dessert. Then add turkey or meat dishes on top. This approach also helps non-Indian vegetarian and vegan friends. See vegetarian mains.

Keep vegetarian and non-vegetarian food separate

  • Cook vegetarian dishes first, or use separate pots, boards and spoons.
  • Serve vegetarian and non-vegetarian food on separate tables or clearly marked ends of the buffet.
  • Give every dish its own serving spoon and ask guests not to move spoons between dishes.
  • Roast paneer or vegetable tikka on a separate tray from the turkey.
  • Do not use turkey stock or drippings in rice, dal or vegetables meant for vegetarians.

Cooking for Jain guests

Jain dietary practice is vegetarian and commonly excludes onion, garlic and root vegetables such as potatoes, carrots and beetroot; many Jains also avoid eating after sunset and some avoid certain other foods. Practice varies, so ask your guests. Good Jain-friendly Thanksgiving options include paneer in a tomato-cashew gravy made without onion and garlic, plain dal with a cumin-hing tempering, jeera rice, green bean poriyal, khatta-meetha kaddu (pumpkin is not a root vegetable), puris and kheer. Cook these first, keep them in separate vessels and label them clearly. If Jain guests eat before sunset, consider serving their meal earlier.

Allergies and common hidden ingredients

IngredientOften hidden in
Cashews and almondsKorma, malai kofta, shahi paneer, makhani gravies, biryani garnish, most sweets
PeanutsPoha, lemon rice, chutneys, Maharashtrian and Gujarati dishes
DairyGhee in breads and dal, cream in gravies, paneer, raita, most sweets
GlutenNaan, kulcha, samosa pastry, some asafoetida (hing) blends contain wheat flour
EggSome naan doughs, cakes and baked desserts

When in doubt, keep the ingredient list on the dish card or a phone note to show guests.

Welcoming guests new to Indian food

  • Keep the main dishes at medium heat and offer green chillies, pickle and hot chutney separately.
  • Put a short description on each card: "Malai kofta : paneer dumplings in a mild creamy tomato sauce."
  • Include one or two familiar Thanksgiving dishes, perhaps lightly spiced.
  • Show how dishes go together: rice with dal, naan with paneer, chutney with samosa.
  • Offer a cooling raita and plenty of water or lassi.

Frequently asked questions

How do I host vegetarian and non-vegetarian guests at Thanksgiving?

Design a complete vegetarian menu first, add non-vegetarian dishes separately, use separate utensils and serving areas, and label every dish.

What can Jain guests eat at Thanksgiving?

Practice varies, so ask; commonly suitable dishes avoid onion, garlic and root vegetables : for example paneer in an onion-garlic-free tomato gravy, dal, jeera rice, green beans, pumpkin sabzi, puris and kheer.

How do I make Indian food less spicy for guests?

Cook mains at medium heat and serve chilli heat on the side through green chutney, pickle and sliced chillies.

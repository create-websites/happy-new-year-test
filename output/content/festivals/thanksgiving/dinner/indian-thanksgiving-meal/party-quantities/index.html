Indian Thanksgiving Party Menus and Quantities for 10, 25 and 50 Guests

By Happy New Year 2027Published November 16, 20264 min read

The question every Thanksgiving host asks is the same: how much food do I actually need? These menus and estimates are a starting point for Indian-style gatherings, based on common home and catering rules of thumb.

Key takeaways

  • Plan by number of adults; count children as roughly half.
  • The more dishes you serve, the less of each you need : total food per person stays roughly constant.
  • Allow about 1 pound of whole turkey per person if turkey is the only main, and less when there are several mains.
  • Make one extra batch of rice and bread; they are the cheapest way to make sure nobody leaves hungry.
  • These are estimates; adjust for your crowd, the length of the party and how many guests take food home.

Planning principles

Indian party planning rules vary by family and caterer, but most converge on a few ideas. Guests eat a fairly constant total amount, so adding more dishes means smaller portions of each. Rice and bread fill people up; rich gravies are eaten in smaller amounts than you expect. And Indian gatherings tend to run long, so guests come back for second rounds and dessert.

The figures below are planning estimates for a dinner where appetizers are served first. Adjust them up for younger crowds or long evenings and down for lunch events or groups with many children.

Menu for 10 guests (home dinner)

  • Appetizers: 25–30 mini samosas, chaat bar for 10, masala chai.
  • Mains: tandoori turkey breast (about 5–6 lb bone-in) or masala-stuffed pumpkin; paneer butter masala (about 6–8 cups).
  • Dal: dal makhani (about 6 cups).
  • Rice: cranberry-nut pulao from about 3½ cups raw basmati.
  • Sides: masala mashed potatoes, green bean poriyal, kachumber.
  • Breads: 20 naan.
  • Condiments: cranberry chutney, mint chutney, raita (about 4 cups).
  • Dessert: pumpkin halwa and cardamom pumpkin pie.

Menu for 25 guests (large family or Friendsgiving)

  • Appetizers: 60–75 samosas or pakoras; paneer tikka (about 5 lb paneer).
  • Mains: two vegetarian mains (malai kofta, chole) plus one turkey or chicken dish if the group eats meat.
  • Dal: one large pot (about 12–15 cups).
  • Rice: vegetable biryani from about 5 cups raw rice plus jeera rice from about 3–4 cups raw rice.
  • Sides: roasted aloo gobi, sweet potato chaat, salad.
  • Breads: 50 naan or kulcha.
  • Dessert: gulab jamun (about 50 pieces), kheer, mithai platter.

Menu for 50 guests (community or association event)

  • Appetizers: 125–150 samosas, a chaat station, chai urns.
  • Mains: three vegetarian mains and one or two non-vegetarian mains, each made in hotel-pan quantities. Total curries and dals combined: roughly 75–100 cups (about 5–6 gallons) across all dishes.
  • Rice: biryani plus plain rice, about 16–18 cups raw rice in total.
  • Breads: about 100 naan, ideally ordered from a restaurant.
  • Sides and salads: 3 vegetable sides, 2 salads, raita in two batches.
  • Dessert: 2–3 desserts, about 100 small portions in total.

At this size, consider a hybrid: catered rice, breads and one main, with the community bringing everything else. See potluck planning.

Quick scaling rules

  • Count children aged roughly 5–12 as half an adult; toddlers as a quarter.
  • Add 10 percent for unexpected guests : common at Indian gatherings.
  • If the event is a lunch or under three hours, reduce mains by about 20 percent.
  • If most guests are teenagers or young adults, increase rice and bread by about 20 percent.
  • Always over-prepare rice and bread rather than curries; they are cheaper and leftovers are easier to use.

Frequently asked questions

How much rice do I need for 50 people?

As a planning estimate, about 16–18 cups of raw basmati in total when rice is one of several mains, split between biryani and plain rice.

How much curry per person for an Indian party?

About 1½ to 2 cups of all curries, dals and gravies combined per adult, divided among the dishes you serve.

How many naans for 25 people?

About 50 naan or kulcha when rice is also served.

How much turkey per person if there are Indian mains too?

About ½ to ¾ pound of whole turkey per person when several other mains are served; about 1 pound if turkey is the only main.

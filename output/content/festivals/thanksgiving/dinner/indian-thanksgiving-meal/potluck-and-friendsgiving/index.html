Organizing a Community Thanksgiving Potluck or Friendsgiving for Indian Families

By Happy New Year 2027Published November 16, 20263 min read

For many Indian Americans, Thanksgiving is a community holiday: friends far from family, cultural associations, apartment neighbours, temple or gurdwara groups, colleagues. A potluck lets everyone bring their region's best : and a little organisation keeps it from becoming twelve trays of paneer.

Key takeaways

  • Assign dishes by category (main, rice, vegetable, snack, dessert) instead of an open 'bring anything' list.
  • The host or organising committee should cover rice, bread, drinks, plates and serving utensils.
  • Ask every contributor to bring a label: dish name, vegetarian or not, nuts, spice level.
  • Plan oven and stove space for reheating : it is the most common bottleneck.
  • Agree on leftovers before the event: take-home boxes, or donation where a food bank accepts prepared food.

Run the sign-up by category

Open-ended potlucks produce duplicates. Create a shared spreadsheet or sign-up form with fixed slots, for example for 40 guests:

  • 2 vegetarian mains (paneer, kofta, chole)
  • 1–2 non-vegetarian mains if the group eats meat (tandoori turkey, chicken curry, biryani)
  • 2 rice dishes (one biryani, one plain or pulao)
  • 3 vegetable sides (one dry, one gravy, one regional speciality)
  • 1 dal
  • 2 snacks or appetizers
  • 2–3 desserts
  • 1 salad or raita

Ask for the dish name when people sign up, not just the category, so the organiser can spot clashes early. Encourage regional variety: a Gujarati farsan, a South Indian rice, a Bengali sweet, an Indo-Chinese starter. See regional menus for ideas.

What the host or committee provides

  • Plain rice and breads, which are bulky and best bought or made centrally.
  • Drinks, ice and chai.
  • Plates, cups, cutlery, napkins, serving spoons, foil and take-home containers.
  • Chafing dishes or warming trays for larger events.
  • Blank labels and markers at the food table.
  • Trash and recycling bags, and a cleanup rota.

Labels and dietary information

A mixed Indian American gathering can include vegetarians, Jains, people who avoid beef or pork, egg-free eaters, people with nut allergies and non-Indian guests unfamiliar with the food. Every dish should carry a card with its name and simple tags: Vegetarian · Contains nuts · Contains dairy · Medium spicy · No onion/garlic. Keep vegetarian and non-vegetarian dishes on separate tables or clearly separate ends, each with its own serving spoons. More detail is in hosting mixed-diet tables.

Logistics that make or break the day

  • Reheating: ask contributors to arrive with food hot or ready to serve; most venues have one oven and four burners.
  • Serving order: appetizers on arrival, a short welcome or gratitude moment, then mains buffet, then dessert and chai.
  • Children: a mild plate (plain rice, dal, naan, paneer, fruit) and a separate kids' table speeds up the line.
  • Food safety: hot food should stay hot and cold food cold; refrigerate perishable leftovers within two hours.
  • Budget: for association events, a small per-family contribution typically covers rented space, drinks and supplies.

Friendsgiving and office potlucks

Friendsgivings are often held the weekend before or after Thanksgiving and are more relaxed. Choose dishes that travel and hold: biryani, chole, paneer tikka, samosas, Indo-Chinese starters such as chilli paneer or gobi Manchurian, and sweets that need no plating. For office potlucks, avoid strongly aromatic tempering at the office microwave, bring dishes ready to serve, and include a short card explaining what each dish is.

Adding seva to the celebration

Many community groups pair their Thanksgiving gathering with giving: collecting canned goods for a food bank, cooking extra for a shelter, or volunteering at a community meal. Check the requirements of the receiving organisation first : many accept only packaged food or food prepared in licensed kitchens.

Frequently asked questions

How do I organize an Indian Thanksgiving potluck?

Create a sign-up by category with fixed slots, have the host provide rice, bread, drinks and supplies, require labels on each dish, and plan reheating and leftovers in advance.

What should the host provide at a potluck?

Typically rice, bread, drinks, plates and cutlery, serving utensils, labels and take-home containers, plus cleanup supplies.

What Indian dishes travel well for Friendsgiving?

Biryani, chole, paneer tikka, samosas, Indo-Chinese starters and dry sweets travel and hold well.

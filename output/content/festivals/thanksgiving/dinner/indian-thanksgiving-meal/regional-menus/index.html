Regional Indian Thanksgiving Menus: Punjabi, South Indian, Gujarati, Bengali and More

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

There is no single Indian cuisine, and there is no single Indian Thanksgiving. These menus show how families from different regions can bring their own festive traditions to the November table, using seasonal American produce.

Key takeaways

  • Every regional cuisine already has a festive meal structure you can adapt.
  • Pumpkin, squash and sweet potato fit naturally into Punjabi, South Indian, Gujarati and Bengali cooking.
  • Mixed-region households can pick one dish from each menu.
  • Community potlucks can assign each family a regional dish for variety.
  • Each menu below is an inspiration list, not a fixed tradition.

Punjabi Thanksgiving

The most natural fusion: tandoor flavours meet the roast bird.

  • Tandoori turkey or paneer tikka platter
  • Makhani gravy, dal makhani, sarson-style greens or palak paneer
  • Chole and jeera rice
  • Garlic naan, laccha onions, mint chutney, cranberry chutney
  • Gajar halwa or pumpkin halwa, pinni

See Punjabi thali and tandoori turkey.

South Indian Thanksgiving

A sadya-inspired spread on banana leaves or large platters makes a memorable harvest meal.

  • Pumpkin and black-eyed pea erissery or a pumpkin kootu
  • Green bean poriyal, cabbage thoran, avial with seasonal vegetables
  • Sambar, rasam, lemon rice or coconut rice
  • Cranberry-tamarind thokku as a pickle-style condiment
  • Pepper-roast turkey or Chettinad-style chicken for meat eaters
  • Payasam with jaggery, or sweet potato payasam

See South Indian thali and Kerala cuisine.

Gujarati Thanksgiving

Gujarati winter cooking is a gift for Thanksgiving: undhiyu is essentially a slow-cooked harvest casserole.

  • Undhiyu with surti papdi, sweet potato, purple yam and methi muthiya
  • Puri and thepla
  • Kadhi and rice
  • Dhokla or khandvi as appetizers
  • Shrikhand or basundi; a jaggery-sweetened cranberry chhundo

See Gujarati thali.

Bengali Thanksgiving

  • Kumro chorchori (pumpkin with panch phoron) or kumro'r chhokka
  • Luchi with cholar dal
  • Shukto or mixed vegetables
  • Kosha mangsho, or a mustard-roast turkey for meat eaters
  • Tomato or cranberry khejur chutney (with dates)
  • Payesh, mishti doi, sandesh

See Bengali food.

Maharashtrian Thanksgiving

  • Bharli vangi or a stuffed-squash version with peanut-coconut masala
  • Masale bhaat as the rice dish
  • Varan with a ghee tempering
  • Koshimbir salads
  • Puran poli or a pumpkin gharge (sweet pumpkin puris)

See Maharashtrian food.

Rajasthani Thanksgiving

  • Dal baati churma as the centerpiece
  • Gatte ki sabzi and ker sangri
  • Bajra roti or missi roti
  • Garlic or red chilli chutney
  • Churma ladoo or ghevar

See Rajasthani cuisine.

The pan-Indian mix

Many Indian American homes span more than one region : a Tamil and Punjabi household, a Gujarati family with Bengali friends. A pan-Indian Thanksgiving can pick one dish per region: Punjabi chole, a South Indian rice, Gujarati dhokla, Bengali payesh and Indo-Chinese gobi Manchurian for the children. It reflects the reality of diaspora tables, and it is a wonderful way for children to learn about family roots.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Punjabi Thanksgiving menu?

Tandoori turkey or paneer tikka, makhani gravy, dal makhani, chole, jeera rice, garlic naan, chutneys and gajar or pumpkin halwa.

What South Indian dishes suit Thanksgiving?

Pumpkin erissery or kootu, green bean poriyal, avial, sambar, rasam, lemon or coconut rice, and payasam.

Which Gujarati dish fits Thanksgiving best?

Undhiyu, a slow-cooked winter mixed-vegetable dish with sweet potato and yam, served with puri.

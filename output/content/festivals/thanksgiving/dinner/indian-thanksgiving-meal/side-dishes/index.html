Indian-Spiced Thanksgiving Sides: Masala Mash, Green Bean Poriyal and More

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

Side dishes are where an Indian Thanksgiving is easiest to pull off. The vegetables are already on every American table in November; a tadka, a spice blend or a squeeze of lime turns them into something your family will recognise as its own.

Key takeaways

  • A tadka of ghee, cumin, mustard seeds and curry leaves transforms almost any Thanksgiving vegetable.
  • Green bean poriyal replaces green bean casserole and is lighter on a heavy table.
  • Roasted sweet potatoes with chaat masala and chutneys make a side that doubles as an appetizer.
  • Keep at least one dry vegetable dish and one fresh salad to balance rich gravies.
  • Most sides can be prepped a day ahead and finished in 15 minutes.

Masala mashed potatoes

Make mashed potatoes the usual way, with butter and milk, but stop before seasoning heavily. Heat ghee, add cumin seeds, finely chopped green chilli, grated ginger and a pinch of turmeric, and pour the sizzling tadka over the mash. Fold in chopped coriander and a squeeze of lemon. The result tastes like the filling of an aloo paratha with the texture of a holiday mash : familiar to everyone at the table.

For a smoother, milder version for children, divide the batch before adding green chilli.

Green bean poriyal instead of casserole

South Indian poriyal is a quick stir-fry of finely chopped vegetables with mustard seeds, urad dal, curry leaves, dried red chilli and freshly grated coconut. Green beans are a classic poriyal vegetable. It is bright, crunchy and light : a welcome contrast when the rest of the table is creamy. Chop beans a day ahead; cook in under 15 minutes.

Roasted aloo gobi and Brussels sprouts sabzi

Aloo gobi becomes a sheet-pan side: toss cauliflower florets and potato wedges with oil, turmeric, cumin, coriander, chilli and salt, roast until browned at the edges, then finish with amchur and coriander. Oven-roasting at a crowd scale is much easier than stovetop.

Brussels sprouts respond the same way. Halve them, roast with oil and salt, then toss with a mustard seed and curry leaf tadka, or with garam masala, lemon and toasted peanuts. See the aloo gobi paratha guide for the classic flavour profile.

Sweet potato: chaat or sabzi

Instead of marshmallow-topped casserole, roast sweet potato cubes until caramelised and serve them as shakarkandi chaat: chaat masala, roasted cumin, lemon, green chutney, tamarind chutney and pomegranate seeds. It works as a side at dinner and as a snack while guests arrive. Families who want the sweet version can mash sweet potato with jaggery, cardamom and ghee.

Corn, squash and other harvest vegetables

  • Masala corn : sweet corn sautéed with butter, chilli, lemon and chaat masala, like the street-style cup sold at Indian fairs.
  • Butternut squash sabzi : cubed squash cooked with panch phoron, Bengali style, slightly sweet and aromatic.
  • Bhindi do pyaza or baingan bharta for families who want a classic North Indian vegetable.

Always add something fresh

A Thanksgiving table needs acidity and crunch. Kachumber : diced cucumber, tomato and onion with lemon, salt and roasted cumin : takes ten minutes. Add a cucumber or boondi raita and a plate of sliced onions, green chillies and lemon wedges. These cost little and make every rich dish taste better.

How many sides do you need?

For most home gatherings, plan one potato or starch side, one green vegetable, one roasted or dry vegetable, one fresh salad and one raita. Larger community events can repeat categories with different dishes rather than adding entirely new types.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make mashed potatoes Indian style?

Make plain mashed potatoes, then pour over a tadka of ghee, cumin, green chilli, ginger and turmeric, and finish with coriander and lemon.

What Indian dish can replace green bean casserole?

Green bean poriyal : beans stir-fried with mustard seeds, curry leaves, urad dal and coconut : is lighter and fresher.

Can I make Indian side dishes ahead?

Chop vegetables, make tadkas and raita bases a day ahead; roast and stir-fry on the day for the best texture.

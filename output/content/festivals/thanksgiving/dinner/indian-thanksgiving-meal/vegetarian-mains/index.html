Vegetarian Indian Thanksgiving Mains: Paneer, Malai Kofta and Stuffed Squash

By Prashant DhingraPublished November 16, 20263 min read

In many Indian American homes, the vegetarian main is not an alternative : it is the dish most of the table will eat. These centerpieces deliver the abundance and drama of Thanksgiving without a turkey.

Key takeaways

  • Choose one 'showpiece' main and one comforting legume dish, such as malai kofta plus dal makhani.
  • A whole roasted pumpkin or squash stuffed with spiced rice, paneer and nuts looks like Thanksgiving and tastes Indian.
  • Paneer should go into gravies at the end to stay soft.
  • Gravy bases can be made one to two days ahead and finished on the day.
  • Mark Jain-friendly or onion-garlic-free versions clearly if you make them.

Make the vegetarian main the star

The most common mistake at mixed Thanksgiving tables is treating vegetarian guests as an afterthought : a plate of sides while the turkey gets the attention. Reverse it. Choose a main that is visually generous, served in your best dish, garnished properly and placed at the centre of the table.

Paneer butter masala or shahi paneer

Paneer in a smooth tomato-cashew gravy is the closest thing to a universal crowd-pleaser at Indian gatherings. It is mild enough for children and guests new to Indian food, and rich enough to feel festive. Make the gravy a day ahead; on the day, warm it gently and add paneer only at the end so it stays tender. Finish with cream, kasuri methi and a swirl of butter. See the full paneer butter masala guide.

Malai kofta

Soft dumplings of paneer and potato in a velvety gravy feel like a special-occasion dish. For a crowd, fry or air-fry the koftas a few hours ahead and keep them separate from the gravy; combine just before serving or let guests ladle gravy over koftas themselves so they do not dissolve on the buffet.

Masala-stuffed pumpkin or squash

This is the dish that makes a vegetarian Thanksgiving look like Thanksgiving. Halve a sugar pumpkin, kabocha, acorn or butternut squash, scoop out the seeds and roast until just tender. Fill with a pulao-style mixture : cooked basmati, sautéed onion, peas, crumbled paneer, cashews, raisins, garam masala and fresh coriander : and return to the oven until hot and lightly crisp on top.

Serve whole and cut at the table like a turkey. Small acorn squash halves make elegant individual portions for a seated dinner.

Kaddu ki sabzi and other seasonal mains

  • Khatta-meetha kaddu : sweet-sour pumpkin with fenugreek, amchur and a little jaggery, a North Indian puja favourite that pairs naturally with puris.
  • Dum aloo : baby potatoes in a spiced yogurt gravy, Kashmiri or Punjabi style.
  • Undhiyu-style mixed vegetables : a Gujarati winter dish of mixed vegetables that suits the harvest theme.
  • Chole : sturdy, holds for hours and loved by everyone.

Add one comforting legume dish

Dal makhani, rajma or chole gives a vegetarian plate the same satisfying weight that turkey and gravy give a traditional plate. Dal makhani in particular improves when made a day ahead and gently reheated. See dal makhani and rajma chawal.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good vegetarian main dish for an Indian Thanksgiving?

Paneer butter masala, malai kofta and a whole pumpkin or squash stuffed with spiced rice and paneer are the strongest centerpieces; pair one with dal makhani or chole.

Can I make paneer dishes ahead for Thanksgiving?

Make the gravy one or two days ahead and refrigerate it; add paneer when reheating on the day so it stays soft.

Is pumpkin used in Indian cooking?

Yes. Pumpkin appears in North Indian khatta-meetha kaddu, in sambar and kootu in the South, and in halwa as a sweet.

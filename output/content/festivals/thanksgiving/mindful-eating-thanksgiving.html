Mindful Eating at the Thanksgiving Table

Savor Thanksgiving dishes mindfully and at a relaxed pace, staying mindful of hunger cues and portion sizes, all without letting the festivities feel like a strict diet regimen.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingGratitude, Wellness & Traditions

Arrive hungry, not ravenous

Not eating meals to "cut calories" can hinder your ability to recognize fullness later on. A balanced breakfast or a light lunch prepares you to savor dinner instead of feeling rushed to eat.

Survey before serving

View the entire selection before making your choices. Pick the dishes that truly appeal to you, including any sentimental favorites, and serve yourself small portions at first. Feel free to go back for seconds if desired.

Slow the first ten minutes

It's common to hurry through the start of a holiday dinner. Remember to pause, engage in conversation, appreciate the flavors and temperatures, and savor each bite. Slowing down your eating pace can enhance your dining experience.

Avoid moral labels for food

Thanksgiving meals don't have to be labeled as "good," "bad," "clean," or "cheat" foods. Food can nourish, bring joy, reflect culture, and create memories simultaneously. If you have specific dietary restrictions, listen to your healthcare provider's recommendations. Otherwise, opting for flexibility over guilt is often more sustainable.

Frequently asked questions

Should I skip breakfast before Thanksgiving dinner?

Eating a balanced meal earlier in the day can help prevent extreme hunger at dinner time, making it easier to enjoy a comfortable meal.

What if I eat more than planned?

Don't feel the need to punish yourself or compensate for one big meal. Just get back to your usual routine and prioritize comfort, hydration, and overall wellbeing.

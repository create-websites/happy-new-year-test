A Stress-Free Thanksgiving Prep Timeline

Follow a two-week Thanksgiving schedule to help with menu planning, shopping, thawing, preparing make-ahead dishes, setting up the table, and ensuring a stress-free final cooking day.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Two weeks out: lock the shape of the day

Please verify the number of guests, any dietary restrictions, and the desired serving time. Select a menu that can be prepared with your home kitchen appliances, such as oven, refrigerator, and number of burners, rather than a menu intended for a professional kitchen.

Delegate tasks now. Determine what items guests should bring, what can be bought, and what merits homemade preparation.

One week out: shop and prep the environment

Purchase shelf-stable items, drinks, serving essentials, and decorations. Ensure you have roasting pans, thermometers, carving utensils, and storage bins. Make room in the fridge before the last grocery run.

If you plan to use a frozen turkey, be sure to start calculating the thawing time in advance. The USDA suggests allowing approximately 24 hours of refrigerator thawing for every four to five pounds of turkey.

Two to three days out: make what improves with time

Get ready for Thanksgiving by making cranberry sauce, pie dough, desserts, salad dressings, and bread cubes for stuffing. If your recipe calls for it, chop up some sturdy vegetables. And if the dining room isn't being used for regular meals, go ahead and set the table.

Thanksgiving Day: work backward from serving time

Record oven activities, including starting and finishing times and resting periods. Begin with dishes that can be kept warm, reserving fragile items for later. Allow extra time before serving to carve and prepare gravy without guests already seated.

Frequently asked questions

What should I make first?

Begin with items that have a longer shelf life: cranberry sauce, baked goods, bread for drying, and certain casseroles. Save the preparation of fresh vegetables and the final turkey cooking for a later time.

How much buffer time should I allow?

Allowing for a minimum of 30 to 60 minutes of free time before serving will accommodate slow cooking, reheating, and carving delays.

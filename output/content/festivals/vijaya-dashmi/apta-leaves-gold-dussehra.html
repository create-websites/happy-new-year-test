Why Apta Leaves Are Exchanged as “Gold” on Dussehra

Learn the stories and social meaning behind exchanging Apta leaves as symbolic “gold” on Dussehra, especially in Maharashtra and neighboring traditions.

Dussehra Hub
Why this guide matters

The custom turns an ordinary leaf into a ritual gift, making prosperity something that can be shared symbolically rather than measured only in money.

A leaf that becomes “sona”

In parts of western India, people exchange leaves from the Apta tree on Dussehra and call them symbolic gold, or sona. The gesture is accompanied by greetings for prosperity, goodwill and success.

The custom is memorable because it makes abundance accessible: a simple leaf can carry the meaning of a precious gift.

Legends of generosity

Popular stories explain the custom through acts of generosity and the distribution of wealth. Versions differ, but they reinforce the same moral idea—prosperity gains meaning when it circulates rather than when it is hoarded.

That is why the exchange often happens among relatives, neighbors, colleagues and friends.

A social ritual of reconciliation

Festival greetings create an easy occasion to reconnect after distance or disagreement. Offering symbolic gold can therefore function as a small ritual of respect and renewed goodwill.

The value is relational, not financial.

Celebrate without harming trees

If you follow the custom, take only what is needed and avoid stripping branches or damaging trees. Communities can also use fallen leaves, shared bundles or symbolic reusable cards when plant access is limited.

The spirit of prosperity should not come at the expense of the living tree that carries the symbolism.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Why are Apta leaves called gold?

They are exchanged as symbolic sona, representing prosperity and goodwill.

Is this custom practiced across India?

It is especially associated with Maharashtra and some neighboring regional traditions.

Can the tradition be made eco-friendly?

Yes. Use small quantities, fallen leaves where appropriate, and avoid damaging trees.

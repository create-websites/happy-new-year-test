2026 Health Resolution Guide

26 Health Resolutions for a Stronger 2026

Turn New Year motivation into a repeatable health system. Build around movement, strength, sleep, nutrition, recovery and preventive care: then choose only the habits you can sustain.

Explore all 26 goalsBuild my routine

Educational content only. Exercise, nutrition, supplement and recovery needs vary by age, health status, medications, pregnancy, injury history and training experience.

Move Every Week

Adults can build toward 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly, or 75–150 minutes vigorous, plus strength work on 2 days.

Train for Function

Strength, cardiorespiratory fitness, mobility and balance each support different parts of health. Your plan does not need to maximize all of them at once.

Recover on Purpose

Most adults need at least 7 hours of sleep. Recovery also includes appropriate training load, food, hydration, easier days and medical care when needed.

Your 2026 Checklist

26 Practical Health Resolutions

You do not need to adopt all 26. Start with two or three behaviors that solve your biggest bottleneck, make them easy to repeat, and expand only after they feel stable.

1

Move 150 Minutes a Week

Build toward at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity each week, or an equivalent amount of vigorous activity.

2

Strength Train Twice Weekly

Train all major muscle groups on at least two days each week, scaling resistance to your current ability.

3

Walk More Often

Use short walks, stairs, errands, and movement breaks to reduce long stretches of sitting.

4

Progress Your Resistance Training

Increase challenge gradually as technique and recovery allow instead of chasing constant maximal effort.

5

Practice the Major Movement Patterns

Include some combination of squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, step, and rotation patterns that are appropriate for you.

6

Protect Your Joints

Use controlled ranges of motion, sensible loading, and exercise substitutions when a movement causes pain.

7

Train Cardiorespiratory Fitness

Mix easier steady activity with occasional harder efforts if your health and experience support it.

8

Build a Mobility Habit

Spend a few minutes most days moving key joints through comfortable ranges you want to maintain.

9

Prioritize Consistency Over Intensity

Choose a weekly plan you can repeat for months, not a heroic plan you can only tolerate for days.

10

Schedule Recovery Days

Alternate demanding sessions with easier days so your training plan includes recovery rather than treating it as an afterthought.

11

Get at Least 7 Hours of Sleep

Protect a consistent sleep opportunity and address persistent sleep problems with a healthcare professional.

12

Keep a Regular Sleep Schedule

Aim for similar bed and wake times and create a repeatable wind-down routine.

13

Eat Protein-Rich Foods Regularly

Include protein sources that fit your dietary pattern, goals, preferences, and medical needs.

14

Build Meals Around Minimally Processed Foods

Make fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, and other nutrient-dense foods routine rather than occasional.

15

Hydrate for Your Conditions

Adjust fluids to heat, activity, sweat rate, diet, and medical guidance rather than following a one-size-fits-all number.

16

Plan Your Food Environment

Keep convenient, satisfying foods available so your default choices support your goals.

17

Use Supplements Selectively

Treat supplements as optional tools. Check evidence, dose, interactions, quality, and whether you actually need them.

18

Track One or Two Useful Metrics

Use a small dashboard: such as workouts completed, weekly activity minutes, sleep, or waist trend: without turning health into constant surveillance.

19

Measure Progress Monthly

Look for trends in strength, endurance, energy, function, and adherence rather than judging progress from a single day.

20

Set a Minimum-Viable Workout

Create a short fallback session for busy days so missed time does not become an abandoned routine.

21

Reduce Long Sitting Bouts

Break up prolonged sitting with standing, stretching, or brief walks when practical.

22

Train for Real-Life Function

Include balance, carries, getting up from the floor, stairs, and other tasks that matter in daily life.

23

Use Pain as Information

Stop or modify movements that produce concerning pain, swelling, dizziness, chest pain, or other warning symptoms.

24

Make Stress Recovery Deliberate

Use walks, breathing exercises, time outdoors, hobbies, social connection, or other healthy decompression practices.

25

Get Preventive Care

Keep up with age- and risk-appropriate checkups, vaccinations, screenings, and dental care with your healthcare team.

26

Review the Plan Every Quarter

Every few months, keep what is working, remove what is not, and set the next small health target for the year.

Common Questions

Health Resolution FAQ

What is the best health resolution to start with?

Start with the behavior that removes your biggest constraint. For many people that is simply walking more, scheduling two strength sessions, protecting sleep, or making meals easier to plan.

Should I do all 26 resolutions?

No. Pick a few priorities and make them repeatable. Add goals only when your current routine is stable enough that another habit will not crowd it out.

How much exercise should adults aim for?

Current U.S. guidance recommends at least 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly, or 75–150 minutes vigorous, plus muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days each week.

How much sleep do adults need?

CDC guidance says adults ages 18–60 generally need at least 7 hours per day; recommendations vary somewhat for older adults. Persistent sleep problems deserve professional evaluation.

Are supplements necessary for a health resolution plan?

Not usually. Training, diet quality, sleep, activity and adherence are the foundation. Some supplements have evidence for specific uses, but benefits, risks and interactions vary.
